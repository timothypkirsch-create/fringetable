Afghan Tomato Sauce

A gently spiced Afghan tomato sauce with onion, garlic and turmeric, designed to spoon over ashak and other dumplings alongside cool garlicky yogurt.

This is a finishing sauce rather than a long-cooked pasta-style tomato sauce. The goal is a bright, savory layer that is thick enough to sit on the dumplings without burying their filling.

Ingredients

  • 1 tbsp neutral oil
  • 1 small onion, finely diced
  • 2 garlic cloves, minced
  • 1 1/2 cups crushed tomatoes
  • 1/2 tsp turmeric
  • Salt and pepper

Method

  1. Cook onion in oil until soft.
  2. Add garlic and turmeric; cook briefly.
  3. Add tomatoes and simmer until thick enough to spoon over dumplings. Season to taste.

How this sauce is used

For Afghan ashak, spoon the warm tomato sauce over the cooked dumplings after adding a layer of garlic yogurt. The contrast between warm tomato, cool yogurt and the herb-filled dumplings is part of the finished dish.

Afghan tomato sauce questions

Is this sauce spicy?

Not necessarily. This version is gently spiced with turmeric and black pepper. Chile can be added to taste, but heat is not required for the sauce to work with ashak.

How thick should it be?

Simmer until a spoon drawn through the pan leaves a brief trail. It should coat a dumpling without running immediately to the bottom of the plate.

Use it with

Afghan recipes

Spoon this sauce over Afghan Ashak or serve it alongside Borani Banjan.

Detailed method

Build a sauce that sits on the dumplings

Warm the oil in a wide skillet over medium-low heat. Add the finely diced onion with a small pinch of salt and cook for 8 to 10 minutes, stirring often, until soft and translucent with light gold at the edges. Avoid deep browning; the finished sauce should retain a fresh tomato character.

Add the garlic and turmeric and stir for about 30 seconds, just until aromatic. Pour in the crushed tomatoes carefully, bring them to a gentle bubble and lower the heat. Simmer uncovered for 12 to 18 minutes, stirring across the bottom of the pan every few minutes.

Thickness checkpoint: drag a spoon through the center. The trail should remain visible for one or two seconds before the sauce flows back. If the tomatoes are especially acidic, a small pinch of sugar can round the flavor, but the sauce should remain savory. Season with black pepper and enough salt to make the tomato taste complete.

How to layer ashak

Spread a thin layer of Afghan garlic yogurt on the serving platter, arrange the cooked ashak over it, then spoon the warm tomato sauce across the dumplings. Finish with more yogurt and dried mint if desired. Keeping some white yogurt visible preserves the contrast between the cool, tangy layer and the warm tomato sauce.

Make-ahead and storage

The sauce can be refrigerated in a covered container for up to three days. Reheat it gently and add a splash of water if it has tightened in the refrigerator. Store it separately from yogurt and cooked dumplings so each component keeps its texture.

Sources and variations

Afghan ashak recipes may use a vegetarian tomato sauce or a tomato-based meat topping, depending on the cook and occasion. This streamlined sauce is intended as a practical meatless topping rather than the only traditional presentation.
