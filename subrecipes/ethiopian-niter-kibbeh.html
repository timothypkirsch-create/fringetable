Ethiopian Niter Kibbeh

Clarified butter infused slowly with aromatics and warm spices, used to give Ethiopian and Eritrean stews a deeper, rounded flavor.

Niter kibbeh varies by household and spice cabinet. This home version focuses on ingredients that are broadly obtainable while preserving the essential technique: gentle clarification plus a long, low infusion. It is especially useful in dishes such as doro wat and shiro, where the cooking fat contributes flavor rather than merely preventing sticking.

Before you begin

Preparation notes

Keep the heat low

The goal is to clarify and perfume the butter, not brown it. If the milk solids darken quickly, lower the heat.

Crack whole spices lightly

A light crush exposes aromatic oils without turning the mixture gritty.

Strain carefully

A fine sieve lined with cheesecloth gives the cleanest finished fat and improves storage.

Make it

Ingredients & detailed method

Ingredients

  • 1 lb unsalted butter
  • 1 small shallot or 1/4 small onion, thinly sliced
  • 3 garlic cloves, lightly crushed
  • 1 tbsp finely chopped fresh ginger
  • 1 tsp fenugreek seeds
  • 1 tsp coriander seeds
  • 1/2 tsp cumin seeds
  • 4 green cardamom pods, cracked
  • 1/2 tsp black peppercorns
  • 1 small cinnamon stick
  • 2 whole cloves

Method

  1. 1. Put the butter in a small heavy saucepan over the lowest practical heat and let it melt without stirring aggressively.
  2. 2. Add shallot, garlic, ginger and all whole spices once the butter is fully liquid.
  3. 3. Cook very gently for 25–35 minutes. Checkpoint: the top should look clear and golden, milk solids should settle toward the bottom, and the aromatics should smell sweet and warm rather than toasted or burnt.
  4. 4. Remove from the heat and let stand 5 minutes so sediment settles.
  5. 5. Strain through a fine sieve or cheesecloth into a clean heatproof jar, leaving any dark sediment behind.
  6. 6. Cool completely, cover and refrigerate. Use a clean spoon each time and substitute it anywhere a recipe calls for niter kibbeh.
