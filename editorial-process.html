Editorial process

Research first. Adapt honestly. Correct openly.

Fringe Table is an organization-led publication. Its recipes are researched adaptations for home cooks, not claims that one formula represents every family, region or community.

This page explains how sources, cultural framing, measurements, automation and corrections are handled—and where the limits of the collection should remain visible.

01
Source selection

Begin with people and institutions close to the food.

Research prioritizes cultural institutions, museums, universities, government and tourism resources, community organizations, experienced cooks, established culinary writers and carefully documented regional sources. A recipe should not rely on one convenient summary when stronger community or institutional material is available.

Sources are used to establish the dish’s place, name, role, ingredients and documented variations. Pages distinguish sourced history from interpretation and link readers to the material used whenever practical. Conflicting accounts are treated as uncertainty rather than blended into a false single origin story.

02
Home-kitchen adaptation

Make a usable version without calling it definitive.

Traditional and community foods change across households, seasons, migrations and available ingredients. Fringe Table therefore presents a clearly framed adaptation: measurements are scaled for a contemporary home kitchen, specialist steps are explained and substitutions are labeled when they materially change the result.

Methods should provide visual, aromatic, textural, temperature or doneness checkpoints rather than relying only on a timer. Food-safety guidance is included when an ingredient or technique creates a meaningful risk. Culturally sensitive ceremonial practices are not converted into generic recipes without reliable public culinary documentation.

03
Editorial review

Publication checks are necessary, but they are not the same as firsthand authority.

Before publication, a recipe is checked for duplication, coherent measurements, method order, source support, cultural specificity, image licensing, internal links, pronunciation, structured data and accessibility. Automated quality checks also verify catalog, sitemap and page integration.

Fringe Table does not describe a recipe as kitchen-tested unless it has actually been prepared and evaluated. Research-based pages should be read as practical editorial adaptations, not as family recipes or claims of lived authority. As the collection develops, recipes with documented kitchen testing and original process photography will identify that evidence directly on the page.

04
Automation and assisted tools

Tools support the workflow; they do not create authority.

Fringe Table uses software and assisted research tools to organize catalog data, check links, validate structured data, maintain pronunciation coverage, identify missing fields and support initial drafting. Those tools can improve consistency, but they cannot replace reliable sourcing, cultural judgment or actual cooking experience.

Automated publishing is paused when the collection requires editorial repair. No page should be published merely to increase the catalog count, target a search phrase or create advertising inventory. When a page cannot yet provide enough independent value, it may remain available to readers while being withheld from search indexing and advertising until it is improved.

05
Images and attribution

Rights and provenance remain visible.

Images must be original, licensed for reuse or in the public domain. Third-party photographs retain a source and license link on the recipe page and in the site’s Image Credits. A representative image is not proof that Fringe Table prepared the pictured dish.

Original cooking and process photography is being introduced gradually. It will not be simulated or presented as documentary evidence when a recipe was not actually prepared for the publication.

06
Corrections and revisions

A maintained page should become more accurate over time.

Substantive changes update the page’s modification date. Corrections may address cultural framing, terminology, measurements, safety, sourcing, attribution or a method that needs clearer checkpoints. Established URLs are preserved whenever possible so readers and cited links continue to reach the improved page.

Readers, cooks, scholars, photographers and community members can send specific corrections or source material to hello@fringetable.com. Useful corrections are evaluated against supporting evidence and incorporated without hiding the fact that the collection continues to learn.
