Georgia / Caucasus

Georgian Chakapuli

Stew / Main · 1 hr 50 min · 6 servings

Lamb cooks under armfuls of spring herbs, tart plum sauce and white wine in chakapuli, a Georgian stew closely associated with Easter and the green season.

Fringe Table adaptation

About this dish

Georgia's national tourism authority describes chakapuli as a symbolic spring and Easter dish built from tender lamb, fresh herbs, sorrel, green onion, green pepper, tarragon, tkemali plum and white wine. The official method layers meat and herbs, adds wine immediately, brings in tkemali before the lamb is fully tender and cooks everything gently so the sour plum, wine and herbs form one bright sauce.

Story & history

Why this dish matters

Chakapuli is a dish in which seasonality is visible in the pot. Georgia Travel emphasizes tarragon, sorrel and other spring greens and links the stew strongly with Easter. It also documents meaningful variation: veal can replace lamb, and contemporary versions may use chicken, fish, mushrooms, eggs or potatoes. The traditional lamb form remains distinctive because fatty meat, sharp tkemali and large quantities of fresh herbs balance one another. Kakhetian qvevri white wine is singled out by the tourism authority, reflecting the dish's connection to Georgia's wine culture as well as its spring table.

This is a scaled home version of the lamb preparation documented by Georgia Travel. A dry Georgian white wine is ideal; another dry, unoaked white wine is a practical cooking substitute but not culturally identical. Fresh tarragon and tkemali are defining flavors and should not be replaced with dried tarragon or ordinary sweet plum jam.

Before you begin

Preparation notes

Buy much more herbs than seems normal

Chakapuli is not a meat stew garnished with herbs. The herbs collapse into the cooking liquid and are a major part of the dish's body and aroma.

Strip tough tarragon stems

Use tender tips and leaves; thick woody stems stay stringy even after simmering.

Keep the simmer gentle

Hard boiling drives off wine aroma quickly and can tighten the lamb before its connective tissue softens. Aim for lazy bubbles, not a rolling boil.

Cook it

Ingredients & detailed method

Ingredients

  • 2 1/4 lb lamb shoulder or neck, cut into 1 1/2-inch pieces, preferably with some fat
  • 1 medium yellow onion, thinly sliced
  • 8 green onions, sliced into 1-inch pieces
  • 4 packed cups fresh tarragon leaves and tender tips
  • 2 packed cups fresh sorrel, roughly chopped
  • 2 packed cups fresh cilantro, roughly chopped
  • 1 packed cup flat-leaf parsley, roughly chopped
  • 1/2 cup fresh mint leaves, roughly torn
  • 1 to 2 fresh green chiles, slit or sliced to taste
  • 1 1/2 cups dry Georgian white wine or another dry unoaked white wine
  • 1 cup green tkemali plum sauce
  • 1 1/4 tsp kosher salt, plus more to taste
  • Freshly ground black pepper to taste

Method

  1. 1.Pat the lamb dry. If it has a few very fatty trimmings, finely chop about 2 tablespoons of them and place them in a heavy Dutch oven over medium-low heat. Cook 4 to 6 minutes until some fat renders; remove any hard browned solids. If your lamb is lean, begin with 1 tablespoon neutral oil instead.
  2. 2.Spread half the sliced onion and green onions over the bottom, add the lamb, then cover with about two-thirds of the tarragon, sorrel, cilantro, parsley and mint. Add the green chile and pour the wine around the meat.
  3. 3.Bring the pot just to a simmer over medium heat, then immediately reduce to low, cover and cook 25 minutes. Checkpoint: the herbs should collapse dramatically, the pot should smell strongly of tarragon and wine, and the liquid should show only lazy bubbles rather than a hard boil.
  4. 4.Add the green tkemali and gently turn the meat and herbs together. Cover partially and continue cooking 35 to 50 minutes, stirring only occasionally so the lamb pieces stay intact.
  5. 5.Checkpoint: the lamb is ready when a fork slides into a piece with little resistance and the connective tissue feels tender rather than rubbery. The sauce should taste distinctly tart and herbaceous, with the alcohol's harsh edge cooked away.
  6. 6.Add the remaining onion, green onion and reserved fresh herbs during the final 5 minutes so the stew regains a fresh green aroma and layered texture. Season with salt and black pepper.
  7. 7.If the pot is very watery after the lamb is tender, uncover and simmer gently 5 to 10 minutes. Do not reduce until thick or brown; chakapuli should retain a loose green-tinted broth around the meat and herbs.
  8. 8.Rest off the heat for 10 minutes, then taste again for salt and tkemali acidity. Serve hot with Georgian bread or another sturdy bread for the broth.
