Iran / Persia

Persian Ash Reshteh

Soup / Main · 2 hrs · 8 servings

A huge amount of herbs, beans and noodles cooks into a thick Persian soup finished with tangy kashk and mint oil.

Fringe Table adaptation

About this dish

Ash reshteh is a Persian herb-and-legume soup featuring reshteh noodles and often kashk, a fermented dairy product. It is thick, green and substantial enough to be a meal, with fried onion and mint adding contrast on top.

Story & history

Why this dish matters

Ash reshteh is associated with gatherings and seasonal celebrations including Nowruz-related traditions. The noodles can carry symbolic associations with paths and good fortune, though household meanings and serving occasions vary.

Kashk gives the most characteristic finish; thick yogurt is only a partial substitute.

Before you begin

Preparation notes

Use lots of herbs

They should dominate visually.

Add noodles near the end

They thicken the soup quickly.

Thin as needed

Ash should be thick but still spoonable.

Cook it

Ingredients & detailed method

Ingredients

  • 1 cup cooked chickpeas
  • 1 cup cooked kidney beans
  • 1 cup cooked lentils
  • 2 cups parsley
  • 2 cups cilantro
  • 2 cups spinach
  • 1 cup scallions
  • 1 onion
  • 8 oz reshteh noodles
  • 8 cups stock
  • 1 tsp turmeric
  • 1/2 cup kashk
  • 2 tbsp dried mint
  • 3 tbsp oil
  • Salt

Method

  1. 1.Fry sliced onion until golden; reserve half.
  2. 2.Add turmeric, legumes and stock to the pot; simmer 20 minutes.
  3. 3.Add chopped herbs and simmer 20 minutes.
  4. 4.Break in reshteh noodles and cook until tender.
  5. 5.Checkpoint: soup should be thick enough that herbs and legumes stay suspended.
  6. 6.Fry dried mint briefly in oil.
  7. 7.Serve with kashk, fried onion and mint oil.
