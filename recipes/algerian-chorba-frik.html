Algeria / Maghreb

Algerian Chorba Frik

Soup · 1 hr 20 min · 6 servings

Lamb, tomato and chickpeas simmer until tender before cracked green wheat thickens the broth into a fragrant Algerian chorba.

Fringe Table adaptation

About this dish

Chorba frik is an Algerian soup built around frik: green wheat harvested young, roasted and cracked. Lamb, tomato, chickpeas, celery and herbs create the broth, while frik contributes a smoky grain flavor and a lightly thickened—not porridge-like—finish.

It is especially associated with Ramadan tables, where a warm bowl is commonly served when the fast is broken, but it is also made as an everyday cool-weather soup.

Story & history

Why this dish matters

“Chorba” names a broad soup family across North Africa, so the grain matters. Algerian chorba frik is distinguished from vermicelli or barley versions by cracked green wheat, whose chew and roasted aroma remain perceptible after simmering.

Recipes change by city and household: lamb or chicken, fresh or soaked chickpeas, varying tomato, and different combinations of cilantro, mint and celery all appear. The useful constant is staged cooking—tenderize the meat before adding the quicker-cooking frik.

This lamb version uses cooked chickpeas for predictable timing. If using soaked dried chickpeas, add them with the stock and confirm they are tender before serving.

Before you begin

Preparation notes

Rinse frik carefully

Use a fine sieve and pick out any grit before the grain enters the soup.

Add grain late

Frik continues absorbing broth; early addition can make the soup overly thick.

Keep hot water nearby

Adjust the final consistency without cooling or stopping the simmer.

Cook it

Ingredients & detailed method

Ingredients

  • 1 pound lamb shoulder, cut into 1-inch cubes
  • 2 tablespoons olive oil
  • 1 large onion, finely chopped
  • 2 celery stalks, finely chopped, leaves reserved
  • 3 garlic cloves, minced
  • 3 ripe tomatoes, grated, or one 14-ounce can crushed tomatoes
  • 1 tablespoon tomato paste
  • 1 teaspoon sweet paprika
  • 1/2 teaspoon ground cinnamon
  • 1/2 teaspoon ground coriander
  • 6 cups low-sodium stock or water, plus more as needed
  • 1 cup cooked chickpeas, drained
  • 1/2 cup frik or coarse cracked freekeh, rinsed
  • 3 tablespoons chopped cilantro
  • 1 tablespoon chopped mint
  • Fine salt and black pepper
  • Lemon wedges, for serving

Method

  1. 1.Pat the lamb dry and season lightly with salt. Heat the olive oil in a heavy pot over medium-high heat.
  2. 2.Brown the lamb in two batches so the pieces sear rather than steam, about 6 minutes per batch. Transfer to a plate.
  3. 3.Lower the heat to medium. Add onion and celery and cook 6–8 minutes until soft. Add garlic, paprika, cinnamon and coriander; stir for 30 seconds.
  4. 4.Add grated tomato and tomato paste. Cook 5–7 minutes, stirring, until the mixture darkens and no longer smells raw.
  5. 5.Return the lamb and its juices. Add stock, bring to a boil, skim excess foam, then cover partially and simmer gently for 35–45 minutes.
  6. 6.Checkpoint: the lamb should be approaching tender before the grain is added. If still firm, continue simmering and add hot water as necessary.
  7. 7.Stir in chickpeas and rinsed frik. Simmer uncovered for 18–25 minutes, stirring occasionally so the grain does not settle and catch.
  8. 8.Checkpoint: the frik should be chewy-tender and the lamb easily pierced. The broth should remain pourable; loosen with hot water if it becomes stew-thick.
  9. 9.Stir in cilantro, mint and chopped celery leaves. Simmer 2 minutes, then season with salt and black pepper.
  10. 10.Rest 5 minutes and serve very hot with lemon wedges. Add water when reheating leftovers because frik continues to swell.
