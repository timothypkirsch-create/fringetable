Ethiopia / Horn of Africa

Ethiopian Doro Wat

Main · 2 hrs 30 min · Serves 6

Chicken and hard-boiled eggs in a patient, deeply reduced sauce of onions, berbere and spiced clarified butter.

Fringe Table adaptation

About this dish

Doro wat is one of Ethiopia’s best-known celebratory stews. Its depth comes less from a long list of ingredients than from concentrating a large amount of onion, blooming berbere without burning it, and giving the chicken enough time to become tender in the sauce. Peeled hard-boiled eggs are added near the end so they take on the color and flavor of the stew.

The two specialty building blocks are berbere, a chile-forward Ethiopian spice blend, and niter kibbeh, clarified butter infused with aromatics and spices. Ghee can work in a pinch, but the finished flavor will be less specifically Ethiopian.

Story & history

A holiday stew built around time together

Saveur has repeatedly documented doro wat as one of Ethiopia’s most recognized dishes and a food strongly associated with holidays and gatherings. In a 2012 recipe, Marcus Samuelsson recalled learning the long-cooking method from a friend in Addis Ababa and later making it with his wife’s sisters, describing the slow preparation as time to catch up while the onions and chicken cook.

The dish is often especially prominent after periods of religious fasting, when meat and dairy return to the table. The communal serving style matters too: doro wat is typically eaten with injera, with diners sharing the food rather than treating each portion as a separate plated entrée.

Recipes vary by household, region, religious practice and spice blend. This is a practical home adaptation inspired by documented Ethiopian methods.

Before you begin

Preparation notes

Chop onions very finely

The onions are the sauce body. Fine chopping helps them disappear into a cohesive, spoon-coating base.

Control the berbere

Spice blends differ dramatically in heat. Start with the listed amount, then adjust near the end rather than committing blindly.

Cook the sauce, not just the chicken

The finished wat should cling to the meat. If watery, remove the lid and reduce before adding the eggs.

Cook it

Ingredients & detailed method

Ingredients

  • 5 medium red or yellow onions, very finely chopped
  • 4 tbsp niter kibbeh or clarified butter
  • 1 tbsp neutral oil
  • 5 garlic cloves, finely grated
  • 1 tbsp grated fresh ginger
  • 1/4 cup berbere spice blend, plus more to taste
  • 1 tsp kosher salt, plus more to taste
  • 8 skinless chicken drumsticks and thighs
  • 2 cups chicken stock or water, plus more as needed
  • 6 hard-boiled eggs, peeled
  • 1 tsp ground korarima or cardamom, optional
  • Injera, for serving

Method

  1. 1.Start with dry onions. Put chopped onions in a heavy pot over medium-low heat without fat. Cook 20–30 minutes, stirring often, as they release water and soften. Checkpoint: the onions should look concentrated and jammy, with little free liquid.
  2. 2.Add the fat and aromatics. Stir in niter kibbeh and oil. Add garlic and ginger and cook 1–2 minutes until fragrant.
  3. 3.Bloom the berbere. Stir in berbere and salt. Add 2–3 tablespoons water so the spices form a paste instead of scorching. Cook 3–5 minutes. Checkpoint: the mixture should turn deep brick red and smell rounded, not burnt or dusty.
  4. 4.Coat the chicken. Add chicken pieces and turn them repeatedly in the onion-spice base for about 5 minutes so every surface is stained red.
  5. 5.Begin the braise. Add 1 1/2 cups stock, bring to a gentle simmer, cover partly and cook 35 minutes, turning the chicken once or twice.
  6. 6.Check tenderness. Continue simmering 20–30 minutes more, adding small amounts of stock only if the sauce threatens to catch. Checkpoint: chicken should be tender at the joint and register at least 165°F in the thickest part.
  7. 7.Reduce the sauce. If the sauce is loose, move chicken to one side of the pot and simmer uncovered 8–12 minutes until thick and glossy enough to cling to a spoon.
  8. 8.Prepare the eggs. Cut four shallow lengthwise slits in each peeled hard-boiled egg. This gives the sauce more surface to season without cutting the eggs apart.
  9. 9.Warm the eggs. Nestle them into the wat and spoon sauce over them. Simmer gently 5 minutes. Add korarima or cardamom if using.
  10. 10.Balance and rest. Taste for salt and berbere. Rest off heat 10 minutes so the fat and sauce settle around the chicken.
  11. 11.Serve communally. Spoon doro wat onto injera or into a central serving dish with injera alongside, making sure each serving gets chicken, egg and plenty of sauce.
