Ethiopia / Horn of Africa

Ethiopian Siga Tibs

Main · 35 min · 4 servings

Tender beef, browned onions, green chile and fragrant spiced butter in one of Ethiopia’s great fast-cooked dishes.

Fringe Table adaptation

About this dish

Tibs refers to a broad family of Ethiopian sautéed or grilled meat dishes rather than one fixed recipe. Siga tibs centers beef and can range from relatively mild versions with onion and rosemary to hotter preparations seasoned with berbere or mitmita. The defining home-kitchen challenge is heat management: the meat should brown quickly instead of releasing enough moisture to stew.

Story & history

Why this dish matters

Saveur’s published siga tibs recipe, adapted from Ethiopian chef and author Yohanis Gebreyesus, describes tibs as a dish eaten year-round and also associated with celebrations that break major Orthodox Christian fasts, including Easter and Christmas. Across Ethiopian restaurants and households, the exact cut of beef, chile level and finishing spices vary, but the combination of hot-pan browning, aromatics and seasoned fat is central to the dish’s appeal.

Tibs styles differ widely across Ethiopia and the diaspora. This is a practical home version of beef siga tibs, not a claim that one seasoning profile represents every household or region.

Before you begin

Preparation notes

Dry the beef well

Surface moisture is the enemy of browning. Blot the cubes thoroughly before they reach the pan.

Use a wide heavy skillet

Crowding drops the pan temperature and turns the first stage into a braise.

Add niter kibbeh late

The spiced butter keeps more of its aroma when it finishes the browned beef rather than taking the full force of the initial sear.

Cook it

Ingredients & detailed method

Ingredients

  • 1 1/2 lb sirloin, ribeye or tender beef, cut into 3/4-inch cubes
  • 1 tsp kosher salt, plus more to taste
  • 1 tbsp neutral oil
  • 1 medium red onion, cut into thin wedges
  • 2 jalapeño or serrano chiles, sliced lengthwise
  • 2 garlic cloves, minced
  • 1 tbsp niter kibbeh
  • 1 tsp berbere, optional, plus more to taste
  • 1 small rosemary sprig or 1/2 tsp finely chopped rosemary
  • Freshly ground black pepper
  • Injera, for serving

Method

  1. 1.Pat the beef very dry and season lightly with salt. Let it sit at room temperature for about 10 minutes while the skillet heats.
  2. 2.Heat a wide cast-iron or stainless-steel skillet over high heat until a drop of water skitters across the surface. Add neutral oil.
  3. 3.Add half the beef in a single layer and leave it untouched for 60–90 seconds before turning. Sear another 1–2 minutes, then remove. Repeat with the remaining beef. Checkpoint: the exterior should develop dark brown patches while the centers remain juicy.
  4. 4.Reduce heat to medium-high. Add onion and chiles to the same pan and cook 3–4 minutes, tossing occasionally. Checkpoint: onion edges should blister and brown while the centers retain some bite.
  5. 5.Add garlic and cook 20–30 seconds until fragrant.
  6. 6.Return beef and any juices to the pan. Add niter kibbeh, berbere if using and rosemary. Toss 1–2 minutes until the butter melts and coats everything.
  7. 7.Taste for salt and black pepper. If using a tender cut, stop cooking as soon as the pieces are hot through; prolonged cooking can toughen them.
  8. 8.Serve immediately with injera and additional vegetables or lentil dishes if making a shared Ethiopian spread.
