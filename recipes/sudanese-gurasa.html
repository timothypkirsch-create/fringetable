Sudan / Northeast Africa

Sudanese Gurasa

Bread · 1 hr 15 min · 8 flatbreads

A loose yeast dough cooks into soft, flexible rounds designed to absorb the sauces of a Sudanese meal.

Fringe Table adaptation

About this dish

Gurasa is a Sudanese wheat flatbread that can range from pancake-like to thicker griddle bread depending on household and region. It is commonly eaten with stews, beans and meat sauces and should remain soft enough to tear and scoop.

Story & history

Why this dish matters

Alongside kisra, gurasa is part of Sudan's diverse bread traditions. Wheat-based gurasa is especially practical in home kitchens without the specialized surfaces used for some traditional breads.

Thickness and fermentation vary widely; this version uses a short yeast fermentation and skillet cooking.

Before you begin

Preparation notes

Keep dough soft

Stiff dough gives tough bread.

Rest until bubbly

Fermentation improves texture.

Do not overbrown

Gurasa should stay soft.

Cook it

Ingredients & detailed method

Ingredients

  • 3 cups all-purpose flour
  • 1 tsp instant yeast
  • 1 tsp salt
  • 2 1/4 cups warm water
  • 1 tbsp oil

Method

  1. 1.Whisk flour, yeast and salt.
  2. 2.Add warm water to make a thick pourable batter-dough.
  3. 3.Cover and rest 35–45 minutes until visibly bubbly.
  4. 4.Lightly oil a skillet and pour about 1/2 cup batter, spreading into a round.
  5. 5.Cook until the surface loses its wet shine, then flip briefly.
  6. 6.Checkpoint: bread should be cooked through but still flexible and pale-golden rather than crisp.
  7. 7.Stack under a towel and serve warm.
