Sudan / Northeast Africa

Sudanese Gurasa

Bread · 1 hr 15 min · 8 flatbreads

A loose, lightly fermented wheat batter cooks into thick, spongy rounds made to stay supple and absorb the sauces of a Sudanese meal.

Fringe Table adaptation

About this dish

Gurasa is a northern Sudanese wheat bread cooked on a hot surface into a round that is thicker than a conventional pancake yet softer than a crisp flatbread. Its open, spongy crumb is especially suited to soaking up mullah—Sudanese stews and sauces.

Whole-wheat flour gives this version the wheaty flavor documented by Sudanese Kitchen. The batter is poured rather than rolled, and a short yeast fermentation supplies both lift and tenderness.

Story & history

Why this dish matters

Sudan does not have a single bread tradition. Gurasa belongs beside breads such as sorghum-based kisra, but its wheat, thickness and griddle method give it a distinct place at the table. It is torn into pieces and used to gather sauce, making texture as important as browning.

Sudanese food writer Omer Al Tijani describes gurasa as a household staple while emphasizing that Sudanese cooking changes across a large and culturally diverse country. Treat the bread as a living family food, not a fixed national formula.

Thickness, flour choice and fermentation vary. This adaptation follows a whole-wheat, short-fermented style and uses a skillet in place of a traditional saaj.

Before you begin

Preparation notes

Aim for thick batter

It should pour slowly and spread with the back of a ladle; it is not a kneadable dough.

Watch the bowl, not the clock

Fermentation is ready when the batter has expanded and bubbles are visible throughout.

Protect the steam

Stack cooked rounds under a slightly damp towel so they remain soft enough to tear.

Cook it

Ingredients & detailed method

Ingredients

  • 3 cups (390 g) whole-wheat flour
  • 1 tablespoon instant yeast
  • 2 teaspoons sugar
  • 2 teaspoons fine salt
  • 3 3/4 to 4 cups (900 ml–1 L) lukewarm water
  • Neutral oil or ghee, for the pan

Method

  1. 1.Whisk the flour, yeast, sugar and salt in a large bowl so the yeast and salt are evenly distributed.
  2. 2.Add 3 3/4 cups lukewarm water gradually while whisking. Continue until no dry pockets remain and the mixture resembles a thick pancake batter; whisk in more water as needed.
  3. 3.Cover the bowl and leave in a warm place for 25–40 minutes. Checkpoint: the batter should increase noticeably in volume and show bubbles across the surface and through the sides of the bowl.
  4. 4.Stir the risen batter gently once. It should still pour in a thick ribbon. If it has become too stiff, mix in lukewarm water 1 tablespoon at a time.
  5. 5.Heat a 10- to 12-inch nonstick or well-seasoned skillet over medium. Wipe it with a thin film of oil or ghee; the surface should be hot without smoking.
  6. 6.Pour about 2/3 cup batter into the center and use the back of the ladle to spread it into a thick 7- to 8-inch round.
  7. 7.Cook for 2–3 minutes, until the top loses its wet shine, bubbles have opened and the underside is golden. Flip carefully and cook the second side for 1–2 minutes.
  8. 8.Checkpoint: the center should be cooked through and spring back lightly, while the round remains bendable. Lower the heat if the outside darkens before the middle sets.
  9. 9.Transfer to a plate and immediately cover with a clean, slightly damp towel. Repeat, wiping the skillet with more fat only when needed.
  10. 10.Serve warm, tearing pieces to scoop a Sudanese stew. Keep covered until the moment of serving.
