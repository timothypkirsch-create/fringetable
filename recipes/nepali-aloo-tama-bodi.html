Nepali Aloo Tama Bodi

Main / Side · 55 min · 6 servings

Potatoes, beans and fermented bamboo shoots in a tangy Nepali curry whose sour edge is the point, not something to tame away.

Fringe Table adaptation

About this dish

Aloo tama combines potatoes with tama—fermented bamboo shoots—and often bodi, or black-eyed peas. The bamboo contributes a sharp, fermented sourness that distinguishes the curry from more familiar potato-and-legume dishes. It is especially associated with Newar cooking while also being widely recognized across Nepal.

Story & history

Why this dish matters

A Lalitpur municipal study of intangible cultural heritage identifies aloo tama as a distinctive curry in Nepalese cuisine and specifically notes its place in Newari food culture. Family and restaurant versions vary in whether black-eyed peas are included and how strongly fermented the bamboo is, but the characteristic sour aroma of tama is what gives the dish its identity. Rather than neutralizing that flavor, the potatoes and beans give it a mellow base to work against.

Fermented bamboo shoots vary dramatically in salt, acidity and aroma. Taste your tama before seasoning the pot, and expect household recipes to differ in spice level, tomato and bean use.

Before you begin

Preparation notes

Taste the bamboo first

Some packaged fermented bamboo is intensely salty or sour. Rinse briefly only if needed; do not wash away all of its character.

Par-cook dried beans

If starting with dried black-eyed peas, cook them until nearly tender before beginning the curry.

Keep potato pieces substantial

One-inch chunks soften into the broth without disappearing during the simmer.

Cook it

Ingredients & detailed method

Ingredients

  • 2 tbsp mustard oil or neutral oil
  • 1 tsp fenugreek seeds
  • 1 medium red onion, finely chopped
  • 3 garlic cloves, minced
  • 1 tbsp grated ginger
  • 1/2 tsp turmeric
  • 1 tsp ground cumin
  • 1 tsp ground coriander
  • 2 medium tomatoes, chopped
  • 1 1/2 lb waxy potatoes, peeled and cut into 1-inch chunks
  • 1 1/2 cups cooked black-eyed peas
  • 1 1/2 cups fermented bamboo shoots, drained and sliced
  • 3 cups water, plus more as needed
  • 1 tsp kosher salt, plus more only after tasting
  • 1/2 tsp timur pepper or black pepper, optional
  • Chopped cilantro, for serving

Method

  1. 1.Heat mustard oil in a heavy pot over medium heat. Add fenugreek seeds and cook until they darken and become fragrant, about 30 seconds; do not let them burn black.
  2. 2.Add onion and cook 6–8 minutes until soft and beginning to brown at the edges. Add garlic and ginger for 1 minute.
  3. 3.Stir in turmeric, cumin and coriander with 2 tablespoons water. Cook 30 seconds until aromatic.
  4. 4.Add tomatoes and cook 5–7 minutes. Checkpoint: they should collapse into a thick paste with oil beginning to glisten around the edges.
  5. 5.Add potatoes and toss for 2 minutes so their surfaces pick up the spice base.
  6. 6.Add black-eyed peas, bamboo shoots and 3 cups water. Bring to a simmer, partially cover and cook 20–25 minutes.
  7. 7.Checkpoint: potatoes should be tender all the way through but still hold their shape; the broth should taste distinctly sour from the bamboo rather than merely tomato-tangy.
  8. 8.Taste before adding the full amount of salt. Season carefully, then add timur or black pepper if desired.
  9. 9.Rest 5 minutes and serve with rice, roti or as part of a larger Nepali meal.
