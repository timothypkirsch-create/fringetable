Eastern Band of Cherokee Indians / Indigenous Americas

Cherokee Buffalo, Hominy and Bean Hot Pot

Stew / Main · 45 min · 4 servings

Bison, hominy and beans come together in a straightforward Cherokee hot pot published by the Eastern Band of Cherokee Indians’ own Tribal Foods program.

Fringe Table adaptation

About this dish

This recipe is based on Buffalo, Hominy and Bean Hot Pot published by Tribal Foods, an Eastern Band of Cherokee Indians resource. The original recipe is credited to Watson Harlan and described as a hearty home favorite suitable for beginner cooks. It intentionally uses practical pantry ingredients such as canned hominy, beans and tomatoes alongside bison.

Story & history

Why this dish matters

The EBCI Tribal Foods project places recipes inside a broader Cherokee foodways effort rather than presenting them as isolated curiosities. Its public recipe describes this hot pot as a home-style meal and explicitly frames canned foods as useful when drained, rinsed and seasoned thoughtfully. That modern practicality belongs to living Cherokee foodways just as much as historical ingredients do; this page therefore follows the community-published recipe instead of inventing an older or more ceremonial origin story.

This is a home adaptation of a specific recipe publicly shared by the Eastern Band of Cherokee Indians Tribal Foods program. It should not be generalized into a universal Cherokee dish or treated as ceremonial food knowledge.

Before you begin

Preparation notes

Drain and rinse canned ingredients

The source recipe specifically notes that canned foods benefit from draining and rinsing so excess canning liquid does not dominate the stew.

Brown bison quickly

Bison is lean. Brown it for flavor, then avoid prolonged hard cooking that can dry it out.

Keep the stew hearty, not soupy

Hominy and beans should remain prominent, with enough tomato-rich liquid to bind the pot rather than flood it.

Cook it

Ingredients & detailed method

Ingredients

  • 1 lb ground or small-diced bison
  • 1 tbsp cooking oil
  • 1/4 large onion, diced
  • 1 can (14.5 oz) diced tomatoes
  • 1 can (15 oz) hominy, drained and rinsed
  • 1 can (15 oz) pinto, kidney or other beans, drained and rinsed
  • 1/2 tsp kosher salt, plus more to taste
  • 1/4 tsp black pepper
  • 1/2 cup water, only if needed

Method

  1. 1.Drain and rinse the hominy and beans thoroughly. Dice the onion and prepare the bison before heating the pan.
  2. 2.Heat oil in a heavy skillet or Dutch oven over medium-high heat. Add bison and brown for 4–5 minutes, breaking ground meat into substantial pieces rather than tiny crumbs.
  3. 3.Add onion and cook 2–3 minutes until it begins to soften.
  4. 4.Add tomatoes, hominy and beans. Stir gently and bring to a simmer.
  5. 5.Reduce heat to medium-low and cook 12–15 minutes, stirring occasionally. Add a small amount of water only if the mixture threatens to catch on the bottom.
  6. 6.Checkpoint: bison should be cooked through but still juicy, hominy should be tender and the tomato liquid should lightly coat the beans rather than pool around them.
  7. 7.Season with salt and pepper and serve hot.
