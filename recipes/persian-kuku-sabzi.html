Iran / Persia

Persian Kuku Sabzi

Main · 45 min · Serves 6

A deeply green, herb-forward kuku in which eggs bind parsley, cilantro, dill and scallions rather than dominating them.

Fringe Table adaptation

About this dish

Kuku is a Persian egg-based dish, but kuku sabzi reverses the usual omelet ratio: the greens dominate and the eggs simply hold them together. Parsley, cilantro, dill and scallions or chives create the dense green interior, while walnuts and tart barberries are common additions.

This version starts on the stovetop and finishes in the oven for even cooking, though many cooks prepare kuku entirely in a skillet.

Story & history

Green herbs on the Nowruz table

Kuku sabzi is especially associated with Nowruz, the Persian New Year celebrated at the spring equinox. Encyclopaedia Iranica documents kuku-ye sabzi among foods connected with Nowruz, while contemporary Persian food writers emphasize the symbolic fit between its abundance of fresh green herbs and the season of renewal.

The dish is older and broader than a single holiday appearance: kuku belongs to a long Persian culinary tradition of egg-bound vegetable and herb dishes. Family versions vary in herb balance, thickness, walnuts, barberries and cooking method.

This is one home-kitchen style. Holiday and regional versions may use different herb mixtures or be cooked entirely on the stovetop.

Before you begin

Preparation notes

Dry the herbs very well

Water trapped on the greens steams the kuku and can make the center spongy instead of tender.

Chop, do not puree

Fine pieces distribute evenly, but herb paste releases too much moisture and produces a muddy texture.

Expect far more herbs than egg

The raw mixture should look almost impossibly green. If it resembles a conventional omelet batter, there are not enough herbs.

Cook it

Ingredients & detailed method

Ingredients

  • 6 large eggs
  • 1 packed cup finely chopped parsley
  • 1 packed cup finely chopped cilantro
  • 1 packed cup finely chopped dill
  • 1 packed cup thinly sliced scallions or chives
  • 2 tbsp finely chopped walnuts, plus more for garnish
  • 2 tbsp dried barberries, rinsed and thoroughly dried, plus more for garnish
  • 1/2 tsp ground turmeric
  • 1 tsp kosher salt
  • 1/2 tsp black pepper
  • 2 tbsp olive oil or neutral oil

Method

  1. 1.Heat the oven. Set it to 350°F and place a rack in the center.
  2. 2.Prepare the herbs. Wash, dry and finely chop all greens. Checkpoint: they should feel dry to the touch and remain fluffy rather than wet and compacted.
  3. 3.Whisk the eggs. Beat eggs with turmeric, salt and pepper only until uniform; excessive aeration is unnecessary.
  4. 4.Fold in the greens. Add herbs, walnuts and barberries and fold until evenly distributed.
  5. 5.Check the ratio. Checkpoint: the mixture should appear mostly herbs held together by a thin coating of egg.
  6. 6.Preheat the skillet. Warm oil in a 9-inch oven-safe skillet over medium heat and swirl it over the bottom and sides.
  7. 7.Start the kuku. Pour in the mixture and gently level the top. Cook 2–3 minutes until the outer edge begins to look set.
  8. 8.Bake. Transfer the skillet to the oven for 15–20 minutes.
  9. 9.Check doneness. Checkpoint: the center should no longer look wet and should spring lightly when pressed, while remaining moist rather than dry or browned hard.
  10. 10.Rest. Cool in the skillet for 10 minutes so the structure firms before cutting.
  11. 11.Serve. Cut into wedges and garnish with walnuts and barberries. Serve warm or at room temperature.
