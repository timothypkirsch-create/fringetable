Racha, Georgia / Caucasus

Rachan Lobiani (Georgian Bean-Filled Bread)

Bread / Main · 11 hrs · 6 filled breads

Beans become bread filling in lobiani; in Racha, smoked pork adds another layer of mountain flavor before the filled rounds go into a very hot oven.

Fringe Table adaptation

About this dish

Lobiani is Georgian bean-filled bread with strong regional associations. Georgia's national tourism authority describes Rachan lobiani as a particularly prized form made with selected beans and Rachan smoked ham. The bread is shaped by enclosing cooled bean filling inside risen yeast dough, then flattening the package before baking. This home version keeps that Rachan structure while using widely available dried beans and smoked pork when the regional ham is unobtainable.

Story & history

Why this dish matters

Georgia Travel connects lobiani with Racha's bean and smoked-pork traditions and also with Barbaroba, the December 17 feast of Saint Barbara, when bean-filled breads appear widely in Georgian homes. The tourism authority notes an older ritual connection to Barbale in which pumpkin-filled pastries were baked before beans became the customary filling. In Racha, some loaves are marked before baking with a carved wooden stamp bearing a sun disk and other designs, adding a regional visual tradition to an already distinctive bread.

Lobiani varies across Georgia and many versions are meatless. This recipe is specifically Rachan-style because it includes smoked pork; leaving the pork out produces a valid Georgian bean bread, but it should not be presented as identical to the ham-rich Rachan form described here. Rachan ham itself is a regional product, so smoked pork belly or a not-too-sweet smoked ham is only a practical substitute.

Before you begin

Preparation notes

Cook beans completely

The beans must mash easily while still warm. Firm centers will tear the dough when the bread is flattened.

Cool the filling

Hot filling weakens yeast dough and creates steam pockets. Let the mashed beans cool to room temperature before shaping.

Flatten from the center

Press the filled dough outward gently and evenly. Forcing one edge thin invites leaks while the center stays too thick.

Cook it

Ingredients & detailed method

Ingredients

  • 2 cups dried red kidney, cranberry or other medium red beans, soaked overnight and drained
  • 6 oz smoked ham or smoked pork belly, cut into small pieces
  • Water for cooking the beans
  • 1 tbsp pork fat, butter or neutral oil, plus more for finishing
  • 1 tsp kosher salt, plus more to taste
  • 1 tsp freshly ground black pepper
  • 4 cups bread flour or all-purpose flour, plus more for dusting
  • 2 tsp instant yeast
  • 1 1/2 tsp kosher salt for the dough
  • 1 1/2 cups warm water, about 100 to 105°F
  • 2 tbsp neutral oil

Method

  1. 1.Cover the soaked beans with fresh water by 2 inches and bring to a boil. Reduce to a lively simmer and cook 35 to 50 minutes. Add the smoked ham or pork for the final 20 minutes so it seasons the beans without disappearing completely.
  2. 2.Checkpoint: a bean should crush to a creamy paste between two fingers with no chalky center. Drain very well, reserving 1/2 cup cooking liquid, then mash the beans and pork with 1 tablespoon fat, 1 teaspoon salt and black pepper. Add reserved liquid only a spoonful at a time if the filling seems dry. Cool completely.
  3. 3.While the beans cook, mix flour, yeast and dough salt. Add warm water and oil, knead 7 to 9 minutes until smooth and elastic, then cover and rise about 1 hour or until roughly doubled.
  4. 4.Checkpoint: press the risen dough with a floured fingertip. The indentation should return slowly and leave a shallow mark; if it springs back immediately, give it another 10 to 15 minutes.
  5. 5.Heat the oven to 475°F with a baking stone, steel or heavy sheet pan preheating inside. Divide the dough into 6 equal pieces and the cooled filling into 6 portions.
  6. 6.Flatten one dough piece into a 6-inch round. Put a portion of filling in the center, gather the dough edges over it and pinch firmly closed. Turn seam-side down and rest 5 minutes. Repeat with the remaining pieces.
  7. 7.Working gently from the center outward, flatten each filled ball into a round about 7 to 8 inches across and roughly 1/2 inch thick. Patch any small split immediately with a pinch of dough rather than letting bean filling dry on the surface.
  8. 8.Bake in batches on the preheated surface for 10 to 13 minutes, rotating once, until the breads are puffed in spots and the top and bottom carry deep golden freckles. A finished lobiani should sound slightly hollow at an exposed dough edge when tapped.
  9. 9.Brush the hot breads lightly with butter, pork fat or oil. Rest 5 minutes before cutting so the filling sets enough to stay inside, then serve warm.
