Southern Vietnamese Bánh Xèo

Crisp Pancake / Main · 1 hr 10 min · 6 pancakes, 4 servings

The name promises the technique: batter should sizzle the instant it hits the pan, then cook into a brittle-edged yellow pancake wrapped around pork, shrimp and sprouts.

Fringe Table adaptation

About this dish

Bánh xèo are Vietnamese savory rice-flour pancakes whose name refers to the sizzling sound of batter meeting a hot pan. Versions differ significantly around Vietnam. SBS sources describing southern and Saigon-style bánh xèo emphasize a larger pancake, turmeric in the batter, coconut, pork or seafood, bean sprouts, fresh herbs and a fish-sauce dipping sauce. This recipe is therefore framed specifically as a southern-style home version rather than as a single national formula.

Story & history

Why this dish matters

Vietnamese cooks and food writers describe bánh xèo as both street food and a highly social dish because the crisp pancake is commonly broken into pieces, wrapped in lettuce and herbs, then dipped in nước chấm. Regional distinctions matter: central Vietnamese forms can be smaller and differently seasoned, while southern examples are often broad, turmeric-yellow and coconut-enriched. The dramatic sizzle is more than a name; it signals that the pan is hot enough to drive moisture from the batter quickly and create the brittle lace that separates good bánh xèo from a soft crepe.

This is a southern Vietnamese adaptation. Bánh xèo varies by region, household, filling and batter; do not treat coconut milk, pancake size or this exact pork-and-shrimp combination as universal throughout Vietnam.

Before you begin

Preparation notes

Rest the batter

Give the rice-flour batter about 30 minutes so the flour fully hydrates. Stir before every pancake because rice flour settles quickly.

Use a genuinely hot pan

A quiet pour produces a soft pancake. The batter should hiss immediately and race across a thin film of hot oil.

Do not overload the center

A modest amount of pork, shrimp and sprouts lets steam escape and gives the bottom enough contact with the pan to become crisp.

Cook it

Ingredients & detailed method

Ingredients

  • 1 1/2 cups rice flour
  • 2 tbsp cornstarch
  • 1 tsp ground turmeric
  • 1/2 tsp kosher salt
  • 1 cup unsweetened coconut milk
  • 1 1/4 cups cold water, plus 2 to 4 tbsp if needed
  • 3 scallions, thinly sliced
  • 8 oz pork belly or pork shoulder, sliced very thin
  • 8 oz medium shrimp, peeled, deveined and halved lengthwise if large
  • 1/2 small yellow onion, very thinly sliced
  • 2 cups fresh bean sprouts
  • Neutral oil, for the pan
  • 1 head soft lettuce, leaves separated, for serving
  • 1 cup mixed mint, Thai basil, cilantro and perilla leaves, as available
  • 1/4 cup Vietnamese fish sauce
  • 1/4 cup fresh lime juice
  • 1/4 cup granulated sugar
  • 1/2 cup warm water
  • 1 garlic clove, finely minced
  • 1 small red chile, thinly sliced, optional

Method

  1. 1.Whisk rice flour, cornstarch, turmeric and salt. Add coconut milk and 1 1/4 cups cold water and whisk until smooth, then stir in scallions. Rest 30 minutes. Meanwhile whisk fish sauce, lime juice, sugar, warm water, garlic and chile until the sugar dissolves; set the nước chấm aside.
  2. 2.Heat a 9- to 10-inch skillet over medium-high until a drop of water skitters and evaporates almost immediately. Add 1 teaspoon oil, then a few slices of pork and onion. Cook 60 to 90 seconds until the pork loses its raw surface and the onion smells sweet; add 2 to 3 shrimp pieces and cook just until their edges begin turning opaque.
  3. 3.Stir the batter from the bottom. Pour about 1/3 cup around the outer edge of the hot pan while tilting and rotating rapidly so it runs into a very thin layer. The pour should produce a loud, immediate sizzle. If the batter sits thickly without hissing, increase the heat before the next pancake.
  4. 4.Cover for 90 seconds to set the top, then uncover and scatter about 1/3 cup bean sprouts over one half. Drizzle 1 teaspoon oil around the rim and continue cooking uncovered 3 to 5 minutes.
  5. 5.Checkpoint: the outer edge should visibly lift and look dry and brittle, the underside should be deep golden rather than pale yellow, and the center should no longer look wet. Shake the pan; a properly crisp pancake should release with little resistance.
  6. 6.Fold the empty half over the filling and slide the bánh xèo onto a plate. Keep it uncovered so escaping steam does not soften the crust. Repeat with remaining batter and filling, wiping out any scorched bits and restoring the pan to high heat between pancakes.
  7. 7.Serve immediately with lettuce, herbs and nước chấm. Tear off a crisp section, wrap it with herbs in lettuce and dip. The ideal bite should combine a brittle edge, tender rice-flour center, juicy filling and cool aromatic greens.
