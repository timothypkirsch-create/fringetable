Great Lakes Ojibwe / Indigenous Americas

Ojibwe Manoomin Bagaan Porridge

Breakfast / Porridge · 20 min · 6 servings

Cooked manoomin becomes a warm, creamy porridge when it is briefly simmered with maple-sweetened milk, then finished with dried cranberries and crushed pecans.

Fringe Table adaptation

About this dish

The Great Lakes Indian Fish & Wildlife Commission published Manoomin Bagaan Porridge in its Winter 2025-2026 Mazina'igan newspaper as a practical community recipe. The formula starts with pre-cooked manoomin and combines it with milk, maple syrup, tallow, dried cranberries, pecans and optional warm spices. This page keeps that modern published structure intact and treats the dish as an Ojibwe community recipe rather than claiming that this exact dairy-and-pecan combination is an ancient or universal preparation.

Story & history

Why this dish matters

GLIFWC describes manoomin as 'the good berry' in Ojibwe and frames the relationship through Ganawenindiwag Manoomin Anishinaabeg - the rice and the people take care of each other. Manoomin is tied to Ojibwe migration histories, Great Lakes stewardship and food sovereignty, not simply to the commercial category called wild rice. GLIFWC serves eleven Ojibwe tribes in Minnesota, Wisconsin and Michigan, so its published porridge recipe is especially useful as a contemporary, community-grounded source. This adaptation stays with information GLIFWC has chosen to make public and does not extend into ceremonial practice.

For the closest result, use hand-harvested, traditionally finished or wood-parched manoomin from an Indigenous producer when available. Cultivated commercial wild rice is a practical substitute for cooking, but it is not culturally or texturally identical and may stay firmer. The GLIFWC recipe specifies tallow; butter can stand in for home convenience, but it changes the published formulation. If you have a cashew allergy, follow GLIFWC's warning and avoid sumac; this version does not require sumac.

Before you begin

Preparation notes

Start with cooked manoomin

The GLIFWC recipe is written for 4 cups pre-cooked manoomin. Cook and cool the grain first; Fringe Table's Anishinaabe manoomin stovetop recipe is a useful internal guide.

Keep the grain distinct

Manoomin should be tender and split at many kernels but still have chew. Overcooked rice can make the porridge pasty before the milk has time to thicken.

Crush the pecans coarsely

Small pieces provide texture and nut aroma; finely ground nuts can make the mixture heavy and oily.

Cook it

Ingredients & detailed method

Ingredients

  • 4 cups cooked hand-harvested or wood-parched manoomin (wild rice)
  • 3 1/2 cups whole milk or an unsweetened neutral milk substitute
  • 1/2 cup pure maple syrup
  • 1 tbsp rendered tallow, or 1 tbsp unsalted butter as a practical substitute
  • 1/2 tsp kosher salt, plus more to taste
  • 1/2 tsp ground cinnamon
  • 1/4 tsp ground cardamom, optional
  • 1/2 cup dried cranberries
  • 1/2 cup pecans, coarsely crushed

Method

  1. 1.Place the milk, maple syrup, tallow, salt, cinnamon and optional cardamom in a medium heavy saucepan. Whisk over medium heat until the tallow melts and the maple syrup is fully dispersed.
  2. 2.Bring the mixture just to a low boil, stirring frequently and scraping the bottom so the milk does not scorch. Checkpoint: small bubbles should break across the surface and the aroma should be maple-forward and warm-spiced, with no toasted or burnt-milk smell.
  3. 3.Add the 4 cups cooked manoomin and immediately reduce the heat to medium-low. Stir continuously for 4 to 5 minutes, following the short simmer used in GLIFWC's published recipe.
  4. 4.Checkpoint: the liquid should thicken to a loose porridge that coats a spoon, while individual manoomin grains remain visible and pleasantly chewy rather than dissolving into paste. If it tightens too quickly, add milk 2 tablespoons at a time.
  5. 5.Fold in the dried cranberries and crushed pecans. Cook 1 minute more, just until the cranberries begin to plump and the nuts smell fragrant.
  6. 6.Remove from the heat and rest 3 to 5 minutes. The porridge will continue thickening as it stands; loosen with a splash of warm milk if needed.
  7. 7.Taste for salt and maple balance, then serve warm. The finished bowl should be creamy around distinct grains, with soft-tart cranberry pieces and noticeable pecan texture rather than a uniformly smooth cereal.
