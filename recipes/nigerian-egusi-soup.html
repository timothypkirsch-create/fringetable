Nigeria / West Africa

Nigerian Egusi Soup

Main · 1 hr 15 min · Serves 6

A rich, deeply savory soup built from ground melon seeds, palm oil, greens and a flavorful stock.

Fringe Table adaptation

About this dish

Egusi soup is named for the protein- and fat-rich melon seeds that thicken it. Across Nigeria, the soup is prepared in many distinct ways and carries different names and techniques among communities. Some cooks fry the ground egusi; others cook it directly in stock; some deliberately form soft egusi curds or dumpling-like clusters.

This version is designed for a home cook who wants distinct egusi texture without an unnecessarily complicated ingredient list. Beef or goat gives the stock body, while crayfish adds another savory layer if available. Spinach works well in a U.S. kitchen, though ugu or properly prepared bitter leaf moves the flavor closer to Nigerian versions.

Story & history

A soup with many legitimate versions

Nigerian recipe writers repeatedly emphasize that there is no single egusi method. All Nigerian Recipes documents caking, fried and molded approaches, while reporting on contemporary Nigerian chefs shows egusi being reinterpreted without losing its cultural identity. That range is part of the dish rather than a problem to be solved.

This is a practical home adaptation, not a claim that one technique represents every Nigerian household or ethnic tradition.

Before you begin

Three things that make the difference

Use real egusi

Ground melon seed should smell fresh and nutty. Rancid seeds will make the whole pot taste stale.

Build a good stock

The egusi absorbs liquid aggressively, so bland stock produces bland soup. Season the meat broth before it meets the seeds.

Watch the oil

As the egusi cooks, the soup changes from raw and pasty to cohesive and glossy. Small beads of oil at the surface are a useful visual cue.

Cook it

Ingredients & detailed method

Ingredients

  • 2 cups ground egusi (melon) seeds
  • 1 1/2 lb beef, goat, or a combination, cut into serving pieces
  • 4 cups flavorful meat stock, plus more as needed
  • 1/2 cup red palm oil
  • 1 medium onion, finely chopped
  • 2 tbsp ground crayfish, optional
  • 1–2 Scotch bonnet or habanero peppers, minced
  • 2 cups chopped spinach, ugu, or properly prepared bitter leaf
  • 1 tsp kosher salt, plus more to taste
  • 1 tsp bouillon powder, optional

Method

  1. 1.Make the stock. Put the meat in a pot with half the onion, salt and enough water to barely cover. Simmer until tender, 30–45 minutes depending on the cut. Remove the meat and measure 4 cups of stock.
  2. 2.Make the egusi paste. Put ground egusi in a bowl. Stir in about 3/4 cup warm stock a little at a time until you have a thick paste that holds on a spoon rather than running.
  3. 3.Start the base. Heat palm oil over medium-low heat. Add the remaining onion and cook 4–5 minutes until softened, not browned.
  4. 4.Set the egusi. Drop heaping spoonfuls of paste into the oil. Leave them alone for 2 minutes so the undersides set, then turn gently. This creates soft curds instead of dissolving everything immediately.
  5. 5.Add stock gradually. Pour in 2 cups stock around the egusi, not directly over every curd. Bring to a gentle simmer. Cook 10 minutes, stirring carefully around the edges.
  6. 6.Cook out the raw seed flavor. Add another cup of stock and simmer 10–15 minutes. The egusi should taste nutty rather than raw and chalky; small beads of oil should begin appearing on top.
  7. 7.Season the soup. Stir in crayfish, Scotch bonnet and bouillon if using. Add the cooked meat back to the pot. Simmer 8–10 minutes. Add stock as needed for a thick spoonable consistency.
  8. 8.Add the greens last. Fold in spinach or ugu and cook 2–4 minutes, just until tender. Bitter leaf may need a little longer depending on preparation.
  9. 9.Balance and rest. Taste for salt and heat. Turn off the burner and rest the soup 5–10 minutes; it will thicken slightly as it stands.
  10. 10.Serve. Spoon into bowls and serve with eba, pounded yam, fufu, semolina or another preferred swallow.
