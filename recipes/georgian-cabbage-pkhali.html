Georgian Cabbage Pkhali

Side · 50 min · 8 appetizer servings

A walnut-rich Georgian vegetable preparation where cabbage becomes a savory, tangy spread rather than a background side.

Fringe Table adaptation

About this dish

Pkhali is a broad Georgian family of chopped or minced vegetable dishes bound with seasoned walnuts. Spinach, beet greens, beans, cabbage and other vegetables can all become pkhali. Georgia Travel describes walnut-based pkhali assortments as a fixture of Georgian vegetarian cooking and highlights regional variation across the country.

Story & history

Why this dish matters

The defining idea is not one vegetable but the relationship between cooked greens or vegetables and a walnut dressing sharpened with garlic, herbs and acid. Georgian sources describe many pkhali forms, from cultivated vegetables to spring field greens. Cabbage pkhali is one documented branch of that larger tradition, often seasoned with coriander, blue fenugreek and wine vinegar.

Pkhali changes by region, season and household. This page focuses specifically on cabbage pkhali rather than treating one walnut mixture as universal.

Before you begin

Preparation notes

Cook cabbage until soft

The leaves need to be tender enough to chop finely without staying stringy.

Squeeze aggressively

Excess water is the biggest enemy of good pkhali texture.

Grind walnuts finely

The walnut mixture should bind the cabbage into a cohesive spread rather than feel like loose chopped nuts.

Cook it

Ingredients & detailed method

Ingredients

  • 2 lb green cabbage, cored and chopped
  • 2 cups walnut halves
  • 3 garlic cloves
  • 1 tsp ground coriander
  • 1/2 tsp ground blue fenugreek
  • 1/2 tsp mild red pepper or paprika
  • 1 small onion, minced very finely
  • 3 to 4 tbsp white wine vinegar, plus more to taste
  • 1 tsp kosher salt, plus more to taste
  • Pomegranate seeds, for garnish
  • Cilantro leaves, optional garnish

Method

  1. 1.Bring a large pot of salted water to a boil. Add cabbage and cook 18–22 minutes until fully tender.
  2. 2.Drain cabbage thoroughly and cool until safe to handle. Squeeze handfuls firmly to remove as much water as possible. Checkpoint: squeezed cabbage should feel damp but should not drip when pressed.
  3. 3.Chop the cabbage very finely and place in a large bowl.
  4. 4.Pulse walnuts, garlic, coriander, blue fenugreek, red pepper and salt in a food processor until finely ground but not turned into oily nut butter.
  5. 5.Mix walnut seasoning with onion and 3 tablespoons vinegar.
  6. 6.Work the walnut mixture into the cabbage with clean hands or a sturdy spoon until evenly distributed. Checkpoint: the mixture should hold together when pressed into a small mound.
  7. 7.Taste and add more vinegar or salt as needed. Chill at least 30 minutes so the flavors settle.
  8. 8.Shape into small mounds or patties and garnish with pomegranate seeds and cilantro if desired. Serve cool or at room temperature.
