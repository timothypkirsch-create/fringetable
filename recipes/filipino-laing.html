Bicol / Philippines

Filipino Laing

Main / Side · 1 hr · 6 servings

Dried taro leaves slowly absorb coconut milk until the pot turns thick, lush and chile-warm.

Fringe Table adaptation

About this dish

Laing is strongly associated with Bicol in the Philippines, where coconut milk and chile are prominent. Dried taro leaves are simmered patiently until fully cooked and tender; undercooked taro can irritate the mouth and throat.

Story & history

Why this dish matters

Bicolano cuisine is well known for coconut milk and chile, and laing is one of its signature dishes. Versions may include pork, dried fish or shrimp paste; this version uses shrimp paste for savory depth.

Taro leaves must be thoroughly cooked. Use food-grade dried leaves intended for cooking.

Before you begin

Preparation notes

Do not stir early

Agitating raw taro leaves excessively can increase irritation.

Cook thoroughly

Leaves must lose all raw itchiness.

Reduce slowly

Coconut milk should become rich, not scorch.

Cook it

Ingredients & detailed method

Ingredients

  • 4 oz dried taro leaves
  • 2 cans coconut milk
  • 1 cup coconut cream
  • 1 onion
  • 5 garlic cloves
  • 1 tbsp ginger
  • 1 tbsp shrimp paste
  • 2 red chiles
  • Salt to taste

Method

  1. 1.Combine coconut milk, onion, garlic, ginger and shrimp paste; bring to a gentle simmer.
  2. 2.Lay dried taro leaves over the liquid without vigorous stirring.
  3. 3.Cover and simmer 20 minutes.
  4. 4.Gently fold leaves into the liquid and add chile.
  5. 5.Simmer uncovered 20–25 minutes with coconut cream.
  6. 6.Checkpoint: leaves should be completely tender with no prickly sensation and sauce thick enough to cling.
  7. 7.Adjust seasoning and serve with rice.
