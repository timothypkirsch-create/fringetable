Roasted Squash with Maple & Pepitas

Side · 50 min · 6 servings

Winter squash caramelizes at high heat before a restrained maple glaze and toasted seeds add sweetness and crunch.

Fringe Table adaptation

About this dish

Squash and maple both have deep histories in Indigenous foodways of northeastern North America. This recipe is intentionally modern: oven-roasted squash finished with maple and pepitas. It connects documented ingredients without falsely labeling the exact combination as a traditional community dish.

Story & history

Why this dish matters

Squash was cultivated across Indigenous North America long before European arrival, while maple sugaring traditions remain strongly associated with Indigenous communities of the Northeast and Great Lakes. Modern recipes can use these ingredients while being explicit about what is adaptation rather than inherited formula.

This is a contemporary recipe inspired by documented Indigenous ingredients, not a specific tribal traditional preparation.

Before you begin

Preparation notes

Cut evenly

Uniform pieces brown at the same rate.

Do not add maple early

It can burn before squash is tender.

Toast seeds separately

They stay crisper.

Cook it

Ingredients & detailed method

Ingredients

  • 3 lb winter squash, peeled and cubed
  • 2 tbsp sunflower oil
  • 1 tsp salt
  • 2 tbsp maple syrup
  • 1/2 cup pepitas
  • 1/4 tsp ground chile, optional

Method

  1. 1.Heat oven to 425°F.
  2. 2.Toss squash with oil and salt and spread in one layer.
  3. 3.Roast 25 minutes, turning once.
  4. 4.Add maple syrup and optional chile and roast 8–10 minutes more.
  5. 5.Toast pepitas in a dry skillet until they begin to pop.
  6. 6.Checkpoint: squash should be browned at edges and fully creamy inside without collapsing.
  7. 7.Scatter pepitas over the squash and serve hot.
