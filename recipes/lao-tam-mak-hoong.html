Laos / Southeast Asia

Lao Tam Mak Hoong

Salad / Side · 25 min · 4 servings

Crunchy green papaya bruised just enough to drink in chile, lime and fermented fish—the Lao pounded salad in its own right.

Fringe Table adaptation

About this dish

Tam mak hoong is Lao green papaya salad. Shredded unripe papaya is bruised in a mortar with garlic, chile, long beans, tomato and a savory-sour dressing. Padaek, Lao fermented fish sauce, is a defining ingredient in many versions and gives the Lao salad a deeper fermented character than sweeter neighboring forms.

Story & history

Why this dish matters

Pha Khao Lao describes tam mak hoong as both a snack and part of a meal and stresses that the balance is highly personal: some versions are sweeter or sourer, while regional salads can use crab paste or other local fermented seasonings. The method matters as much as the formula. The mortar should bruise and season the papaya, not crush it into wet pulp. Sticky rice commonly accompanies the salad and helps balance its chile and fermented intensity.

Padaek can be pungent and varies by producer. Use a commercially prepared product intended for ready-to-eat seasoning, or substitute standard fish sauce with the understanding that the flavor becomes cleaner and less distinctly Lao.

Before you begin

Preparation notes

Use truly green papaya

Ripe papaya is sweet and soft and cannot provide the required crisp texture.

Pound in stages

Garlic and chile need more force than papaya and tomato. Add delicate ingredients later.

Keep the papaya crunchy

Once strands soften slightly and absorb dressing, stop. More pounding is not better.

Cook it

Ingredients & detailed method

Ingredients

  • 1 small green papaya, peeled, seeded and shredded into about 5 cups strands
  • 2 garlic cloves
  • 2 to 5 fresh bird's eye chiles, to taste
  • 1/2 tsp kosher salt
  • 2 tsp palm or brown sugar, plus more to taste
  • 1 1/2 tbsp padaek or 1 tbsp fish sauce
  • 2 tbsp fresh lime juice
  • 6 yardlong beans, cut into 2-inch pieces
  • 1 cup cherry tomatoes, halved
  • 1/2 tsp shrimp or crab paste, optional

Method

  1. 1.Pound garlic, chiles and salt in a large mortar until broken into a coarse paste.
  2. 2.Add sugar, padaek, lime juice and shrimp or crab paste if using. Pound and stir until the sugar dissolves.
  3. 3.Add long beans and pound 4–5 times to bruise them while keeping distinct pieces.
  4. 4.Add tomatoes and press gently with the pestle just until they release some juice.
  5. 5.Add shredded papaya in two or three additions, using a spoon to lift strands from the bottom while the pestle lightly bruises from above.
  6. 6.Checkpoint: papaya should bend more easily and look glossy with dressing but remain crisp and separate, never mashed.
  7. 7.Taste for the balance you prefer—salty-fermented, hot and sour, with sweetness in a supporting role. Adjust lime, padaek, chile or sugar and serve immediately with sticky rice.
