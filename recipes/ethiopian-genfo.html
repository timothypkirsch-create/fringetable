Ethiopia / Horn of Africa

Ethiopian Genfo

Breakfast / Staple · 40 min · 4 servings

Flour and water become a dense, smooth breakfast porridge built around the brilliant red pool of spiced butter at its center.

Fringe Table adaptation

About this dish

Genfo is a thick Ethiopian porridge prepared from cereal flour and water. Wheat, barley, maize and other grains are used depending on household and region. The cooked porridge is shaped in a bowl and a well is made in the center for niter kibbeh mixed with berbere; diners pull pieces from the outer mound and dip them into the seasoned fat.

Story & history

Why this dish matters

An Ethiopian Ministry of Labor and Skills training module describes genfo as a common breakfast made by stirring roasted cereal flour into boiling water until extremely thick and smooth, then filling a central well with clarified spiced butter and berbere. Published Ethiopian home recipes also associate genfo with nourishment for new mothers. The exact flour varies, which is why this accessible version uses wheat while acknowledging barley and maize traditions.

Regional names, grains and accompaniments differ, including ga'at in Tigrinya-speaking areas. Claims about postpartum health benefits should be understood as cultural practice rather than medical advice.

Before you begin

Preparation notes

Use a sturdy spoon

The mixture becomes much thicker than oatmeal and requires force to fold smooth.

Add flour gradually

Dumping it all at once traps dry pockets that are difficult to remove.

Serve immediately

Genfo firms dramatically as it cools.

Cook it

Ingredients & detailed method

Ingredients

  • 2 cups all-purpose or whole-wheat flour
  • 4 cups water, plus more as needed
  • 1/2 tsp ground cardamom, optional
  • 1/2 tsp kosher salt
  • 4 tbsp niter kibbeh
  • 1 to 2 tbsp berbere, to taste
  • Plain yogurt, optional for serving

Method

  1. 1.Whisk flour with cardamom if using. Bring water and salt to a strong boil in a heavy saucepan.
  2. 2.Reduce heat to medium-low and add the flour gradually while stirring vigorously with a sturdy wooden spoon.
  3. 3.Continue stirring, pressing and folding for 10–15 minutes. Add small splashes of hot water only if dry flour remains trapped.
  4. 4.Checkpoint: the porridge should become smooth, glossy and extremely thick enough to pull away from parts of the pan in one mass.
  5. 5.Transfer to a shallow bowl and smooth into a mound. Use the back of a cup or spoon to press a deep well into the center.
  6. 6.Melt niter kibbeh gently in a small pan and stir in berbere for about 30 seconds, just until fragrant.
  7. 7.Pour the red seasoned butter into the central well. Serve very hot, optionally with yogurt alongside, pulling spoonfuls of porridge through the spiced butter.
