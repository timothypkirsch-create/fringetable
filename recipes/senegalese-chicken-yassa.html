Senegal / Casamance / West Africa

Senegalese Chicken Yassa

Main · 4 hrs · 6 servings

Chicken spends hours with lemon and onions before meeting the grill or broiler, then returns to a mountain of soft, sharp onions.

Fringe Table adaptation

About this dish

Chicken yassa is a Senegalese dish strongly associated with Casamance and built around an unusually large quantity of onions, citrus and chile. The chicken is marinated, browned separately and then finished in the softened onion marinade. Mustard appears in many contemporary versions, though simpler formulas may focus almost entirely on lemon, onion and oil.

Story & history

Why this dish matters

Food historian Jessica B. Harris has written about first tasting chicken yassa in Senegal and describes the contrast of lemon, chile, onion and chicken as foundational to the dish. The browned chicken and long-cooked onions create a flavor much deeper than the short ingredient list suggests. Recipes vary enough that even Senegalese cooks disagree about olives, carrots, mustard and sweetness.

Yassa varies by household and region. This version uses mustard and olives as documented additions while keeping lemon and onions dominant.

Before you begin

Preparation notes

Marinate at least two hours

The onions and citrus season the chicken and generate the sauce base.

Brown chicken outside the onion sauce

Broiling or grilling adds roasted flavor before the pieces return to the braise.

Give onions time

The sauce should be mostly softened onion, not a watery lemon broth.

Cook it

Ingredients & detailed method

Ingredients

  • 1 whole chicken, about 3 1/2 lb, cut into 8 pieces
  • 1/3 cup fresh lemon juice
  • 5 large onions, thinly sliced
  • 1/4 cup peanut or neutral oil, divided
  • 1 habanero, minced or left whole for less heat
  • 1 tbsp Dijon mustard
  • 1 tsp kosher salt, plus more to taste
  • 1/2 tsp black pepper
  • 1/2 cup water or chicken stock
  • 1/2 cup green olives, optional
  • Cooked white rice, for serving

Method

  1. 1.Combine chicken, lemon juice, onions, 3 tablespoons oil, chile, salt and pepper. Refrigerate at least 2 hours, turning once.
  2. 2.Remove chicken and shake off onions, reserving all marinade. Broil or grill the pieces until browned on both sides; they do not need to be cooked through yet.
  3. 3.Heat remaining oil in a Dutch oven. Add the marinated onions with as little liquid as possible and cook over medium-low heat 20–25 minutes until very soft.
  4. 4.Add reserved marinade, mustard and water or stock and bring to a simmer.
  5. 5.Nestle in the browned chicken and olives if using. Cover partially and simmer 20–25 minutes.
  6. 6.Checkpoint: chicken must reach at least 165°F at the thickest part and onions should have collapsed into a glossy, intensely savory sauce.
  7. 7.Taste for salt and lemon balance and serve hot over white rice.
