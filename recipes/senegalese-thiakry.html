Senegal / West Africa

Senegalese Thiakry

Dessert · 1 hr + chilling · Serves 6

Tender millet grains folded into cool sweetened cultured dairy with vanilla and nutmeg.

Fringe Table adaptation

About this dish

Thiakry combines millet couscous or granules with sweetened dairy. The grain should remain distinct enough to give the dessert texture while the yogurt mixture stays loose, cool and creamy. Vanilla, nutmeg, raisins and other flavorings vary by household.

This version uses packaged millet couscous for accessibility; traditionally prepared millet granules may require steaming rather than simple absorption cooking.

Story & history

Millet, dairy and celebration

Millet has deep roots across the Sahel and remains especially important in areas where it thrives under dry conditions. Senegalese thiakry turns that staple grain into a cool dessert by pairing it with sweetened cultured dairy.

Senegalese cultural food sources describe thiakry as both everyday refreshment and a dish for gatherings, naming ceremonies, Ramadan evenings and other celebrations. Modern versions may use yogurt, fermented milk, evaporated milk or condensed milk in different combinations.

Sweetness, dairy type and grain texture vary significantly. This version aims for a lightly sweet, spoonable balance rather than a fixed “authentic” ratio.

Before you begin

Preparation notes

Cool the millet completely

Warm grain can thin or curdle the yogurt mixture and gives the dessert a heavy texture.

Separate every clump

The pleasure of thiakry is in tiny distinct grains suspended in dairy, not dense millet lumps.

Adjust after chilling

Millet continues to absorb liquid in the refrigerator, so the final consistency should be checked just before serving.

Make it

Ingredients & detailed method

Ingredients

  • 1 cup millet couscous or fine millet granules
  • 1 1/4 cups water
  • Pinch of salt
  • 2 cups plain whole-milk yogurt
  • 1/2 cup evaporated milk or whole milk
  • 1/3 cup sweetened condensed milk, or sugar to taste
  • 1 tsp vanilla extract
  • 1/4 tsp freshly grated nutmeg
  • 2 tbsp raisins, optional

Method

  1. 1.Cook the millet. Follow the package directions for your millet couscous, using the listed water and a pinch of salt as a starting point.
  2. 2.Check the grain. Checkpoint: the millet should be tender but still granular rather than wet and porridge-like.
  3. 3.Fluff immediately. Spread the cooked grain on a wide tray and rake it with a fork to release steam.
  4. 4.Cool completely. Let the millet reach room temperature, then chill if necessary. Break up clumps with clean fingertips.
  5. 5.Make the dairy mixture. Whisk yogurt, milk, condensed milk or sugar, vanilla and nutmeg until smooth.
  6. 6.Set the sweetness. Taste before adding all the sweetener. It should taste pleasantly sweet but still tangy.
  7. 7.Fold in the millet. Add the cooled grains in several additions, folding so they stay separated.
  8. 8.Add raisins if using. Stir them in, then refrigerate at least 30 minutes.
  9. 9.Check after chilling. Checkpoint: the mixture should be creamy and spoonable with grains suspended throughout. If too thick, loosen with milk one tablespoon at a time.
  10. 10.Serve cold. Taste once more for sweetness and nutmeg before portioning into bowls or glasses.
