Laos / Southeast Asia

Lao Sticky Rice (Khao Niew)

Staple / Side · 7 hrs · 6 servings

The staple at the center of Lao meals: soaked glutinous rice steamed rather than boiled, then rested in a woven basket.

Fringe Table adaptation

About this dish

Khao niew, Lao sticky rice, is not a side note to Lao cooking. Laos's official tourism authority describes sticky rice as a long-standing staple and a symbol of culture, lifestyle and social bonds. The traditional method soaks glutinous rice for hours, then steams it above water in a cone-shaped bamboo huad so the grains cook through without becoming wet or porridge-like.

Story & history

Why this dish matters

Sticky rice is eaten with an extraordinary range of Lao foods and is traditionally kept warm in a woven katip khao basket. Pha Khao Lao documents that sticky rice accounts for a large share of rice consumption in Laos and gives a detailed home method using the huad and maw nung steamer. Diners commonly pinch off a small portion, shape it with the fingers and use it to accompany dishes such as larb and jeow.

Use Thai/Lao glutinous rice labeled sticky or sweet rice; ordinary jasmine rice does not become the same food. A metal steamer lined with cloth can work in a pinch, but the traditional bamboo huad handles moisture especially well.

Before you begin

Preparation notes

Soaking is essential

Six hours is a reliable baseline; older rice may need longer. Hot-water soaking can shorten the wait in a pinch.

Steam, do not boil

The rice should sit above the water. Direct boiling changes the texture completely.

Release steam before storing

Spreading the finished rice briefly keeps trapped condensation from turning the grains soggy in the basket.

Cook it

Ingredients & detailed method

Ingredients

  • 3 cups Lao or Thai glutinous rice
  • Plenty of cool water, for rinsing and soaking

Method

  1. 1.Rinse the glutinous rice briefly to remove loose husk or dust without washing excessively.
  2. 2.Cover with at least 1 inch of cool water and soak 6 hours or overnight. For a faster method, soak in hot water about 2 hours.
  3. 3.Drain the rice thoroughly. Bring water to a boil in a traditional sticky-rice steaming pot or another pot that can support a bamboo cone steamer above the water line.
  4. 4.Transfer rice to the dampened bamboo huad, level it loosely and cover with a bamboo lid or clean cloth.
  5. 5.Steam 15 minutes. Carefully flip or turn the mass of rice so the upper portion moves closer to the steam.
  6. 6.Steam another 10–15 minutes. Checkpoint: grains should be fully tender with no hard white core, cling together when pinched, and taste softly nutty rather than waterlogged.
  7. 7.Turn the rice onto a clean tray, cloth or banana leaf and spread it briefly with a wooden paddle to release excess steam.
  8. 8.Gather into a loose mound and transfer to a woven sticky-rice basket or covered serving container. Serve warm with larb, jeow, grilled foods or stews.
