Himachal Pradesh / India

Himachali Siddu

Bread / Main · 2 hrs · 8 buns

A slow rise and gentle steaming turn whole-wheat dough into soft Himachali buns wrapped around a coarse walnut-and-poppy-seed filling.

Fringe Table adaptation

About this dish

Siddu is a steamed wheat bread associated with Himachal Pradesh in northern India, especially the Kullu and Shimla areas. A yeasted dough encloses a filling that may be nutty, savory, sweet or lentil-based before the shaped bread is steamed rather than baked.

This version uses walnuts, poppy seeds, scallion and cumin. The filling should remain coarse and aromatic, while the bread should be fully cooked yet soft enough to absorb melted ghee.

Story & history

Why this dish matters

Siddu reflects a mountain bread tradition in which fermentation and steam make sturdy wheat easier to eat and keep the crumb moist. Contemporary Himachali sources document both sweet and savory fillings, including walnuts, poppy seeds and ground lentils.

It is commonly served warm with ghee, dal, chutney or a regional meat gravy. That range matters: siddu is a technique and family of filled breads, not one immutable stuffing.

Flour, shape, fermentation time and filling vary across Himachal Pradesh. This walnut version is one documented style adapted to a household steamer.

Before you begin

Preparation notes

Let the dough truly rise

Whole-wheat dough needs visible expansion; a warm room may take longer than one hour.

Keep the filling coarse

A paste can leak and turn heavy. Small walnut pieces preserve texture.

Test the center

Steam until the seam and thickest dough have no raw, tacky line.

Cook it

Ingredients & detailed method

Ingredients

  • 3 cups (390 g) whole-wheat flour, plus more for shaping
  • 1 1/2 teaspoons instant yeast
  • 1 teaspoon fine salt
  • 1 tablespoon neutral oil
  • 1 1/4 cups lukewarm water, plus more as needed
  • 1 cup walnuts, lightly toasted
  • 2 tablespoons poppy seeds, optional
  • 2 scallions, finely chopped
  • 1/2 teaspoon ground cumin
  • 1/2 teaspoon fine salt for the filling
  • 3 tablespoons ghee or melted butter, for serving

Method

  1. 1.Whisk the flour, yeast and salt in a large bowl. Add the oil and most of the lukewarm water; mix until no dry flour remains.
  2. 2.Knead 8–10 minutes, adding the remaining water a teaspoon at a time if needed. Checkpoint: the dough should feel soft and slightly tacky but hold its shape.
  3. 3.Cover and rise in a warm place for 60–90 minutes, until visibly puffy and nearly doubled. Judge the dough rather than the clock.
  4. 4.Pulse or chop the walnuts into coarse crumbs. Mix with the poppy seeds, scallions, cumin and filling salt; the mixture should hold loosely when squeezed without becoming oily paste.
  5. 5.Press out excess gas and divide the dough into 8 equal pieces. Cover and rest 10 minutes so the gluten relaxes.
  6. 6.Flatten one piece into a 4-inch round, leaving the center slightly thicker. Add about 2 tablespoons filling, gather the edges and pinch firmly closed. Repeat.
  7. 7.Set the buns seam-side down on individual parchment squares in a steamer, leaving space between them. Cover and rest 15 minutes while the steaming water comes to a boil.
  8. 8.Steam over steadily boiling water for 18–22 minutes without crowding the basket. Avoid opening the lid during the first 15 minutes.
  9. 9.Checkpoint: a bun should feel springy, and a skewer inserted through the thickest dough should emerge without sticky raw dough. Steam 3–5 minutes longer if needed.
  10. 10.Rest uncovered for 2 minutes so surface moisture evaporates. Brush generously with ghee and serve hot with more ghee, chutney, dal or gravy.
