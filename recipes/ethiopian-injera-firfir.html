Ethiopia / Horn of Africa

Ethiopian Injera Firfir

Breakfast / Main · 35 min · 4 servings

Yesterday's injera becomes breakfast when it meets a glossy berbere-tomato sauce and just enough heat to soften without dissolving.

Fringe Table adaptation

About this dish

Firfir is an Ethiopian way of dressing torn bread with a seasoned sauce, and injera firfir makes especially good use of leftover injera. Berbere-based versions are common for breakfast and quick meals. The goal is not soup: the pieces of injera should absorb the sauce and soften while remaining identifiable rather than becoming a uniform paste.

Story & history

Why this dish matters

Firfir reflects a practical part of Ethiopian bread culture: injera is valuable food, and yesterday's bread can be transformed rather than discarded. Contemporary Ethiopian recipe sources document versions built from onion, berbere, tomato and torn injera, while broader Ethiopian food scholarship emphasizes injera's central role as both staple and eating surface. Versions may include meat, clarified butter or different breads, but this page focuses on a straightforward plant-based injera firfir.

Firfir and fit-fit names and methods vary by region and household. This version specifically uses injera and a berbere-tomato sauce; other Ethiopian and Eritrean preparations can differ substantially.

Before you begin

Preparation notes

Use day-old injera

Slightly dry injera absorbs sauce without immediately collapsing.

Build the sauce before adding bread

Once injera goes in, the dish comes together quickly. Make sure the onions, berbere and tomato already taste finished.

Fold gently

Stirring aggressively can turn the softened injera into paste.

Cook it

Ingredients & detailed method

Ingredients

  • 3 large injera, preferably day-old, torn into 2-inch pieces
  • 3 tbsp neutral oil or niter kibbeh
  • 1 large red onion, finely chopped
  • 3 garlic cloves, minced
  • 1 tsp grated ginger
  • 1 1/2 to 2 tbsp berbere, to taste
  • 2 tbsp tomato paste
  • 2 medium tomatoes, finely chopped
  • 3/4 cup water, plus more as needed
  • 1 tsp kosher salt, plus more to taste
  • 1 jalapeño, thinly sliced, optional

Method

  1. 1.Tear the injera into irregular 2-inch pieces and set aside. If it is very fresh and moist, let the pieces sit uncovered while you make the sauce.
  2. 2.Heat oil or niter kibbeh in a wide skillet over medium heat. Add onion and cook 8–10 minutes until very soft and lightly golden.
  3. 3.Add garlic and ginger and cook 1 minute until fragrant.
  4. 4.Stir in berbere and 2 tablespoons water. Cook 1–2 minutes. Checkpoint: the spice should smell warm and rounded, not dry or scorched.
  5. 5.Add tomato paste and chopped tomatoes. Cook 5–7 minutes until thick and glossy.
  6. 6.Add the remaining water and salt and simmer 2 minutes. The sauce should be loose enough to soak into bread but not watery.
  7. 7.Add torn injera in two additions, folding gently after each. Cook 2–3 minutes over low heat.
  8. 8.Checkpoint: most sauce should be absorbed and the injera should be soft and coated, with individual pieces still visible. Add a splash of water only if dry patches remain.
  9. 9.Fold in sliced jalapeño if using and serve immediately.
