Ethiopia / Horn of Africa

Ethiopian Chechebsa (Kita Firfir)

Breakfast / Bread · 55 min · 4 servings

A simple pan-cooked kita becomes breakfast when it is torn warm and coated with fragrant niter kibbeh and berbere.

Fringe Table adaptation

About this dish

An Ethiopian Ministry of Labor and Skills training module describes chechebsa, or kita firfir, as shredded kita flatbread fried with spices and niter kibbeh and lists it among Ethiopia's popular breakfast dishes. Ethiopian food-system educators also document teff-based versions. This recipe uses the ministry module's accessible wheat-flatbread path while noting that grain and enrichment vary.

Story & history

Why this dish matters

Chechebsa belongs to a wider Ethiopian and Eritrean family of torn-bread breakfasts, but names and breads matter. Ethiopian sources use chechebsa for kita firfir, built from a pan-cooked flatbread rather than leftover sour injera. In some regions teff is important; in others wheat flour is common. Niter kibbeh supplies aromatic fat and berbere gives chile warmth, while yogurt or honey may soften or sweeten the plate according to household preference.

Do not collapse chechebsa into injera firfir: this version starts with freshly cooked kita. Grain, fat and serving additions vary across Ethiopian households and regions, and neighboring Eritrean kitcha fit-fit is closely related without being interchangeable in every local usage.

Before you begin

Preparation notes

Rest the dough

A 20-minute rest relaxes the wheat dough enough to roll thinly without springing back.

Cook visible brown spots

Kita should be fully cooked and flexible, with scattered light-brown spots on both sides rather than a raw floury center.

Toss while warm

Warm bread absorbs the spiced butter evenly; cold bread tends to remain greasy on the surface.

Cook it

Ingredients & detailed method

Ingredients

  • 2 cups all-purpose flour
  • 1/2 tsp kosher salt
  • 3/4 cup warm water, plus 1-2 tbsp if needed
  • 1 tbsp neutral oil, plus a thin film for the skillet
  • 4 tbsp niter kibbeh (Ethiopian spiced clarified butter)
  • 1 tbsp Ethiopian berbere, plus more to taste
  • Plain yogurt, optional for serving
  • Honey, optional for serving

Method

  1. 1.Mix flour and salt. Add warm water gradually and knead 4-5 minutes until the dough is smooth, medium-firm and no longer shaggy. Knead in 1 tablespoon oil, cover and rest 20 minutes.
  2. 2.Divide the dough in two and roll each piece into a thin round about 9 inches across. Heat a lightly oiled skillet over medium heat.
  3. 3.Cook each kita 2-3 minutes per side, pressing any bubbles gently so the surface cooks evenly.
  4. 4.Checkpoint: the flatbread should show scattered light-brown spots, feel cooked through rather than gummy at the center, and remain flexible enough to tear.
  5. 5.Tear the warm kita into irregular bite-size pieces. Melt niter kibbeh in the skillet over low heat and stir in berbere for 20-30 seconds until fragrant but not scorched.
  6. 6.Add the torn bread and fold continuously for 2-3 minutes until every piece is glossy red-orange and some edges begin to toast.
  7. 7.Checkpoint: the chechebsa should be moist from butter but not swimming in fat, with tender centers and a few lightly crisp edges.
  8. 8.Serve immediately. Offer plain yogurt or a modest drizzle of honey separately so diners can adjust heat and richness without changing the base dish.
