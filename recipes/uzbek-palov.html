Uzbekistan / Central Asia

Uzbek Palov

Rice / Main · 2 hrs · Serves 8

Lamb, carrots and cumin form a rich zirvak beneath rice that steams until each grain is tender, separate and deeply seasoned.

Fringe Table adaptation

About this dish

Palov—also called plov or osh—is one of Uzbekistan’s defining foods. Rice, meat, carrots, onions and spices are layered and cooked so the seasoned meat-and-vegetable base, called the zirvak, flavors the rice above it. The method matters as much as the ingredient list: brown first, simmer the zirvak, then steam the rice without turning the pot into a stirred pilaf.

A traditional qozon or kazan is a wide, often rounded cast-iron cauldron. A broad Dutch oven works well at home because it gives the rice room to steam in an even layer.

Story & history

A dish of hospitality, celebration and remembrance

UNESCO inscribed Uzbekistan’s palov culture and tradition on the Representative List of the Intangible Cultural Heritage of Humanity in 2016. The UNESCO description emphasizes that palov is more than a recipe: it is served to guests, prepared for weddings and New Year celebrations, shared in acts of support, and used to honor loved ones who have died. Knowledge is passed through families, community institutions and master-apprentice relationships.

National Geographic has also described the distinct cooking logic of Uzbek plov: meat is fried, carrots are boiled in the seasoned base, and rice is steamed. Regional methods differ—Samarkand-style and Tashkent-style palov, for example, can organize the layers differently—so the home method below should be understood as one practical expression of a broad tradition.

This recipe is scaled for a home pot and cannot reproduce the heat dynamics of a large qozon used by an experienced oshpaz cooking for a crowd.

Before you begin

Preparation notes

Cut carrots into sticks

Do not grate them. Thick matchsticks soften while retaining enough structure to remain distinct in the finished rice.

Rinse and soak the rice

Clear rinse water means less loose starch; soaking helps the grains cook through during the final steam.

Do not stir after layering rice

Once the rice goes over the zirvak, keep the layers separate until the final fold after resting.

Cook it

Ingredients & detailed method

Ingredients

  • 3 cups devzira rice or a sturdy medium-grain rice
  • 2 lb lamb shoulder, cut into 1 1/2-inch chunks
  • 1/2 cup sunflower or neutral oil
  • 2 large onions, thinly sliced
  • 2 lb carrots, cut into thick matchsticks
  • 2 tsp whole cumin seeds, lightly crushed
  • 1 tsp coriander seeds, lightly crushed
  • 2 whole heads garlic, loose papery skin removed
  • 1 cup cooked chickpeas, optional
  • 1/4 cup dried barberries or raisins, optional
  • About 5 cups boiling water
  • 2 1/2 tsp kosher salt, plus more to taste
  • Black pepper to taste

Method

  1. 1.Rinse and soak. Rinse rice in several changes of cool water until the water is mostly clear. Cover with warm salted water and soak 30–45 minutes.
  2. 2.Heat the pot. Heat a wide heavy Dutch oven or kazan over medium-high to high heat. Add oil and heat until shimmering. Work carefully; the pot should be hot enough to brown rather than stew.
  3. 3.Brown the lamb. Add lamb in batches and sear deeply on at least two sides, 6–8 minutes per batch. Checkpoint: browned surfaces should be dark golden, not gray.
  4. 4.Cook the onion. Return all lamb to the pot, add sliced onions and cook 6–8 minutes until they collapse and turn golden around the edges.
  5. 5.Add carrots. Spread carrot matchsticks over the meat and onions. Cook 5 minutes without stirring, then turn them gently and cook 5 minutes more. They should soften slightly but keep their shape.
  6. 6.Build the zirvak. Add cumin, coriander, salt and pepper. Nestle in the garlic heads, add chickpeas if using, and pour in enough boiling water to cover the meat by about 1 inch. Simmer 35–45 minutes. Checkpoint: the lamb should be nearly tender and the broth should taste a little stronger than finished rice needs.
  7. 7.Layer the rice. Drain soaked rice thoroughly. Spread it evenly over the zirvak. Do not stir downward into the meat and carrots.
  8. 8.Set the water level. Add boiling water gently over the back of a spoon until the rice is just barely covered. Cook uncovered over medium-high heat until visible surface water is gone, 8–12 minutes.
  9. 9.Check the grain. Taste a grain from the top. It should be swollen and partly tender but still firm at the center. If still chalky, sprinkle in a few tablespoons of hot water.
  10. 10.Steam. Mound the rice slightly toward the center and poke 5–6 holes down through the rice with a spoon handle to let steam travel. Cover tightly, reduce heat to low and steam 20–25 minutes.
  11. 11.Rest. Turn off the heat and leave covered 10 minutes. Checkpoint: rice should be tender and separate, not wet or crusted into a sticky mass.
  12. 12.Fold and serve. Remove garlic heads. Gently fold rice with carrots and meat from the bottom. Transfer to a large platter, return garlic to the top and scatter barberries or raisins if using.
Ingredient sourcing

Ingredients that make the style easier to reproduce

Devzira rice is prized for palov, though a sturdy medium-grain rice can work. Barberries are optional but useful in versions that include a tart dried-fruit accent.
