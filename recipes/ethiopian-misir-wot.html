Ethiopia / Horn of Africa

Ethiopian Misir Wot

Main · 55 min · 6 servings

A deeply savory Ethiopian red lentil stew built on patient onions, berbere and spiced fat.

Fringe Table adaptation

About this dish

Misir wot is an Ethiopian lentil stew in which red lentils cook into a thick, spoonable sauce flavored with onion, berbere and often niter kibbeh. The dish can be part of a larger injera spread and is especially useful in meatless meals because the lentils bring both body and protein.

Story & history

Why this dish matters

Documented Ethiopian versions vary in heat and fat, but the common thread is concentration: onions are cooked until sweet and dense before the lentils finish in the spiced base. Saveur attributes one widely published version to Ethiopian home cook Alemtshaye Yigezu, while Ethiopian restaurants and cooks often emphasize the importance of letting berbere bloom without scorching. This version keeps those principles while using a practical home-kitchen timeline.

Berbere blends and household methods differ considerably. Adjust heat gradually, and treat this as a practical adaptation rather than a single definitive formula.

Before you begin

Preparation notes

Rinse the lentils

Rinse until the water is mostly clear so the finished stew tastes clean rather than dusty.

Cook onions patiently

The onion base should be soft and concentrated before the lentils go in.

Watch the berbere

Add a splash of water when blooming the spice so it darkens and perfumes the pot without burning.

Cook it

Ingredients & detailed method

Ingredients

  • 2 cups red lentils
  • 3 tbsp niter kibbeh, ghee or neutral oil
  • 1 large yellow onion, very finely chopped
  • 4 garlic cloves, minced
  • 1 tbsp grated ginger
  • 2 to 3 tbsp berbere, to taste
  • 1 medium tomato, finely chopped
  • 5 cups water or light vegetable stock, plus more as needed
  • 1 tsp kosher salt, plus more to taste
  • Injera, for serving

Method

  1. 1.Rinse lentils under cool water until the runoff is mostly clear; drain well.
  2. 2.Cook onion in a heavy saucepan over medium-low heat for 12–15 minutes, stirring often. Checkpoint: it should be soft, sweet-smelling and lightly golden, not browned hard at the edges.
  3. 3.Add niter kibbeh, garlic and ginger; cook 1 minute until fragrant.
  4. 4.Stir in berbere and 2 tablespoons water. Cook 2–3 minutes. Checkpoint: the spice should smell rounded and warm, never acrid or scorched.
  5. 5.Add tomato and cook 4–5 minutes until it collapses into the onion mixture.
  6. 6.Add lentils and 4 cups water or stock. Bring to a simmer, then cook uncovered 25–30 minutes, stirring more often as the pot thickens.
  7. 7.Add extra liquid in small amounts if the lentils tighten before becoming tender. Checkpoint: the lentils should be completely soft while the stew remains thick enough to mound on a spoon.
  8. 8.Season with salt and additional berbere if needed. Rest 5 minutes before serving so the texture settles.
  9. 9.Serve with injera, ideally as part of a broader shared spread.
