Tunisia / Maghreb

Tunisian Lablabi

Main / Street Food · 40 min · Serves 4

Hot cumin-scented chickpeas and broth poured over torn bread, then customized with harissa, lemon, olive oil and toppings.

Fringe Table adaptation

About this dish

Lablabi is a Tunisian chickpea-and-bread dish in which assembly happens in the bowl. Hot chickpeas and their cumin-garlic broth soften pieces of day-old bread, and the eater brings the bowl into balance with harissa, lemon and olive oil.

Egg, tuna, capers and parsley are common additions, but the bowl is intentionally flexible. Use the North African Harissa Essential when you want to make the chile paste yourself.

Story & history

Street-food comfort assembled to taste

Lablabi is closely associated with everyday Tunisian eating and especially with cool-weather street food. Its ingredients are economical—chickpeas, bread and broth—but the final bowl becomes highly personal through the amount of harissa, lemon, oil and toppings each diner adds.

That choose-your-own finish is part of the dish's logic. The cook provides very tender chickpeas and flavorful hot broth; the table supplies heat, acidity and richness.

Toppings and broth strength vary widely by vendor and household. This version keeps the core chickpea-bread structure and lets diners season individually.

Before you begin

Preparation notes

Use stale or toasted bread

Fresh soft bread can collapse instantly. Day-old bread absorbs broth while retaining some structure.

Cook chickpeas very tender

The beans should yield easily to a spoon; lablabi is not the place for firm salad-style chickpeas.

Season at the table

Harissa heat and lemon acidity are personal. Start modestly and let each bowl be adjusted.

Make it

Ingredients & detailed method

Ingredients

  • 2 cans (15 oz each) chickpeas, drained and rinsed
  • 4 cups water or light vegetable broth
  • 4 garlic cloves, lightly crushed
  • 2 tsp ground cumin
  • 1 tsp kosher salt
  • 4 cups torn day-old crusty bread
  • 2–4 tbsp harissa
  • 1 lemon, cut into wedges
  • Extra-virgin olive oil
  • Optional toppings: soft-cooked eggs, tuna, capers, chopped parsley

Method

  1. 1.Build the broth. Combine chickpeas, water or stock, garlic, cumin and salt in a saucepan.
  2. 2.Simmer thoroughly. Cook 20–25 minutes. Checkpoint: chickpeas should be very tender and the broth should smell strongly of cumin and garlic.
  3. 3.Adjust the liquid. If the broth has reduced too far, add hot water. You need enough liquid to soak the bread generously.
  4. 4.Prepare the bowls. Tear bread into bite-size pieces and divide among deep bowls.
  5. 5.Add the chickpeas first. Spoon a generous portion of hot chickpeas over each bowl.
  6. 6.Moisten with broth. Ladle broth over the bread in stages. Checkpoint: bread should soften and slump while still leaving some discernible pieces rather than becoming soup-thick paste.
  7. 7.Add harissa. Start with about 1 teaspoon per bowl and stir it into the hot broth.
  8. 8.Balance with lemon and oil. Squeeze in lemon and drizzle with olive oil. Taste before adding more harissa.
  9. 9.Add toppings. Finish with egg, tuna, capers or parsley as desired.
  10. 10.Serve immediately. Lablabi is best while the broth is hot and the bread has just softened.
