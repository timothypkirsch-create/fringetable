Betawi Gado-Gado

Salad / Main · 50 min · 6 servings

A Jakarta plate of vegetables, tofu, tempeh and egg becomes gado-gado when everything disappears under a freshly ground peanut dressing.

Fringe Table adaptation

About this dish

Gado-gado is an Indonesian vegetable dish with strong Javanese and Betawi associations. Indonesia’s official tourism site specifically presents Gado-Gado Betawi as a Jakarta specialty combining vegetables with thick peanut sauce, ketupat, tofu and tempeh. Eggs and crackers are also common additions.

Story & history

Why this dish matters

Indonesia Travel uses gado-gado as an example of Jakarta’s diverse Betawi food culture, describing many ingredients brought together on one plate. The vegetables are generally cooked separately so each retains its own texture, while the peanut sauce binds the meal. Rather than labeling every Indonesian peanut-dressed vegetable plate identically, this recipe follows the Betawi/Jakarta framing supplied by the national tourism authority.

Gado-gado varies around Java and Indonesia. This version is explicitly Betawi/Jakarta-style and should not be treated as the only legitimate regional form.

Before you begin

Preparation notes

Cook vegetables separately

Beans, cabbage and sprouts need different times; separate blanching keeps them from all becoming equally soft.

Use fried tofu and tempeh

Their browned surfaces hold peanut sauce much better than plain steamed pieces.

Make sauce thick

Gado-gado dressing should cling heavily to the ingredients, not run like vinaigrette.

Cook it

Ingredients & detailed method

Ingredients

  • 1/2 small cabbage, cut into wedges
  • 8 oz green beans, trimmed
  • 2 cups bean sprouts
  • 2 medium potatoes, boiled and sliced
  • 8 oz firm tofu, fried or pan-browned and cubed
  • 8 oz tempeh, pan-browned and sliced
  • 3 hard-boiled eggs, quartered
  • 1 cucumber, sliced
  • 1 cup roasted peanuts
  • 2 garlic cloves
  • 2 red chiles, to taste
  • 2 tbsp palm or brown sugar
  • 1 tbsp tamarind concentrate
  • 1 tbsp sweet soy sauce (kecap manis)
  • 1/2 tsp salt
  • 3/4 to 1 cup hot water
  • Fried shallots and crackers, for serving

Method

  1. 1.Blanch cabbage, green beans and bean sprouts separately until just tender, cooling each promptly so they retain texture.
  2. 2.Arrange cooked vegetables, potatoes, tofu, tempeh, eggs and cucumber on a large platter.
  3. 3.Grind peanuts, garlic and chiles in a mortar or food processor until very fine but not completely smooth.
  4. 4.Add sugar, tamarind, kecap manis and salt. Work in hot water gradually.
  5. 5.Checkpoint: the sauce should be thick enough to coat the back of a spoon heavily and mound over vegetables rather than pour like broth.
  6. 6.Taste for sweet, sour, salty and chile balance.
  7. 7.Spoon sauce generously over the arranged ingredients and finish with fried shallots and crackers. Serve immediately.
