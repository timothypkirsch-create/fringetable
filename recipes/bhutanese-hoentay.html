Bhutanese Hoentay

Dumplings / Main · 1 hr 30 min · 20 dumplings

Earthy buckwheat dough wraps a savory greens-and-cheese filling in a dumpling strongly associated with Bhutan's Haa Valley.

Fringe Table adaptation

About this dish

Hoentay are Bhutanese dumplings associated with Haa, commonly made with buckwheat dough and a filling of turnip greens, cheese and aromatics. The dough is more fragile than wheat dumpling dough, so a small amount of wheat flour helps a home version hold together.

Story & history

Why this dish matters

Bhutanese tourism and cultural materials identify hoentay as a Haa specialty, especially connected with local buckwheat agriculture. Fillings vary, but greens and cheese are recurring elements.

This home adaptation includes wheat flour for handling; traditional formulas may use a higher proportion of buckwheat.

Ingredients & detailed method

Ingredients

  • 1 1/2 cups buckwheat flour
  • 1 cup all-purpose flour
  • 3/4 cup warm water
  • 2 cups chopped turnip greens or spinach
  • 1 cup crumbled farmer cheese
  • 2 scallions
  • 1 tbsp butter
  • 1 tsp salt

Method

  1. 1.Mix flours and water into a soft dough; rest covered 20 minutes.
  2. 2.Cook greens in butter until wilted and dry.
  3. 3.Cool, then mix with cheese, scallions and salt.
  4. 4.Roll dough thin and cut 4-inch rounds.
  5. 5.Fill, fold and seal firmly.
  6. 6.Steam 12–15 minutes.
  7. 7.Checkpoint: wrappers should be tender and filling hot through without watery leakage.

Working with buckwheat

How to shape hoentay without tearing the wrappers

Buckwheat supplies the earthy flavor and dark color that distinguish hoentay from wheat-based momos, but it contains no gluten. This home adaptation uses all-purpose flour to make the wrappers easier to roll and seal. Measure the flours by spooning them lightly into cups, then add the warm water gradually; humidity and flour brands can change how much the dough needs.

After resting, the dough should bend without splitting at the edge. If a test round cracks, knead in water one teaspoon at a time and rest it again for five minutes. If it sticks heavily, dust the work surface rather than kneading a large amount of extra flour into the dough.

Squeeze or cook excess moisture from the greens before mixing the filling. Wet filling softens the center of a wrapper and makes the seam difficult to close. Leave a clean border around each portion, press out trapped air, and pinch the edge firmly from one end to the other.

Steaming checkpoints

Line the steamer so the dumplings do not stick and leave space between them. Keep the water at a steady simmer without allowing it to touch the basket. The wrappers are ready when they look set rather than floury, feel tender at the folded edge and release from the liner without splitting. Rest them for two minutes before lifting them out.

Serving and variation

Serve hoentay hot as a dumpling course or light meal. Bhutanese household fillings vary, and local cheese and greens do not have exact supermarket equivalents. Farmer cheese offers a mild, crumbly substitute; spinach is easier to obtain than turnip greens but produces a softer, less peppery filling. These substitutions make the recipe practical while changing the result.
