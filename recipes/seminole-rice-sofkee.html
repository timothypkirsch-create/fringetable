Seminole Tribe of Florida / Indigenous Americas

Seminole Rice Sofkee

Porridge / Drink · 35 min · 6 servings

Rice cooks past the point of ordinary steamed grains into a loose, sustaining porridge-drink that Seminole sources describe as both everyday comfort and a food offered to guests.

Fringe Table adaptation

About this dish

Florida Seminole Tourism describes sofkee as a warm porridge-drink made from corn or rice and notes that the Seminole Tribe of Florida has shared a simple rice-sofkee formula using water, white rice, cornstarch and baking soda. Sofkee can be plain or flavored, but this page stays with that basic rice version so the documented community recipe remains recognizable.

Story & history

Why this dish matters

Seminole sources describe a pot of sofkee traditionally kept over the cooking-chickee fire and offered to visitors, with distinctive carved sofkee spoons used for serving. Florida Seminole Tourism also emphasizes that sofkee remains popular today and can range from plain to versions flavored with fruit, pumpkin, spices, meat or vegetables. That living flexibility is important: this is not a museum-only food, and the simple rice version below follows a recipe the Tribe has made public.

This recipe follows the public rice-sofkee method shared through Florida Seminole Tourism. Historical corn-sofkee techniques involving ash-treated corn are a different preparation and are not improvised here; do not substitute homemade lye or ash processing for the baking-soda method below.

Before you begin

Preparation notes

Use plain white rice

Long- or medium-grain white rice works well. Aromatic or seasoned rice changes the clean, neutral base of this public recipe.

Expect a drinkable porridge

Sofkee is looser than breakfast oatmeal. The finished mixture should ladle easily rather than sit in a stiff mound.

Cool before serving

The thickened liquid holds heat for a long time. Let it stand until comfortably warm before drinking or spooning.

Cook it

Ingredients & detailed method

Ingredients

  • 2 quarts (8 cups) water
  • 2 cups plain white rice, rinsed briefly and drained
  • 3 tbsp cornstarch
  • 1 tsp baking soda

Method

  1. 1.Bring 7 cups of the water to a full boil in a heavy 4-quart pot. Whisk the cornstarch into the remaining 1 cup cool water until completely smooth and set it beside the stove.
  2. 2.Add the rinsed rice to the boiling water and stir well so no grains cling to the bottom. Return to a steady boil and cook about 10 minutes, stirring every 2 to 3 minutes.
  3. 3.Whisk the cornstarch slurry once more, then pour it into the pot in a thin stream while stirring. Continue boiling about 2 minutes so the liquid loses its raw-starch cloudiness and begins to feel lightly silky.
  4. 4.Reduce the heat to low and stir in the baking soda. The pot may foam briefly; use a roomy pot and keep the heat gentle.
  5. 5.Continue cooking 12 to 18 minutes, stirring frequently and scraping the bottom. Checkpoint: the rice should be very tender with some grains beginning to break at the edges, and the surrounding liquid should be loose, opaque and lightly thickened rather than gluey.
  6. 6.If the sofkee becomes thicker than a drinkable porridge, stir in hot water 1/4 cup at a time. It should pour from a ladle in a slow stream and smell simply of cooked rice, with no raw cornstarch aroma.
  7. 7.Remove from the heat and let stand 10 to 15 minutes before serving. Check the temperature carefully; serve warm or allow to cool further, keeping this basic version plain so optional household flavorings remain optional rather than defining the dish.
