Afghanistan / Central Asia

Borani Banjan

Main / Side · 1 hr 5 min · Serves 4

Soft, deeply browned eggplant layered with tomato and cool garlic yogurt.

Fringe Table adaptation

About this dish

Borani banjan is an Afghan eggplant preparation built around contrast: hot or warm eggplant and tomato against cool garlicky yogurt. Some versions fry the eggplant generously; others use less oil or bake it. This home version pan-fries in a controlled amount of oil, then finishes the slices in tomato sauce so they become silky without being saturated.

Use the Afghan garlic yogurt Essential for the finishing layer.

Story & history

A gathering dish that travels through families

Afghan and regional versions of borani-style eggplant move through families as much as through formal recipes. In a 2025 Epicurious feature, writer Maryam Jillani describes a borani banjan learned by a friend from her Kabul-born grandmother and now prepared as a festive side for large gatherings. That kind of family transmission helps explain why proportions, frying methods and layering styles vary so widely.

The name borani is used across a broader region for dishes paired with yogurt, but borani banjan's Afghan identity comes through the combination of eggplant, tomato, garlic yogurt and herbs.

There is no single household formula. This version prioritizes clear texture cues and moderate oil use for a home kitchen.

Before you begin

Preparation notes

Salt only briefly

A 20-minute salt rest seasons the eggplant and draws off some surface moisture. Blot it very dry before frying.

Brown before braising

Color builds flavor. The slices should have golden patches before they go into the tomato sauce.

Keep yogurt cool

Do not boil the garlic yogurt into the tomato. Layer it at serving time so the temperature and flavor contrast stays intact.

Cook it

Ingredients & detailed method

Ingredients

  • 2 medium eggplants, cut into 1/2-inch rounds
  • Kosher salt
  • Neutral oil, as needed for shallow pan-frying
  • 1 medium onion, thinly sliced
  • 3 ripe tomatoes, chopped
  • 2 garlic cloves, minced
  • 1 tsp ground coriander
  • 1/2 tsp turmeric
  • 1 cup Afghan garlic yogurt
  • Dried mint, for finishing

Method

  1. 1.Salt the eggplant. Arrange slices on a tray, season lightly on both sides and rest 20 minutes.
  2. 2.Dry thoroughly. Blot each slice with towels. Checkpoint: the surface should feel dry rather than slick with beads of moisture.
  3. 3.Brown in batches. Heat a thin layer of oil over medium-high heat. Fry eggplant 2–3 minutes per side. Add small amounts of oil only as needed.
  4. 4.Watch the texture. Checkpoint: slices should be golden at the edges and beginning to soften but still sturdy enough to transfer without collapsing.
  5. 5.Cook the onion. Remove excess oil, leaving about 1 tablespoon. Cook onion 7–9 minutes until soft and lightly golden.
  6. 6.Add aromatics. Stir in garlic, coriander and turmeric for 30 seconds, just until fragrant.
  7. 7.Make the tomato sauce. Add chopped tomatoes and 1/2 cup water. Simmer 10–12 minutes, crushing the tomatoes as they soften. Checkpoint: the sauce should be thick enough to coat a spoon.
  8. 8.Braise the eggplant. Nestle the browned slices into the sauce, cover and simmer gently 10–12 minutes.
  9. 9.Check doneness. Checkpoint: a spoon should cut through the eggplant with almost no resistance, but the slices should still be recognizable.
  10. 10.Layer the dish. Spread some garlic yogurt on a platter, add eggplant and tomato sauce, then spoon more yogurt over the top. Finish with dried mint.
