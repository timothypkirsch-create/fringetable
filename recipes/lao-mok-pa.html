Laos / Southeast Asia

Lao Mok Pa

Main · 1 hr · 4 servings

Fish, herbs and aromatics steam inside banana leaf, trapping a distinctly Lao perfume of lemongrass, dill and chile.

Fringe Table adaptation

About this dish

Mok pa is a Lao steamed fish preparation in which seasoned fish is enclosed in banana leaf and gently cooked. Dill is especially characteristic, alongside lemongrass, makrut lime and other fresh aromatics.

Story & history

Why this dish matters

Mok-style banana-leaf steaming is part of Lao cooking's emphasis on fresh herbs and gentle moist heat. Fish species and herb proportions change with river, season and household.

Foil-lined parchment can substitute for banana leaf for containment, but the aroma will differ.

Before you begin

Preparation notes

Soften banana leaves

Brief heat makes them flexible.

Cut fish evenly

Pieces should finish together.

Do not oversteam

Fish should stay moist.

Cook it

Ingredients & detailed method

Ingredients

  • 1 1/2 lb firm white fish
  • 2 lemongrass stalks, minced
  • 1 cup dill
  • 1/2 cup cilantro
  • 4 scallions
  • 2 makrut lime leaves, shredded
  • 1 chile
  • 2 tbsp fish sauce
  • 1 egg
  • Banana leaves

Method

  1. 1.Warm banana leaves briefly over heat to soften.
  2. 2.Mix fish with lemongrass, dill, cilantro, scallion, lime leaf, chile, fish sauce and egg.
  3. 3.Divide mixture among leaf squares and fold into tight packets.
  4. 4.Arrange packets in a steamer.
  5. 5.Steam 20–25 minutes.
  6. 6.Checkpoint: fish should be opaque, flaky and at least 145°F in the center.
  7. 7.Open carefully and serve with sticky rice.
