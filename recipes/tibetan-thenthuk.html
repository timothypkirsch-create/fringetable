Tibet / Himalayas

Tibetan Thenthuk

Soup / Noodles · 1 hr · 4 servings

A Tibetan pull-noodle soup where the noodles are torn by hand straight into the boiling broth.

Fringe Table adaptation

About this dish

Thenthuk is a Tibetan noodle soup and one form within the broader thukpa family. Its name is commonly translated around the idea of pulled noodles: strips of rested wheat dough are stretched and torn into the simmering soup. Meat versions traditionally include mutton or yak in Tibet, while vegetarian versions are also well established, including those cooked by Tibetan Buddhist nuns in India.

Story & history

Why this dish matters

The Tibetan Nuns Project describes thenthuk as a common Tibetan comfort food, especially associated with Amdo, and publishes the vegetarian version prepared by nuns in India. Tibetan food writers Lobsang Wangdu and Yolanda O'Bannon likewise describe it as a warming soup for long Tibetan winters and explain the hand-pulled dough technique. This home recipe follows the vegetarian path so the noodle-making method remains the central lesson.

Thenthuk changes by region and household, and meat versions are deeply traditional. This vegetarian adaptation is grounded in a publicly shared Tibetan Nuns Project recipe rather than presented as the only form of the dish.

Before you begin

Preparation notes

Rest the noodle dough

Even 15–20 minutes makes the dough much easier to stretch without snapping.

Prepare everything before pulling

Once you begin tearing noodles into boiling broth, the soup moves quickly.

Make irregular pieces on purpose

Hand-pulled thenthuk noodles should feel handmade; exact identical shapes are unnecessary.

Cook it

Ingredients & detailed method

Ingredients

  • 2 cups all-purpose flour
  • 3/4 to 1 cup water for the dough
  • 1/2 tsp salt
  • 1 tbsp neutral oil, plus more for coating dough
  • 1 medium onion, chopped
  • 1 tbsp minced ginger
  • 3 garlic cloves, minced
  • 2 medium tomatoes, chopped
  • 1 medium carrot, cut into thin strips
  • 1 small daikon, cut into thin strips, optional
  • 7 cups vegetable stock or water
  • 2 tbsp soy sauce, plus more to taste
  • 2 cups spinach
  • 3 scallions, chopped
  • 1/2 cup cilantro, chopped

Method

  1. 1.Mix flour and salt. Add water gradually and knead 4–6 minutes until the dough is smooth and firm but flexible.
  2. 2.Divide into 4 pieces and roll each into a short thick rope. Coat lightly with oil, cover and rest 20 minutes.
  3. 3.Heat 1 tablespoon oil in a soup pot over medium heat. Cook onion, ginger and garlic 2–3 minutes until aromatic.
  4. 4.Add tomatoes, carrot and daikon if using and cook 3 minutes. Add stock and soy sauce and bring to a strong simmer.
  5. 5.Flatten one rested dough rope into a wide ribbon with your fingers. Stretch gently, then tear thumb-sized flat pieces directly into the simmering broth. Repeat with remaining dough.
  6. 6.Cook 3–5 minutes after the last noodle goes in. Checkpoint: noodle pieces should be tender through the center with a pleasant chew and no raw flour line.
  7. 7.Add spinach, scallions and cilantro and cook only 30–60 seconds so the greens stay bright.
  8. 8.Taste for soy sauce or salt and serve immediately while the hand-pulled noodles are springy.
