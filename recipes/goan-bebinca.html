Goa, India / Konkan Coast

Goan Bebinca

Layered Pudding Cake / Dessert · 2 hrs 15 min, plus cooling · 12 thin slices

Bebinca turns a short ingredient list into a patient showpiece: each thin pour is browned before the next is added, leaving distinct coconut-rich layers in every slice.

Fringe Table adaptation

About this dish

India's Geographical Indications Registry records Goan Bebinca as a protected product associated with Goa, registered in 2023. Goan food writing describes the dessert as a coconut-milk, egg-yolk, flour and sugar batter cooked one layer at a time, with ghee applied between layers and nutmeg providing its characteristic perfume. This recipe uses seven layers in an 8-inch pan, a manageable home scale that preserves the repeated browning technique without claiming that one layer count represents every Goan household or bakery.

Story & history

Why this dish matters

Bebinca is strongly associated with Goan Catholic celebrations, especially Christmas, weddings and other festive tables, and its ingredients and labor-intensive method reflect Goa's Indo-Portuguese culinary history. A popular origin story credits a convent nun and connects seven layers to the hills of Old Goa or Lisbon, but that account is better treated as culinary legend than settled documentary history. What can be observed directly is the dessert's craft: each layer records a separate pass under heat, so a clean slice makes the cook's time and attention visible.

Bebinca is sometimes confused online with Filipino bibingka because the names are related, but they are not the same cake. Goan bebinca is defined here by a pour-and-brown layering process, not by a single baked rice cake. Layer counts vary, and commercial examples may contain more than seven. Coconut beverage from the refrigerated carton is too thin; use full-fat canned coconut milk or freshly extracted thick coconut milk.

Before you begin

Preparation notes

Know your broiler

Broilers vary dramatically. Position the rack so the pan sits about 6 to 8 inches below the element, and stay in the kitchen throughout; later layers often brown faster as the pan heats.

Strain and re-stir

Straining removes flour lumps and egg strands. Stir the waiting batter before measuring every layer because flour settles while the cake cooks.

Cool completely

Warm bebinca is too soft to show clean layers. Cool it fully in the pan, then chill several hours or overnight before unmolding and slicing with a lightly greased knife.

Cook it

Ingredients & detailed method

Ingredients

  • 9 large egg yolks
  • 1 1/4 cups granulated sugar
  • 2 cups full-fat unsweetened coconut milk, well stirred
  • 1 cup all-purpose flour, spooned and leveled
  • 1/4 tsp kosher salt
  • 1/2 tsp freshly grated nutmeg
  • 1/2 cup ghee, melted, plus more for greasing the pan

Method

  1. 1.Grease an 8-inch round metal cake pan generously with ghee, including the sides. Heat the oven to 350°F with a rack in the lower-middle position.
  2. 2.Whisk the egg yolks and sugar for 2 to 3 minutes, until the mixture is smooth and slightly lighter. Whisk in coconut milk, then sift in flour, salt and nutmeg and whisk only until no dry pockets remain.
  3. 3.Pass the batter through a fine-mesh sieve into a large measuring jug. Checkpoint: it should pour like light cream and show no flour beads or egg strands. You should have roughly 4 cups.
  4. 4.Pour 1 tablespoon melted ghee into the prepared pan, followed by a scant 1/2 cup batter. Tilt the pan to make an even layer. Bake 10 to 12 minutes, until the first layer is set through and lightly golden at the edges.
  5. 5.Switch the oven to high broil. Keep the pan 6 to 8 inches from the element. Broil the first layer 1 to 3 minutes more, watching continuously, until its surface is evenly amber rather than pale.
  6. 6.Remove the pan to a heatproof surface. Brush the browned layer with about 2 teaspoons ghee. Stir the batter, pour on another scant 1/2 cup and tilt gently without scraping the layer beneath.
  7. 7.Broil 3 to 6 minutes, rotating the pan if needed. Checkpoint: the new layer should no longer ripple when the pan is nudged and should be mottled deep gold to amber across the surface, with no wet batter at the center.
  8. 8.Repeat the ghee, batter and broiling sequence until seven layers are complete. Begin checking after 2 minutes on later layers because the hot pan can shorten the cooking time. If any spot blackens, lower the rack before continuing.
  9. 9.After the final layer is browned, switch off the broiler, set the oven to 325°F and bake 10 minutes. A thin skewer inserted through the center should emerge without liquid batter, though a little moist crumb or ghee is expected.
  10. 10.Cool in the pan on a rack for at least 2 hours. Run a thin knife around the edge, chill 4 hours or overnight, then briefly warm the base of the pan over hot water and invert onto a plate.
  11. 11.Bring to cool room temperature for 20 to 30 minutes before serving. Slice thinly with a lightly greased knife, wiping the blade between cuts so the alternating layers remain distinct.
