Haiti / Caribbean

Haitian Mayi Moulen

Side / Staple · 35 min · 4 servings

A humble Haitian staple that turns coarse cornmeal into a creamy, savory base for beans, fish, greens and avocado.

Fringe Table adaptation

About this dish

Mayi moulen means ground corn in Haitian Creole. The dish is a savory cornmeal preparation that can appear at breakfast or later meals and is commonly served with black bean sauce, salted herring, avocado or vegetable preparations. Its strength is versatility: the cornmeal provides a filling base while the accompanying sauce or side supplies much of the meal’s contrast.

Story & history

Why this dish matters

Haitian cooks describe mayi moulen as a staple comparable in everyday importance to rice. Published Haitian family recipes emphasize coarse cornmeal, garlic and a creamy finished texture, while Bon Appétit’s Lesley Enston pairs it with sos pwa nwa and discusses the meal as deeply comforting Haitian food. The formula is simple, but getting the texture smooth rather than gritty takes patient cooking.

Cornmeal grind, seasonings and accompaniments vary by household. This is a plain savory foundation designed to be served with other Haitian dishes.

Before you begin

Preparation notes

Choose coarse cornmeal

A medium-to-coarse grind gives more character than instant cornmeal while still becoming creamy.

Whisk while adding

Sprinkle cornmeal gradually into boiling liquid to prevent hard lumps.

Finish by texture

If the spoon leaves a stiff trench, add hot water; mayi moulen should be creamy and spoonable.

Cook it

Ingredients & detailed method

Ingredients

  • 1 cup coarse yellow cornmeal
  • 4 cups water, plus more as needed
  • 1 tbsp olive or neutral oil
  • 2 garlic cloves, minced
  • 1/2 tsp dried thyme or 2 fresh thyme sprigs
  • 1 tsp kosher salt, plus more to taste
  • 2 tbsp chopped parsley
  • 1 tbsp butter or additional oil, optional

Method

  1. 1.Heat oil in a heavy saucepan over medium-low heat. Add garlic and cook about 30 seconds until fragrant without browning.
  2. 2.Add water, thyme and salt and bring to a full boil.
  3. 3.Whisk constantly while slowly raining in the cornmeal. Continue whisking 2 minutes until no dry lumps remain.
  4. 4.Reduce heat to low, cover partially and cook 20–25 minutes, stirring vigorously every 4–5 minutes and scraping the bottom.
  5. 5.Checkpoint: the grains should taste tender rather than sandy, and the porridge should fall slowly from a spoon instead of standing in a stiff mass.
  6. 6.Add hot water a few tablespoons at a time if it becomes too thick before the cornmeal is fully tender.
  7. 7.Stir in parsley and butter or extra oil if using. Taste for salt.
  8. 8.Serve hot with sos pwa nwa, avocado, stewed greens, fish or another Haitian accompaniment.
