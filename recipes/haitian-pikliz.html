Haiti / Caribbean

Haitian Pikliz

Condiment · 24 hrs · 1 quart

Sharp, crunchy and unapologetically hot—the condiment that makes rich Haitian fried foods snap into focus.

Fringe Table adaptation

About this dish

Pikliz is a Haitian pickled vegetable condiment most often built from shredded cabbage, carrots, onion and hot chiles in vinegar. Its job at the table is practical as much as flavorful: acidity, heat and crunch cut through rich fried foods such as griot and plantains.

Story & history

Why this dish matters

The Smithsonian National Museum of American History included pikliz in its Cooking Up History program on Haitian Carnival food traditions with Haitian chef Jouvens Jean and curator Joanne Hyppolite. That context underscores how pikliz belongs alongside other foods rather than functioning as an isolated pickle. Household versions vary in vegetable mix, chile level, spices and resting time.

This is a refrigerator pickle, not a shelf-stable canning formula. Keep it refrigerated and use clean utensils when serving.

Before you begin

Preparation notes

Wear gloves for Scotch bonnet

Capsaicin transfers easily from hands to eyes and skin.

Slice vegetables thinly

Thin shreds soften just enough while keeping the crunch that defines good pikliz.

Give it time

Twenty minutes produces a quick pickle, but a full day gives a more integrated vinegar-and-chile flavor.

Cook it

Ingredients & detailed method

Ingredients

  • 4 cups finely shredded green cabbage
  • 1 large carrot, julienned or coarsely grated
  • 1 small red onion, very thinly sliced
  • 1/2 red bell pepper, thinly sliced
  • 1/2 green bell pepper, thinly sliced
  • 2 Scotch bonnet or habanero peppers, thinly sliced, seeds included for more heat
  • 4 garlic cloves, thinly sliced
  • 1 tsp kosher salt
  • 8 black peppercorns
  • 4 whole cloves
  • 2 cups distilled white vinegar, plus more as needed to cover
  • 2 tbsp fresh lime juice

Method

  1. 1.Combine cabbage, carrot, onion, bell peppers, hot peppers and garlic in a large nonreactive bowl.
  2. 2.Add salt and toss firmly for about 1 minute. Checkpoint: vegetables should begin to glisten but remain crisp.
  3. 3.Pack vegetables tightly into a clean 1-quart jar, adding peppercorns and cloves as you go.
  4. 4.Mix vinegar and lime juice, then pour over the vegetables until fully submerged. Add a little more vinegar if needed.
  5. 5.Press vegetables below the brine, close the jar and refrigerate.
  6. 6.Checkpoint after 24 hours: cabbage should still crunch, but the vinegar should taste infused with chile, onion and spice rather than raw.
  7. 7.Serve cold with griot, fried plantains, tassot or other rich Haitian dishes. Keep refrigerated.
