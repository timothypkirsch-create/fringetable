Wampanoag / Indigenous Americas

Wampanoag Nasaump

Breakfast / Porridge · 40 min · 4 servings

Cornmeal becomes a warm, sustaining porridge with little more than water, time and a modest finishing sweetness.

Fringe Table adaptation

About this dish

Nasaump is documented in Wampanoag food-history materials as a cornmeal porridge that could be eaten hot or cold. Modern educational recipes often include berries, nuts or maple, but the core preparation remains corn simmered into a thick spoonable cereal.

Story & history

Why this dish matters

Plimoth Patuxet Museums and Wampanoag educators document corn as foundational to local foodways and nasaump as one way ground corn was prepared. This home version keeps the method simple and avoids presenting optional modern additions as a fixed historical formula.

This is a home adaptation based on public Wampanoag educational sources; ingredients and sweetness vary in modern versions.

Before you begin

Preparation notes

Use coarse cornmeal

It gives more texture than fine flour.

Whisk gradually

Adding meal slowly prevents lumps.

Expect thickening

Nasaump continues to set as it rests.

Cook it

Ingredients & detailed method

Ingredients

  • 1 cup coarse cornmeal
  • 4 cups water
  • 1/2 tsp salt
  • 2 tbsp maple syrup, optional
  • 1/2 cup berries, optional

Method

  1. 1.Bring water and salt to a simmer.
  2. 2.Whisk in cornmeal in a thin stream.
  3. 3.Lower heat and cook 20–25 minutes, stirring often.
  4. 4.Checkpoint: grains should be fully tender with no gritty raw center.
  5. 5.Add water if needed to keep the porridge spoonable.
  6. 6.Stir in maple syrup only if desired.
  7. 7.Rest 5 minutes and serve with berries or plain.
