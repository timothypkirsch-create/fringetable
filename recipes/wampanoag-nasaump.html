Wampanoag / Indigenous Americas

Wampanoag Nasaump

Breakfast / Porridge · 40 min · 4 servings

Coarse corn, berries and crushed nuts simmer into a warm, sustaining porridge documented in public Wampanoag food-history materials.

Fringe Table adaptation

About this dish

Nasaump is a Wampanoag corn porridge. Plimoth Patuxet Museums describes a preparation of dried corn or cornmeal cooked in water with local berries and crushed nuts until thick, with maple syrup or sugar offered as an optional modern finishing choice.

This adaptation follows that public museum recipe closely while expanding the technique for a modern saucepan. Coarse grits give a more distinct grain; cornmeal cooks faster and produces a smoother bowl.

Story & history

Why this dish matters

Corn is foundational to Wampanoag foodways, but nasaump should not be reduced to a generic colonial-era cereal. The name and preparation belong to a living Indigenous community whose food knowledge predates English settlement.

The museum distinguishes nasaump from samp, an English adaptation of the word and dish recorded in seventeenth-century writing. Keeping those labels separate matters: an English documentary record is not the origin of Wampanoag corn cookery.

This home recipe relies on public educational material from Plimoth Patuxet Museums. It does not claim to represent private family knowledge or every contemporary Wampanoag preparation.

Before you begin

Preparation notes

Choose the grain

Coarse grits need more time; ordinary cornmeal usually thickens within 15–20 minutes.

Crush, do not powder

Coarsely crushed nuts or seeds add body while preserving some texture.

Follow the grain

The porridge is done when the corn is tender, not merely when the liquid looks thick.

Cook it

Ingredients & detailed method

Ingredients

  • 1 1/2 cups coarse corn grits or cornmeal
  • 1 cup fresh or frozen strawberries, raspberries, blueberries, or a mixture
  • 1/2 cup crushed walnuts, hazelnuts, sunflower seeds, or a mixture
  • 4 cups water
  • Maple syrup, to taste (optional)

Method

  1. 1.If using large berries, cut them into bite-size pieces. Crush the nuts or seeds to coarse crumbs rather than a smooth flour.
  2. 2.Whisk the cornmeal with 1 cup of the cool water in a heavy saucepan until no dry pockets remain. This cold slurry helps prevent lumps.
  3. 3.Whisk in the remaining 3 cups water, then stir in the berries and crushed nuts or seeds.
  4. 4.Set the pan over medium heat and stir frequently as it approaches a boil, scraping the corners and bottom where cornmeal settles.
  5. 5.As soon as the mixture bubbles, lower the heat to maintain a gentle simmer. Partially cover to contain spatters while leaving room for steam to escape.
  6. 6.Cook 15–20 minutes for cornmeal, or longer as the package directs for coarse grits, stirring every 1–2 minutes.
  7. 7.Checkpoint: the grain should be fully tender with no hard or chalky center. The porridge should fall slowly from a spoon; add hot water in small amounts if it becomes stiff before the grain is tender.
  8. 8.Fold in maple syrup only if desired. Taste the berries first—the museum recipe presents sweetener as optional, not required.
  9. 9.Remove from the heat and rest for 5 minutes. Stir once and loosen with hot water if needed, because the porridge continues to thicken as it stands.
  10. 10.Serve warm. Cool leftovers promptly; reheat gently with additional water until the original spoonable texture returns.
