Sierra Leone / West Africa

Sierra Leonean Cassava Leaf Stew

Leaf Stew / Main · 2 hrs 30 min · 8 servings

This deep-green Sierra Leonean stew builds its body from finely ground cassava leaves and groundnut paste, with smoked fish, beef and fermented seasoning rounding the pot.

Fringe Table adaptation

About this dish

Sierra Leone's National Tourist Board and Ministry of Tourism regularly feature cassava leaves in official showcases of the country's gastronomy. Sierra Leonean cook Nina's Cookery Corner describes the dish as a nationally loved sauce for rice and documents a home method using ground cassava leaves, palm oil, groundnut paste, meat, smoked fish, onion, chile and ogiri. This version stays within that framework while specifying commercially prepared frozen ground leaves for a safer and more reproducible diaspora-kitchen route.

Story & history

Why this dish matters

Cassava leaf stew belongs to the broader Sierra Leonean family of leaf stews often described as plasas or palaver sauces, but the leaf and method are not generic. Cassava leaves are pounded very fine and cooked longer than the more visibly leafy potato-leaf or green-leaf stews. Families and communities differ over proteins, thickening and seasoning; debates about whose cassava leaves are best are part of the dish's living social context. Rice is the expected partner, absorbing the concentrated, smoky sauce rather than functioning as an optional garnish.

Cassava leaves naturally contain cyanogenic compounds and must never be eaten raw or casually substituted with unprocessed leaves. This recipe intentionally calls only for a labeled food product of commercially prepared, finely ground frozen cassava leaves; follow any additional package directions and do not use ornamental or foraged cassava. Groundnut paste, smoked fish and ogiri vary by household. Omitting ogiri reduces the fermented depth, while replacing red palm oil changes the dish's characteristic flavor and color.

Before you begin

Preparation notes

Buy the right leaf product

Look for frozen, finely ground cassava leaves sold for cooking at a West African market. Keep them frozen until needed, thaw in the refrigerator and never taste them before their full cook.

Debone smoked fish carefully

Soak the fish briefly, then use your fingers to separate it into flakes and feel for fine bones. Add it late enough to retain some pieces while still seasoning the sauce.

Use a heavy pot

The ground leaves and peanut paste settle readily. A heavy-bottomed 6- to 8-quart pot and regular scraping along the base reduce scorching during the long simmer.

Cook it

Ingredients & detailed method

Ingredients

  • 2 lb commercially prepared frozen ground cassava leaves, thawed in the refrigerator
  • 1 1/2 lb boneless beef chuck, cut into 1-inch cubes
  • 8 oz hot-smoked mackerel or other firm smoked fish, skin and bones removed
  • 2 large yellow onions, divided
  • 1 to 2 Scotch bonnet peppers, stemmed; seed one for less heat
  • 1 tbsp ogiri or other food-grade West African fermented seasoning
  • 4 cups water, plus more as needed
  • 1/2 cup smooth unsweetened natural peanut butter or finely ground groundnut paste
  • 3/4 cup unrefined red palm oil
  • 2 tbsp ground dried shrimp or crayfish, optional
  • 1 cup thinly sliced okra, optional
  • 2 low-sodium bouillon cubes, optional
  • 1 1/2 tsp kosher salt, divided, plus more to taste
  • Steamed long-grain or Sierra Leonean country rice, for serving

Method

  1. 1.Cut one onion into wedges and finely chop the other. Blend the onion wedges, Scotch bonnet and ogiri with 1/2 cup water into a thick paste. Avoid breathing directly over the blender when opening it because aerosolized chile can sting.
  2. 2.Put beef, half the chopped onion, 1 teaspoon salt, optional bouillon and 3 cups water in a heavy pot. Bring to a boil, skim any foam, then cover partially and simmer 40 to 55 minutes, until the beef is nearly tender. Add water as needed to keep about 2 cups broth in the pot.
  3. 3.Soak the smoked fish in hot water for 10 minutes, drain and flake it, feeling carefully for bones. Whisk the peanut butter with 1 cup warm beef broth until smooth and pourable.
  4. 4.Add the thawed ground cassava leaves and blended onion-chile mixture to the beef and remaining broth. Stir thoroughly, bring back to an active simmer, then lower the heat and cook partially covered for 45 minutes, scraping the bottom every 8 to 10 minutes.
  5. 5.Checkpoint: the leaves should lose their bright raw-green color and grassy smell, becoming darker, softer and more cohesive. Keep the pot at a genuine simmer and add hot water by the 1/2 cup if it becomes too dry to bubble.
  6. 6.Stir in the peanut mixture, red palm oil, flaked smoked fish and optional dried shrimp. Simmer uncovered 25 minutes, stirring frequently and pressing any remaining clumps of leaf against the side of the pot.
  7. 7.Add the remaining chopped onion and optional okra. Continue simmering 15 to 20 minutes, until the onion has softened, the okra is tender and no raw edge remains in the greens.
  8. 8.Checkpoint: the finished stew should be very thick and dark green, with red oil beginning to glisten at the edges and on the surface. It should smell smoky, earthy and rounded rather than sharply vegetal, and the beef should yield easily to a fork.
  9. 9.Taste only after the full cooking time, then add the remaining 1/2 teaspoon salt if needed. If the sauce is loose, simmer uncovered a little longer; if it is pasty and catches immediately, stir in a small amount of hot water.
  10. 10.Serve hot over plenty of steamed rice. Cool leftovers quickly in shallow containers and refrigerate within 2 hours; reheat to a full simmer, stirring often, before serving.
