Egyptian Molokhia (Jute Leaf Soup with Tasha)

Finely Chopped Jute Leaf Soup / Main · 1 hr 40 min · 6 servings

Molokhia rewards restraint: finely chopped leaves need only gentle heat before a pale-gold garlic and coriander tasha perfumes the silky green broth.

Fringe Table adaptation

About this dish

Molokhia is both the Arabic name for Corchorus olitorius leaves and a family of dishes made from them. In this familiar Egyptian form, the leaves are chopped extremely fine, loosened with meat or poultry broth and finished with tasha, a hot mixture of garlic and ground coriander. The leaves naturally give the soup a light, cohesive viscosity; the goal is a pourable broth that coats rice, not a purée or a pot boiled until dull and stringy.

Story & history

Why this dish matters

Nawaya's Egyptian foodways documentation shows molokhia as a spring and summer crop with an unusually broad life in the kitchen: fresh leaves chopped with a two-handled makhrata, dried leaves made into shalawlaw, and soups based on chicken, duck, rabbit, meat, fava-bean or coastal shrimp broth. Across those forms, the garlic-coriander tasha is a recurring aromatic signature. This page follows a practical chicken-broth version using frozen minced leaves, the form most accessible to cooks outside Egypt, while retaining the gentle heating and last-minute tasha that define the bowl.

Egyptian households differ over broth, garlic, coriander, tomato, lemon and whether molokhia should ever visibly simmer after the leaves enter. Frozen finely minced Egyptian molokhia is a sound substitute for freshly picked and hand-chopped leaves, but whole dried leaves produce a different dish. The soup's natural mucilaginous texture is characteristic rather than a fault. A vegetable or sprouted-fava broth can make a fasting version, but it will not taste identical to this chicken-based preparation.

Before you begin

Preparation notes

Buy finely minced leaves

Look for a 14-ounce package labeled Egyptian molokhia or minced jute mallow. Whole-leaf Lebanese-style molokhia cooks and eats differently.

Season the broth first

The leaves cook too briefly to repair a weak base. Taste the strained chicken broth before adding molokhia; it should be savory but not aggressively salty.

Keep the green pot below a boil

After the leaves dissolve, use low heat and look for steam with only an occasional bubble at the edge. Hard boiling dulls the color and coarsens the texture.

Watch the tasha continuously

Garlic should be pale gold and fragrant when coriander is added. Dark brown garlic makes the entire soup bitter.

Cook it

Ingredients & detailed method

Ingredients

  • 3 lb bone-in chicken thighs and drumsticks
  • 8 cups cool water
  • 1 medium yellow onion, quartered
  • 4 green cardamom pods, lightly crushed
  • 1 small cinnamon stick
  • 1 bay leaf
  • 2 1/2 tsp kosher salt, divided, plus more to taste
  • 1/2 tsp black pepper
  • 1 package (14 oz / 400 g) frozen finely minced Egyptian molokhia
  • 2 tbsp ghee or unsalted butter
  • 6 large garlic cloves, finely minced or pounded
  • 2 tsp ground coriander
  • Cooked Egyptian vermicelli rice, for serving
  • Lemon wedges, for serving, optional

Method

  1. 1.Put the chicken, water, onion, cardamom, cinnamon, bay leaf, 2 teaspoons salt and black pepper in a 6-quart pot. Bring slowly to a bare simmer over medium heat, skimming the gray foam that rises during the first 10 minutes.
  2. 2.Partially cover and poach at a gentle simmer for 35 to 50 minutes, turning the pieces once. Checkpoint: the thickest chicken piece must reach 165°F / 74°C and the joint should move easily, while the broth remains mostly clear rather than violently clouded.
  3. 3.Lift the chicken onto a rack. Strain the broth through a fine sieve, discard the spent aromatics and measure 5 cups back into a clean pot. Reserve extra broth for adjusting the soup or cooking the rice.
  4. 4.For browned chicken like the pictured service, pat the cooked pieces dry, brush lightly with ghee and broil 6 inches from the heat for 3 to 6 minutes, watching continuously, until the skin has crisp golden patches. Keep warm.
  5. 5.Bring the 5 cups strained broth just to a simmer, then lower the heat. Add the frozen molokhia in chunks and stir gently for 5 to 8 minutes as it thaws and disperses completely.
  6. 6.Checkpoint: the soup should be vivid dark green, pour in a continuous silky stream from a spoon and show steam with only small bubbles at the rim. If thick enough to mound, loosen it with reserved hot broth 1/4 cup at a time.
  7. 7.Melt the ghee in a small skillet over medium heat. Add the garlic and remaining 1/2 teaspoon salt; cook 30 to 60 seconds, stirring constantly, until the garlic is fragrant and pale gold but not brown.
  8. 8.Stir the ground coriander into the garlic for 10 to 15 seconds. As soon as it smells toasted, pour the sizzling tasha into the molokhia. Swirl a ladleful of soup through the skillet to capture the garlic and return it to the pot.
  9. 9.Stir once, warm for 1 minute without boiling and remove from the heat. Taste only after the tasha is incorporated; adjust salt and thin with a little broth if the soup tightened while standing.
  10. 10.Serve immediately in bowls with vermicelli rice and the browned chicken alongside. Offer lemon separately because its brightness is a household preference rather than a required finish.
  11. 11.Cool leftovers in shallow containers within 2 hours and refrigerate for up to 3 days. Reheat gently, stirring often and stopping before a rolling boil.
