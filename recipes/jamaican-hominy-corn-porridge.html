Jamaican Hominy Corn Porridge

Breakfast / Porridge · 9 hrs 15 min · 6 servings

Whole hominy gives this Jamaican breakfast porridge a chewy, creamy texture that is very different from smooth cornmeal porridge, while coconut milk, cinnamon, nutmeg and vanilla make the bowl unmistakably comforting.

Fringe Table adaptation

About this dish

Hominy corn porridge is one of Jamaica's familiar breakfast porridges, alongside cornmeal, peanut, green banana and plantain versions. Dried hominy is soaked and simmered until each kernel is tender, then coconut milk and spices turn the cooking liquid creamy. Sweetened condensed milk is a common modern finishing sweetener; the amount is best adjusted at the end because the porridge concentrates as it cooks.

Story & history

Why this dish matters

Jamaican breakfast culture gives porridges an important everyday role because they are filling, economical and adaptable. Hominy corn porridge preserves the distinct bite of whole processed maize rather than grinding it into meal. Jamaican recipe traditions differ in whether they use cow's milk, coconut milk or both, and in the balance of condensed milk, brown sugar, cinnamon, nutmeg and vanilla. This version uses coconut milk prominently and treats sweetness as adjustable rather than fixed.

This is specifically the whole-hominy porridge, not Jamaican cornmeal porridge. Canned hominy can make a quicker cereal but lacks the long-cooked flavor and chewy tenderness of soaked dried hominy, so the two methods should not be treated as identical.

Before you begin

Preparation notes

Soak overnight

Dried hominy softens much more evenly after an 8-hour soak and is less likely to stay hard at the center while the outside breaks down.

Sweeten last

Condensed milk can scorch and the porridge becomes sweeter as liquid reduces. Add it only after the corn is fully tender.

Keep coconut milk below a hard boil

Once coconut milk is added, use a gentle simmer and stir often so the bottom stays creamy rather than catching and tasting toasted or burnt.

Cook it

Ingredients & detailed method

Ingredients

  • 2 cups dried white hominy corn, rinsed and soaked overnight
  • 7 cups water, plus more as needed
  • 1 cinnamon stick
  • 1/2 tsp fine salt
  • 1 1/2 cups full-fat unsweetened coconut milk
  • 1/2 tsp freshly grated nutmeg
  • 1 tsp pure vanilla extract
  • 1/3 to 1/2 cup sweetened condensed milk, to taste
  • 2 tbsp brown sugar, optional only if additional sweetness is wanted

Method

  1. 1.Drain the soaked hominy and combine it with 7 cups fresh water, the cinnamon stick and salt in a heavy pot. Bring to a boil, then lower to a steady simmer.
  2. 2.Cook 45 to 75 minutes, stirring every 10 minutes and adding hot water if the kernels become exposed before they are tender.
  3. 3.Checkpoint: bite a kernel through its thickest part. It should be plump and tender with a gentle chew but no chalky or hard center. The cooking liquid will look cloudy and slightly starchy.
  4. 4.Stir in the coconut milk and nutmeg. Return only to a gentle simmer and cook 12 to 15 minutes, stirring frequently along the bottom and corners of the pot.
  5. 5.Checkpoint: the porridge should smell of coconut and warm spice, and the liquid should coat a spoon while the kernels remain distinct. If the coconut milk looks oily or separated, lower the heat and stir in a small splash of hot water.
  6. 6.Remove the cinnamon stick. Stir in vanilla and 1/3 cup condensed milk. Taste before adding more condensed milk or the optional brown sugar.
  7. 7.Simmer 2 to 3 minutes more. The finished porridge should be creamy and spoonable, not stiff; loosen with hot water or a little coconut milk if needed because it thickens as it stands.
  8. 8.Rest 5 minutes and serve warm. Stir each bowl before eating so the hominy stays evenly distributed through the creamy liquid.
