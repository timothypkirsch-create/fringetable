Jamaican Beef Patties (Scotch Bonnet-Spiced Pastry)

Spiced Beef-Filled Pastry / Snack or Light Meal · 3 hrs, including chilling and cooling · 10 large patties

A good Jamaican patty is built on contrast: a brittle, unmistakably yellow crust around beef that stays finely textured, juicy and aromatic without leaking through the seam.

Fringe Table adaptation

About this dish

The Jamaican patty is a crescent-shaped pastry sold by bakeries, shops and street vendors across the island and carried widely through Jamaican migration. Beef is the best-known filling, though chicken, vegetable, seafood and other versions are established parts of the category. Its shell is thin, yellow from turmeric or Jamaican curry powder, and flaky from firm fat; the beef inside is not a dry crumble but a cohesive, spoonable mixture scented with scallion, thyme, allspice and Scotch bonnet. In Jamaica, a patty may be eaten alone or tucked into soft coco bread for a more substantial meal.

Story & history

Why this dish matters

Jamaica's official tourism authority describes the patty as an island lunchtime favorite derived from the Cornish pasty introduced under British rule and transformed through Jamaican seasoning and technique. That concise history explains the form without treating the modern patty as merely imported: Scotch bonnet, thyme, scallion, allspice and the yellow crust give it a distinctly Jamaican identity. Jamaican cookbook authors Michelle and Suzanne Rousseau emphasize two present-day standards—the shell should shower flakes, while the beef should remain loose and gently stew-like rather than dense. Commercial patty shops made the portable pastry part of everyday island life, and diaspora bakeries carried it into cities far beyond Jamaica.

There is no single mandatory patty formula. Jamaican bakeries guard distinct doughs, spice blends, crimps and filling textures, and many use beef suet for a particularly crisp crust. This home version combines butter and shortening for reliable handling; replacing both with suet changes flavor and texture but is also documented. Scotch bonnets vary sharply in heat, so wear gloves, avoid touching your face and begin with half a seeded chile if needed. Cool the filling completely before assembly, keep the dough cold, and refrigerate cooked patties within 2 hours.

Before you begin

Preparation notes

Keep the fat cold

Visible pieces of cold butter and shortening create the flaky shell. If the dough becomes glossy or soft while rolling, chill it for 15 minutes before continuing.

Reduce, then cool, the filling

The beef should be moist enough to spread but leave no liquid trail when a spoon crosses the skillet. Warm filling melts the pastry fat and makes weak, leaking seams.

Season a test spoonful

Cool a teaspoon of filling before judging salt and chile. The flavor should be slightly more assertive than a plated stew because the pastry will soften its impact.

Cook it

Ingredients & detailed method

Ingredients

  • 3 cups all-purpose flour, plus more for rolling
  • 1 tbsp Jamaican curry powder
  • 1 tsp ground turmeric
  • 1 tsp baking powder
  • 1 tsp kosher salt
  • 1/2 cup cold unsalted butter, cut into 1/2-inch cubes
  • 1/2 cup cold vegetable shortening, cut into small pieces
  • 2/3 cup ice water, plus up to 2 tbsp as needed
  • 1 large egg beaten with 1 tbsp water, for egg wash
  • 1 tbsp neutral oil
  • 1 1/4 lb ground beef, preferably 85 percent lean
  • 1 small yellow onion, very finely chopped
  • 3 scallions, white and green parts finely chopped
  • 3 garlic cloves, minced
  • 1/2 to 1 Scotch bonnet chile, seeded and minced with gloves
  • 2 tsp fresh thyme leaves
  • 1 1/2 tsp ground allspice
  • 1 tsp freshly ground black pepper
  • 1 tsp Jamaican curry powder
  • 1 tsp kosher salt, plus more to taste
  • 1 cup low-sodium beef stock
  • 1 tsp browning sauce, optional
  • 3/4 cup fine dry breadcrumbs

Method

  1. 1.Whisk the flour, 1 tablespoon curry powder, turmeric, baking powder and 1 teaspoon salt in a wide bowl. Add the cold butter and shortening and cut them in with a pastry blender or fingertips until most pieces are pea-size and a few remain slightly larger.
  2. 2.Drizzle in 2/3 cup ice water while tossing with a fork. Squeeze a handful: it should hold together without feeling wet. Add more ice water 1 tablespoon at a time only if dry flour remains, then press the shaggy dough into a disk without kneading it smooth.
  3. 3.Wrap the dough and refrigerate at least 1 hour. Checkpoint: before rolling, it should feel firm and cool but yield slightly when pressed; a rock-hard disk will crack, while a soft one will lose its flakes.
  4. 4.Heat the neutral oil in a wide skillet over medium-high. Add the beef and cook 5 to 7 minutes, breaking it into very fine pieces, until no pink remains and the released liquid has mostly evaporated. Drain only if more than 2 tablespoons of fat pool in the pan.
  5. 5.Add the onion, scallions, garlic, Scotch bonnet and thyme. Cook over medium heat 6 to 8 minutes, stirring, until the onion is soft and the sharp raw-chile aroma has mellowed. Stir in the allspice, black pepper, remaining curry powder and 1 teaspoon salt for 30 seconds.
  6. 6.Pour in the stock and optional browning sauce. Simmer uncovered 12 to 18 minutes, stirring and crushing the beef, until the mixture is moist but no broth pools when dragged aside. Stir in the breadcrumbs and cook 2 minutes; the filling should spread like a thick paste while retaining fine pieces of beef.
  7. 7.Transfer the filling to a shallow pan and cool uncovered for 20 minutes, then refrigerate until cold, at least 30 minutes. Taste a cooled spoonful and adjust salt or chile before assembling.
  8. 8.Line two baking sheets with parchment. Divide the dough into 10 equal pieces, keeping all but one refrigerated. On a lightly floured surface, roll each piece into a 6 1/2-inch round about 1/8 inch thick; trim around a bowl if a neater edge is desired.
  9. 9.Place a scant 1/3 cup cold filling just off-center on each round, leaving a 1/2-inch clean border. Brush the border lightly with cold water, fold into a half-moon and press out trapped air before sealing. Crimp firmly with a fork without piercing the filled center.
  10. 10.Arrange the patties 1 inch apart on the sheets and chill 20 minutes. Meanwhile heat the oven to 375°F with racks in the upper and lower thirds. Checkpoint: the patties should feel cold and the edges should remain tightly sealed before baking.
  11. 11.Brush lightly with egg wash and cut one small steam slit in each top. Bake 28 to 35 minutes, rotating and switching the sheets halfway, until the crust is dry, crisp and deep golden at the edges and the centers register at least 165°F.
  12. 12.Cool 8 to 10 minutes before eating; the filling will be dangerously hot straight from the oven. Serve on their own or inside coco bread. Refrigerate leftovers up to 3 days and reheat at 350°F until the crust is crisp and the center is steaming.
