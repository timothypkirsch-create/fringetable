Newar communities / Nepal

Newari Yomari

Dessert · 1 hr 10 min · Makes 12

Tapered steamed rice-flour dumplings with a molten filling of chaku or jaggery and toasted sesame.

Fringe Table adaptation

About this dish

Yomari are closely associated with the Newar communities of the Kathmandu Valley. A warm rice-flour shell encloses a sweet filling commonly made with chaku and sesame, though other fillings exist. The pointed, elongated shape is distinctive, but a well-sealed dumpling matters more for a first-time home cook than sculptural perfection.

If chaku is difficult to source, dark jaggery creates a useful home approximation, while the site's ingredient sourcing can point readers toward the traditional ingredient.

Story & history

Yomari Punhi and the new rice harvest

The Nepal Tourism Board describes Yomari Punhi as an auspicious Newar festival associated with Annapurna, food, crops and prayers for a good rice harvest. Yomari are made from new-harvest rice flour and filled with chaku or khuwa, linking the food directly to agricultural season and celebration.

The tourism board also records a popular origin story from Panauti in which a Newar couple combined new rice flour with molasses and shared the resulting sweet with their community. Like many food-origin stories, it is best understood as cultural narrative rather than a provable first invention.

Shapes, fillings and festival practices vary among Newar families and communities. This is a practical chaku-sesame style.

Before you begin

Preparation notes

Work with warm dough

Rice-flour dough becomes less flexible as it cools. Keep unused portions covered and slightly warm.

Cool the filling before shaping

Hot, runny chaku is difficult to seal inside a thin rice shell.

Keep walls even

Thin spots can burst in the steamer; thick spots remain dense and pasty.

Cook it

Ingredients & detailed method

Ingredients

  • 2 cups fine rice flour
  • 1 1/4–1 1/2 cups very hot water
  • Pinch of salt
  • 1 tsp neutral oil or ghee
  • 3/4 cup grated chaku or dark jaggery
  • 3 tbsp toasted sesame seeds, lightly crushed
  • 2 tbsp unsweetened coconut, optional
  • 1/4 tsp ground cardamom

Method

  1. 1.Make the dough. Mix rice flour and salt, then pour in hot water gradually while stirring with a sturdy spoon.
  2. 2.Knead warm. Once safe to handle, knead until smooth and work in the oil or ghee. Checkpoint: the dough should feel soft and pliable without cracking around the edges.
  3. 3.Keep covered. Wrap the dough or cover with a damp towel while you prepare the filling.
  4. 4.Make the filling. Melt chaku or jaggery over low heat with 1–2 tablespoons water. Stir in sesame, coconut and cardamom.
  5. 5.Cool to spoonable. Checkpoint: the filling should be thick enough to sit on a spoon rather than run like syrup.
  6. 6.Form a shell. Roll a golf-ball-size dough piece, then use a damp finger to hollow it while pinching and rotating to create a tapered cup.
  7. 7.Check the walls. They should be about 1/8 inch thick and reasonably even, with no translucent weak spots.
  8. 8.Fill and seal. Add 1–2 teaspoons filling, leaving clean dough at the rim. Pinch the opening firmly closed and refine the pointed shape.
  9. 9.Prepare the steamer. Line or lightly oil the basket and arrange yomari with room between them.
  10. 10.Steam. Steam over actively simmering water for 15–18 minutes.
  11. 11.Check doneness. Checkpoint: the exterior should look slightly glossy and set rather than powdery, and the shell should feel firm enough to lift without sagging.
  12. 12.Rest and serve. Let stand 2–3 minutes, then serve warm while the center is soft.
