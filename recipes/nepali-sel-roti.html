Nepal / Himalayas

Nepali Sel Roti

Bread / Sweet · 2 hrs · 10 to 12 rings

A ring of lightly sweet rice batter fried until crisp outside and soft within, inseparable from Nepali festival tables and family gatherings.

Fringe Table adaptation

About this dish

Sel roti is a traditional Nepali ring-shaped fried rice bread. Unlike a wheat doughnut, it starts with soaked rice ground into a pourable batter, often sweetened and enriched with milk, ghee or banana depending on the household. The challenge is batter consistency: it must flow smoothly enough to form a continuous ring while holding together as soon as it hits hot oil.

Story & history

Why this dish matters

The Nepal Tourism Board highlights sel roti as a traditional food of Dashain and also notes its presence at Tamu Lhosar celebrations, where shared foods help carry community traditions between generations. It is also strongly associated with Tihar, weddings and other auspicious gatherings. Because the batter is often made by feel, experienced cooks can pour near-perfect rings directly by hand; a squeeze bottle or measuring cup makes the first attempts more manageable at home.

Rice varieties, soaking times, sweeteners and enrichments vary across Nepali households and communities. This version uses a blender-friendly batter and a squeeze bottle option for safer, more repeatable shaping.

Before you begin

Preparation notes

Soak the rice long enough

Four hours is workable; overnight soaking makes the rice easier to blend completely smooth.

Aim for thick ribbon consistency

The batter should pour continuously but not spread flat like crepe batter.

Practice over a cool bowl first

Before working over hot oil, test whether you can pour one continuous ring from your bottle or cup.

Cook it

Ingredients & detailed method

Ingredients

  • 2 cups uncooked medium- or long-grain white rice
  • 1/2 cup whole milk, plus more as needed
  • 1/3 cup sugar
  • 2 tbsp melted ghee or butter
  • 1 small ripe banana, optional
  • 1/2 tsp ground cardamom
  • 1/4 tsp ground fennel, optional
  • Pinch of salt
  • Neutral oil, for deep frying

Method

  1. 1.Rinse rice well and soak in plenty of cool water for at least 4 hours or overnight. Drain thoroughly.
  2. 2.Blend soaked rice with milk, sugar, ghee, banana if using, cardamom, fennel and salt until extremely smooth. Stop and scrape the blender several times.
  3. 3.Rest the batter 30 minutes. Checkpoint: it should fall from a spoon in a thick continuous ribbon. Add milk 1 tablespoon at a time if too stiff, or a spoonful of rice flour if excessively thin.
  4. 4.Heat 1 1/2 to 2 inches of oil in a wide heavy pan to about 350°F.
  5. 5.Transfer batter to a squeeze bottle, pitcher with a narrow spout or measuring cup. Pour a continuous 5- to 6-inch ring directly into the oil, joining the end to the beginning.
  6. 6.Fry until the underside is golden and firm, about 60–90 seconds, then turn carefully with tongs or a skewer.
  7. 7.Cook the second side until deep golden. Checkpoint: the ring should be crisp at the edges yet spring back slightly when pressed, not feel hard all the way through.
  8. 8.Drain on a rack and repeat, letting the oil return to temperature between rings.
  9. 9.Serve warm or at room temperature with tea, yogurt, achar or festival foods.
