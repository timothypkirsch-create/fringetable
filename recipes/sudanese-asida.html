Sudan / Northeast Africa

Sudanese Asida

Staple / Side · 35 min · 6 servings

Flour and water become a cohesive, glossy mound through constant stirring and careful hydration.

Fringe Table adaptation

About this dish

Asida is a thick cooked flour staple found in Sudan and across parts of North and East Africa. Sudanese versions may be made from wheat, sorghum or other flours and are served with savory sauces or sometimes sweet accompaniments.

Story & history

Why this dish matters

The defining technique is vigorous stirring as flour cooks into water, producing a smooth elastic mass with no raw-flour taste. Grain choice varies according to household and region.

This wheat-flour version is a practical home preparation; sorghum-based versions have a different flavor and texture.

Before you begin

Preparation notes

Use a sturdy spoon

The mixture becomes very thick.

Add flour gradually

This prevents hard lumps.

Cook out raw flavor

Do not stop as soon as it thickens.

Cook it

Ingredients & detailed method

Ingredients

  • 2 cups all-purpose flour
  • 4 cups water
  • 1 tsp salt
  • 1 tbsp oil, for shaping

Method

  1. 1.Bring 3 cups water and salt to a boil.
  2. 2.Whisk flour with remaining cool water into a smooth slurry.
  3. 3.Pour slurry into boiling water while stirring constantly.
  4. 4.Switch to a sturdy wooden spoon and beat over medium-low heat 15–20 minutes.
  5. 5.Checkpoint: mixture should pull cleanly from the pot sides, look glossy and taste fully cooked rather than floury.
  6. 6.Oil a bowl lightly and pack in the asida.
  7. 7.Invert onto a serving plate and serve with stew or sauce.
