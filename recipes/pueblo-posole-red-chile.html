Pueblo Posole with Red Chile

Soup / Main · 4 hrs · 8 servings

A long-simmered bowl where prepared corn, pork and red chile become more than the sum of a short ingredient list.

Fringe Table adaptation

About this dish

Posole is both the prepared hominy corn and the stew made from it. Native New Mexico food resources document hominy stews across Pueblo communities, where large pots of posole are associated with feast days, New Year gatherings and cold-weather meals. This version uses dried or frozen prepared posole and a simple red-chile seasoning.

Story & history

Why this dish matters

Lois Ellen Frank’s Foods of the Southwest Indian Nations describes posole as a rustic stew common throughout New Mexico pueblos and often prepared in quantity for feast days. New Mexico State University extension materials likewise document Native New Mexico holiday posole built from prepared corn, meat, onion, garlic and red chile. Corn remains the cultural center of the dish; the pork and chile reflect layers of regional history that developed over time.

Pueblo communities are distinct nations with their own food traditions. This is a public, home-kitchen adaptation based on documented New Mexico Pueblo-associated posole, not a ceremonial formula.

Before you begin

Preparation notes

Use prepared posole corn

Dried or frozen posole is nixtamalized corn intended for this style of stew; ordinary canned sweet corn is not a substitute.

Start dried corn early

Dried posole may take several hours before the kernels flower open.

Keep chile adjustable

New Mexico red chile varies in heat. Build flavor first, then add more chile near the end if desired.

Cook it

Ingredients & detailed method

Ingredients

  • 1 lb dried posole corn, soaked overnight, or 2 lb frozen prepared posole
  • 2 lb pork shoulder, cut into 1 1/2-inch pieces
  • 10 cups water, plus more as needed
  • 1 medium onion, chopped
  • 4 garlic cloves, minced
  • 3 to 6 dried New Mexico red chile pods, stemmed and seeded, or 1/3 cup pure New Mexico red chile powder
  • 1 tsp dried oregano
  • 1 tsp ground cumin
  • 2 tsp kosher salt, plus more to taste

Method

  1. 1.If using dried posole, drain the soaked corn and place it in a large heavy pot with 10 cups fresh water. Bring to a boil, then reduce to a steady simmer for 1 1/2 hours before adding meat.
  2. 2.Add pork, onion and garlic. Simmer gently 1 hour, skimming only excessive foam or fat.
  3. 3.Toast dried chile pods briefly in a dry skillet until fragrant but not blackened, then soak them in hot water 20 minutes and blend smooth with some soaking liquid. If using pure chile powder, whisk it with hot broth from the pot.
  4. 4.Stir the red chile into the stew with oregano and cumin. Continue simmering 1 to 1 1/2 hours.
  5. 5.Checkpoint: the posole kernels should swell and begin to open like small flowers, while the pork should be fork-tender. Add hot water as needed to keep the stew brothy.
  6. 6.Season with salt and continue 10 minutes so the seasoning moves through the broth.
  7. 7.Rest off heat 15 minutes before serving. The broth should be deeply corn-scented with red chile supporting rather than masking the posole.
