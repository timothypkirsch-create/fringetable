Armenian Zhingyalov Hats (Herb-Stuffed Flatbread)

Stuffed Flatbread / Snack or Light Meal · 1 hr 20 min · 4 large flatbreads

The dough is deliberately spare so the greens lead: spinach and chard give body, herbs supply perfume, and sorrel or watercress brings the sour edge that keeps the filling vivid.

Fringe Table adaptation

About this dish

A Smithsonian cooking demonstration based on the cookbook Lavash presents zhingyalov hats as a thin Armenian flatbread packed with a carefully balanced mixture of greens and herbs. Armenian usage literally describes bread with zhengyal, a dialect word for herbs or greens. The dish is particularly associated with Armenian communities from Artsakh and Syunik. A home skillet replaces the large saj griddle, but the essential method remains: roll unleavened dough thin, enclose a substantial raw herb filling, seal it securely and cook it dry until blistered.

Story & history

Why this dish matters

The abundance of greens is not decorative. Cooks build the filling from several roles: mild leaves form the bulk, aromatic herbs add intensity, and sour or peppery greens keep the mixture lively. The exact plants change with season, place and family knowledge, and Armenian cooks may use far more wild and cultivated greens than a supermarket version can reproduce. Contemporary Armenian restaurants in the diaspora preserve that flexibility; the MICHELIN Guide records a Glendale bakery using more than a dozen varieties while emphasizing that families do not share one mandatory formula.

Spellings including zhingyalov hats, zhengyalov hatz and jingalov hats represent the same Armenian name in Latin letters. This recipe uses cultivated greens that are realistic and safe for a North American kitchen; do not forage unfamiliar wild plants to imitate a longer traditional list. Qutab and gözleme are neighboring filled-flatbread traditions, but their dough, shapes, fillings and serving contexts should not be treated as interchangeable with this Armenian dish.

Before you begin

Preparation notes

Dry every leaf

Wash greens well ahead and dry them completely. Surface water steams the dough, makes sealing difficult and leaves the interior soggy.

Balance three kinds of green

Use roughly half mild leaves, one quarter aromatic herbs and one quarter sour or peppery leaves rather than letting one assertive herb dominate.

Salt only before filling

Salt draws water from chopped greens quickly. Mix it in after the dough is rolled and cook each filled bread without delay.

Cook it

Ingredients & detailed method

Ingredients

  • 1 1/2 cups all-purpose flour, plus more for dusting
  • 1 tsp kosher salt
  • 2/3 cup lukewarm water, plus 1 to 2 tbsp only if needed
  • 4 packed cups baby spinach and thinly sliced Swiss chard
  • 1 packed cup finely sliced flat-leaf parsley
  • 1/2 packed cup finely chopped dill
  • 1/2 packed cup finely chopped cilantro or chervil
  • 1 packed cup finely sliced sorrel, watercress or peppery arugula
  • 3 scallions, white and green parts thinly sliced
  • 1 tsp sweet paprika
  • 1/2 tsp Aleppo pepper or mild red-pepper flakes
  • 1 1/2 tbsp sunflower or neutral oil
  • 1 tbsp fresh lemon juice
  • 3/4 tsp kosher salt, for the filling
  • Plain yogurt, tan or lemon wedges, optional for serving

Method

  1. 1.Dissolve 1 teaspoon salt in the lukewarm water. Add the flour and mix until no dry patches remain, adding up to 2 tablespoons more water only if the dough will not come together.
  2. 2.Knead on a lightly floured surface for 5 to 7 minutes, until smooth and elastic. Cover and rest 25 to 40 minutes. Checkpoint: a pressed indentation should relax slowly, and the dough should roll without springing immediately back.
  3. 3.While the dough rests, stack and slice all greens and herbs very fine. Spread them briefly on a clean towel and blot again; the mixture should feel leafy and dry rather than wet or compacted.
  4. 4.Combine the mild greens, aromatic herbs, sour or peppery greens and scallions in a very large bowl. Leave the paprika, Aleppo pepper, oil, lemon juice and filling salt separate until the first dough round is ready.
  5. 5.Divide the rested dough into 4 equal balls and keep three covered. Roll one ball on a floured surface into an 8- to 9-inch circle, turning often, until it is about 1/16 inch thick and faintly translucent without holes.
  6. 6.Toss the entire greens mixture with paprika, Aleppo pepper, oil, lemon juice and 3/4 teaspoon salt. Use your hands to distribute the seasoning without crushing the leaves. Divide the filling visually into four portions.
  7. 7.Mound one portion of greens down the center of the dough, leaving a 1-inch border. Bring the left and right edges together over the filling and pinch from one pointed end to the other, expelling large air pockets as you go.
  8. 8.Turn the sealed bread seam-side down and press gently with your palms into a flattened oval about 1/3 inch thick. Checkpoint: greens should reach the pointed ends, the seam should remain closed and no stem should pierce the dough.
  9. 9.Heat a large dry cast-iron skillet or griddle over medium-high heat. Lay in the bread seam-side down, reduce to medium and cook 2 1/2 to 3 minutes, until the underside has browned spots and releases easily.
  10. 10.Flip and cook 2 to 3 minutes more. Press lightly around any pale areas and turn once or twice as needed. The finished dough should be cooked through and flexible with scattered dark blisters; the greens inside should be collapsed, aromatic and still bright rather than watery.
  11. 11.Repeat with the remaining dough and filling, wiping loose flour from the skillet between breads so it does not burn. Keep finished flatbreads under a clean towel for a softer surface or on a rack for a slightly crisper one.
  12. 12.Serve warm or at room temperature, whole or cut into strips, with optional yogurt, tan or lemon. Cool leftovers completely before wrapping; refrigerate up to 2 days and reheat in a dry skillet.
