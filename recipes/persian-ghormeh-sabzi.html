Iran / Persia

Persian Ghormeh Sabzi

Main · 3 hrs · 6 servings

A generous mass of herbs is patiently fried, then simmered with lamb, beans and dried lime into one of Iran’s defining khoreshes.

Fringe Table adaptation

About this dish

Ghormeh sabzi is a Persian khoresh, or slow-simmered stew, built on chopped herbs fried until dark and intensely fragrant. Lamb or beef, beans and pierced dried limes then cook together until the meat is tender and the sauce looks deep green-brown.

The herb mixture is the engine of the dish. Parsley, cilantro and scallion provide volume, while fenugreek contributes a concentrated aroma that can turn bitter if used carelessly. Dried lime gives a rounded tart-bitter flavor rather than fresh lemon's brightness.

Story & history

Why this dish matters

Ghormeh sabzi is a familiar reference point in Iranian home cooking, yet there is no single national formula. Bean choices, herb ratios and meat vary by region and family; kidney beans are common, while black-eyed peas appear in some southern versions.

The technique belongs to the wider Persian khoresh tradition: ingredients are browned or fried separately for depth, then given time to merge into a sauce served with rice. A successful pot tastes integrated rather than like chopped fresh herbs floating in broth.

This version uses lamb and cooked kidney beans. It preserves the characteristic herb, fenugreek and dried-lime structure while acknowledging substantial household variation.

Before you begin

Preparation notes

Dry herbs thoroughly

Surface water makes the herbs steam and delays the crucial frying stage.

Use fenugreek carefully

Its aroma should be clear, but excess can make the entire stew bitter.

Manage dried lime

Pierce and soak the limes; remove them early if the bitterness becomes dominant.

Cook it

Ingredients & detailed method

Ingredients

  • 1 pound boneless lamb shoulder, cut into 1-inch pieces
  • 1 large onion, finely chopped
  • 1 teaspoon ground turmeric
  • 1/4 cup neutral oil, divided
  • 2 packed cups finely chopped flat-leaf parsley
  • 1 packed cup finely chopped cilantro
  • 1 packed cup finely chopped scallions or Persian leeks
  • 2 tablespoons dried fenugreek leaves
  • 3 dried Persian limes (limoo omani)
  • 4 cups hot water, plus more as needed
  • 1 1/2 cups cooked kidney beans, drained
  • 1 1/4 teaspoons fine salt, divided
  • 1/2 teaspoon freshly ground black pepper
  • Steamed Persian rice, for serving

Method

  1. 1.Rinse the dried limes, pierce each several times with a skewer and soak in hot water for 20 minutes. Drain.
  2. 2.Wash the fresh herbs, dry them very thoroughly and chop finely. Crush the dried fenugreek between your palms.
  3. 3.Heat 3 tablespoons oil in a wide skillet over medium heat. Fry the fresh herbs 15–20 minutes, stirring often, until dark green, reduced and deeply aromatic.
  4. 4.Add the fenugreek for the final 2 minutes. Transfer the herb mixture to a bowl before it scorches.
  5. 5.Heat the remaining oil in a heavy pot. Cook the onion 7–9 minutes until golden, then add turmeric and black pepper for 30 seconds.
  6. 6.Add the lamb and 3/4 teaspoon salt. Brown the pieces on several sides, 6–8 minutes. Stir in the fried herbs.
  7. 7.Add 4 cups hot water and the pierced limes. Bring to a boil, then lower to a bare simmer, cover partially and cook 75–90 minutes.
  8. 8.Checkpoint: the lamb should be nearly tender and the sauce dark and aromatic. Taste the liquid; remove one lime if its bitterness is already strong.
  9. 9.Add the cooked beans and simmer uncovered 25–35 minutes. Stir occasionally and add hot water only if the stew threatens to catch.
  10. 10.Checkpoint: the lamb should yield to a spoon and a little green oil should glisten on a spoon-coating sauce. Season with the remaining salt as needed.
  11. 11.Rest off heat for 10 minutes. Serve with steamed Persian rice, warning diners that the whole dried limes are intensely flavored.
