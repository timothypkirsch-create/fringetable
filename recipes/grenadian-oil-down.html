Grenada / Eastern Caribbean

Grenadian Oil Down

One-Pot Stew / Main · 2 hrs 15 min · 8 servings

Grenada's national dish is packed rather than stirred: breadfruit, seasoned meat, callaloo and dumplings cook together while coconut milk reduces into a rich coating.

Fringe Table adaptation

About this dish

The Government of Grenada identifies oil down as the national dish and documents its defining combination of breadfruit, young dasheen leaves, salted meat, dumplings, coconut milk, aromatics and turmeric, which Grenadian cooks often call saffron. Grenadian chef Keith Le Liber likewise emphasizes breadfruit, callaloo and turmeric-scented coconut milk, along with the method of packing the pot and leaving it unstirred. This home-kitchen version keeps those anchors, uses a heavy Dutch oven in place of the large outdoor pot common at group cook-ups, and scales the dish for eight.

Story & history

Why this dish matters

Oil down takes its name from its finish: water in the coconut milk cooks away until the separated coconut oil seasons and glosses the packed ingredients. The pot is also a social form. Grenadian cooks describe making it on beaches, over wood fires and for groups in which people contribute ingredients and company. No single meat or vegetable list covers every household. Salted pigtail, salt beef, chicken, fish, crab and meatless versions all appear, but breadfruit and the slow reduction of the coconut-rich pot remain central to the dish's Grenadian identity.

Breadfruit is not interchangeable with potato if the finished dish is to be presented as oil down; Grenadian sources consistently treat it as foundational. Salted meats vary by household, so use salted pigtail or salt beef according to availability and desalinate it before the main cook. True callaloo or young dasheen leaves must be thoroughly cooked because raw taro-family leaves are irritating; packaged frozen callaloo is the most practical option where fresh edible leaves and knowledgeable handling are unavailable.

Before you begin

Preparation notes

Desalt before seasoning

Soak salted pigtail or salt beef overnight in the refrigerator, changing the water once or twice. A preliminary boil removes more salt and prevents the coconut reduction from becoming harshly salty.

Pack a wide, heavy pot

A 6- to 8-quart Dutch oven gives the breadfruit room to line the bottom and sides. Once the layers are assembled, do not stir; disturbing them can break the breadfruit and turn the dish soupy.

Listen near the finish

The sound changes from a wet simmer to a quieter sizzle as the water cooks away. Check frequently at that point so the coconut solids and breadfruit do not scorch.

Cook it

Ingredients & detailed method

Ingredients

  • 1 1/4 lb salted pigtail or salt beef, soaked overnight in cold water
  • 1 medium mature breadfruit, about 3 lb, peeled, cored and cut into 2-inch wedges
  • 2 green bananas, peeled and cut into 2-inch pieces
  • 2 medium carrots, cut into thick coins
  • 8 oz packaged frozen callaloo or properly prepared young dasheen leaves, thawed
  • 1 large yellow onion, chopped
  • 4 scallions, thinly sliced
  • 4 garlic cloves, minced
  • 1 tbsp fresh thyme leaves, or 1 tsp dried thyme
  • 2 tsp ground turmeric
  • 1/4 tsp freshly grated nutmeg
  • 1/2 tsp freshly ground black pepper
  • 1 whole Scotch bonnet pepper, left uncut
  • 4 cups full-fat unsweetened coconut milk
  • 1 1/2 cups all-purpose flour
  • 1/2 tsp kosher salt, for the dumplings
  • 1/2 to 2/3 cup water, for the dumplings
  • Kosher salt only if needed after cooking

Method

  1. 1.Drain the soaked meat, cover it with fresh water in a saucepan and boil 15 minutes. Drain, repeat with fresh water, then simmer in a third change of water for 20 to 30 minutes, until the meat is beginning to tenderize but is not falling apart.
  2. 2.Checkpoint: taste a tiny trimmed piece of the cooked meat. It should still be pleasantly seasoned but no longer sharply salty. Drain well and cut large pieces into 2-inch portions.
  3. 3.Mix flour and 1/2 teaspoon salt. Add 1/2 cup water and knead briefly, adding only enough of the remaining water to make a firm, smooth dough. Cover and rest 15 minutes, then roll into 16 slender dumplings about 3 inches long.
  4. 4.Whisk onion, scallions, garlic, thyme, turmeric, nutmeg and black pepper into the coconut milk. Pour about 1 cup of this seasoned milk into a 6- to 8-quart heavy pot.
  5. 5.Pack the breadfruit wedges closely across the bottom and up the sides. Set the drained meat in the center, then layer in green banana and carrots. Pour in the remaining seasoned coconut milk; it should reach roughly halfway to two-thirds up the packed ingredients rather than covering them completely.
  6. 6.Spread the callaloo over the top and tuck in the whole Scotch bonnet without piercing it. Cover tightly, bring to a steady boil over medium heat, then reduce to medium-low and cook 30 minutes without stirring.
  7. 7.Arrange the dumplings over the greens, cover again and simmer 25 to 35 minutes. Turn the pot a quarter turn on the burner occasionally if your heat is uneven, but do not stir the layers.
  8. 8.Checkpoint: the callaloo must be fully wilted and tender, the dumplings should be firm and cooked through at the center, and a knife should slide into the breadfruit without resistance. Continue covered cooking if any component is still firm.
  9. 9.Uncover for the final 10 to 20 minutes and reduce the heat to low. Watch and listen closely as the remaining liquid thickens; the dish is ready when there is no watery broth, coconut oil glistens around the edges and the pot makes a gentle frying sound.
  10. 10.Remove the whole pepper. Rest 10 minutes, then serve by lifting through the layers so each portion includes breadfruit, meat, greens, vegetables and a dumpling. Taste before adding any salt.
  11. 11.Refrigerate leftovers within 2 hours. Reheat covered with a small splash of water until steaming hot throughout; the breadfruit will soften further but should remain in identifiable pieces.
