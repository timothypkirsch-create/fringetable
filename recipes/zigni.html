Eritrea

Zigni

Main · 2 hr 30 min · Serves 6

A slow-simmered Eritrean beef stew with onions, tomato and the deep chile warmth of berbere.

Fringe Table adaptation

About this dish

Zigni is one of Eritrea’s best-known stews, commonly served with injera. Recipes vary in heat, meat cut and the balance of onion, tomato and berbere. This adaptation favors a long, gentle simmer that develops body without requiring specialty equipment.

Cook it

Ingredients & method

Ingredients

  • 2 lb beef chuck, cut into 1-inch cubes
  • 2 tbsp neutral oil
  • 2 large onions, finely chopped
  • 4 garlic cloves, minced
  • 2–3 tbsp berbere, to taste
  • 2 tbsp tomato paste
  • 1 can (14 oz) crushed tomatoes
  • 2 cups beef stock or water
  • 1 tsp kosher salt, plus more to taste
  • Injera, for serving

Method

  1. 1. Brown the beef in batches in a heavy pot with the oil; transfer to a plate.
  2. 2. Lower heat and cook onions until deeply softened, 12–15 minutes. Stir in garlic and berbere for 1 minute.
  3. 3. Add tomato paste and crushed tomatoes, scraping the pot. Return beef and add stock.
  4. 4. Bring to a low simmer, cover partially and cook 1 1/2–2 hours, until the beef is tender and the sauce has thickened.
  5. 5. Adjust salt and berbere, then serve with injera.
