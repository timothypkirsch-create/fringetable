South Carolina Lowcountry

Lowcountry Shrimp and Grits

Main · 45 min · 4 servings

Slow-cooked stone-ground grits meet quickly seared shrimp and a bright, restrained pan sauce in this coastal Carolina supper.

Fringe Table adaptation

About this dish

Shrimp and grits pairs two ingredients deeply at home on the southeastern coast: freshly milled corn grits and local shrimp. The older breakfast pairing was simpler than many contemporary restaurant plates; later versions added bacon, cheese, cream and elaborate sauces.

This home adaptation uses bacon for savory depth but keeps the sauce light enough that the corn and shrimp remain distinct. Stone-ground grits need patient cooking and more liquid than quick grits, while shrimp need only minutes.

Story & history

Why this dish matters

The dish belongs within the layered food history of the South Carolina Lowcountry, not to a single restaurant origin story. The Gullah Geechee Cultural Heritage Corridor describes a coastal food system shaped by African knowledge, Native American corn, local seafood and the labor and creativity of enslaved cooks.

That history deserves more than a decorative label. Corn, fishing, cast-net traditions and one-pot thrift all help explain why grits and seafood became durable coastal partners long before shrimp and grits became a fine-dining signature.

Recipes vary across families and eras. This is a restrained Lowcountry-style interpretation, not a claim to reproduce one definitive Gullah Geechee household recipe.

Before you begin

Preparation notes

Choose stone-ground grits

Their fuller corn flavor and texture are central; instant grits behave differently.

Season in layers

Salt the grits, then taste the bacon before seasoning the shrimp sauce.

Cook shrimp last

Have every garnish ready because shrimp turn firm quickly.

Cook it

Ingredients & detailed method

Ingredients

  • 1 cup stone-ground grits
  • 4 cups water or unsalted chicken stock, plus more as needed
  • 1 teaspoon fine salt, divided
  • 2 tablespoons unsalted butter
  • 4 ounces bacon, cut into small pieces
  • 1 pound large shrimp, peeled and deveined
  • 2 garlic cloves, finely chopped
  • 1/4 teaspoon freshly ground black pepper
  • 2 scallions, thinly sliced, white and green parts separated
  • 1 tablespoon fresh lemon juice
  • 1/4 cup hot water or stock, for the pan sauce

Method

  1. 1.Bring 4 cups water or stock and 3/4 teaspoon salt to a boil in a heavy saucepan. Whisk in the grits gradually so they do not clump.
  2. 2.Lower the heat until the surface barely bubbles. Cook uncovered 25–40 minutes, stirring often and scraping the bottom; add hot water when the mixture tightens before the grains are tender.
  3. 3.Checkpoint: the grits should taste creamy but retain a faint corn texture, with no hard center. Stir in the butter, cover and hold over the lowest heat.
  4. 4.Meanwhile, pat the shrimp very dry. Season with black pepper and only a small pinch of the remaining salt.
  5. 5.Render the bacon in a wide skillet over medium heat until crisp, 6–8 minutes. Transfer the pieces to a plate and leave about 2 tablespoons fat in the pan.
  6. 6.Add scallion whites and garlic to the skillet and stir for 30 seconds. Raise the heat to medium-high and arrange the shrimp in one layer.
  7. 7.Cook about 1–2 minutes per side, turning when the bottom edges turn opaque. Remove the shrimp as soon as they are just pink and loosely curled.
  8. 8.Add 1/4 cup hot water or stock and scrape up the browned bits. Simmer 1 minute, then turn off the heat and stir in lemon juice, bacon and the shrimp.
  9. 9.Checkpoint: the sauce should lightly glaze the shrimp rather than pool like gravy. Taste before adding any remaining salt.
  10. 10.Loosen the grits if necessary, divide among warm bowls and spoon the shrimp and pan juices over them. Finish with scallion greens and serve immediately.
