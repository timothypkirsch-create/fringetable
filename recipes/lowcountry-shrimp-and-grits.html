South Carolina Lowcountry

Lowcountry Shrimp and Grits

Main · 45 min · 4 servings

Creamy stone-ground grits provide the foundation while shrimp cook fast in a savory skillet sauce above them.

Fringe Table adaptation

About this dish

Shrimp and grits grew from coastal Carolina foodways where corn grits and local shrimp were practical staples. Restaurant versions became richer and more elaborate in the late twentieth century, but the basic pairing is much older.

Story & history

Why this dish matters

Gullah Geechee food history is essential to understanding Lowcountry rice, corn and seafood cooking. This version keeps the grits central and avoids drowning the dish in cream or excessive cheese.

Modern restaurant shrimp and grits vary enormously. This is a restrained Lowcountry-style home version.

Before you begin

Preparation notes

Use stone-ground grits

They have more corn flavor and texture.

Cook grits slowly

They need time to soften fully.

Shrimp cook last

Overcooked shrimp turn rubbery.

Cook it

Ingredients & detailed method

Ingredients

  • 1 cup stone-ground grits
  • 4 cups water or stock
  • 1 tsp salt
  • 2 tbsp butter
  • 1 lb peeled shrimp
  • 4 oz bacon, chopped
  • 2 scallions
  • 2 garlic cloves
  • 1 tbsp lemon juice
  • Black pepper

Method

  1. 1.Simmer grits in salted water or stock, stirring often, until creamy and tender, 25–35 minutes.
  2. 2.Stir in butter and keep warm.
  3. 3.Render bacon in a skillet; remove excess fat if needed.
  4. 4.Add garlic, then shrimp in one layer.
  5. 5.Cook shrimp 1–2 minutes per side.
  6. 6.Checkpoint: shrimp should be opaque and just curled, not tightly contracted.
  7. 7.Finish with lemon, pepper and scallions and spoon over grits.
