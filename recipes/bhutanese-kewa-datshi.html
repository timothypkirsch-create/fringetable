Bhutan / Himalayas

Bhutanese Kewa Datshi

Main / Side · 30 min · 4 servings

Potatoes and cheese make the gentler cousin of ema datshi, still unmistakably Bhutanese in its chile-and-datshi logic.

Fringe Table adaptation

About this dish

Kewa datshi means potato and cheese. It belongs to Bhutan’s datshi family of dishes, where local cheese forms a sauce around vegetables or chile. Compared with ema datshi, potatoes make this version milder and more substantial, though green chile is still a normal part of the pot.

Story & history

Why this dish matters

Bhutan’s Ministry of Energy and Natural Resources eCookbook identifies kewa datshi as one of the country’s famous dishes and publishes a straightforward potato, onion, tomato, green chile and cheese method. Bhutan tourism resources similarly describe it as a popular, nourishing specialty and a milder relative of ema datshi. Imported cheese substitutions change the flavor, but the simmer-then-melt method remains accessible to home cooks.

Traditional datshi cheese is difficult to source outside Bhutan. A mix of farmer cheese, feta and mild melting cheese can approximate its tang and body but is still a substitute.

Before you begin

Preparation notes

Slice potatoes evenly

Thin uniform pieces cook before the cheese is added.

Do not brown the onions

The official-style method is closer to simmering than sautéing deeply.

Let cheese melt before stirring hard

Gentle heat keeps the sauce cohesive.

Cook it

Ingredients & detailed method

Ingredients

  • 1 1/2 lb waxy potatoes, peeled and sliced 1/4 inch thick
  • 1 medium onion, thinly sliced
  • 1 medium tomato, sliced
  • 2 to 4 green chiles, sliced lengthwise
  • 1 tbsp butter or vegetable oil
  • 2 cups water, approximately
  • 6 oz farmer cheese, feta or a blend with mild melting cheese
  • 1 tsp kosher salt, plus more to taste
  • Chopped scallion or cilantro, optional

Method

  1. 1.Combine potatoes, onion, tomato, chiles, oil and enough water to almost cover the potatoes in a wide saucepan.
  2. 2.Bring to a boil, cover and cook over medium-high heat 10–12 minutes.
  3. 3.Checkpoint: potatoes should be nearly tender but still hold their slices.
  4. 4.Scatter cheese over the pot, reduce heat and cover again for 3–5 minutes until melted.
  5. 5.Gently fold the cheese through the cooking liquid rather than stirring aggressively.
  6. 6.Simmer uncovered another 2–4 minutes until the sauce lightly coats the potatoes.
  7. 7.Taste for salt, rest 2 minutes and serve hot with Bhutanese red rice or steamed white rice.
