Haudenosaunee / Indigenous Americas

Haudenosaunee White Corn Soup — Home Adaptation

Soup / Main · 4 hrs · 8 servings

Prepared white corn, beans and meat simmer together for hours in a soup whose importance reaches far beyond its short ingredient list.

Fringe Table adaptation

About this dish

White corn soup is a deeply important Haudenosaunee food with distinct nation, community and family versions. Public Mohawk and Seneca recipes commonly combine specially prepared white corn with beans and meat. This page deliberately starts with already hulled, food-ready Haudenosaunee white corn so it does not turn the culturally significant hardwood-ash preparation process into a casual shortcut tutorial.

Story & history

Why this dish matters

Onondaga Nation describes Onehogwa', corn soup, as a food served within community life and ceremony and emphasizes the importance of white corn grown among Haudenosaunee communities. First Nations Health Authority publishes Akwesasne Mohawk cook Richard Jock's family corn-soup recipe and explicitly notes the friendly rivalries among Iroquois communities over beans, vegetables and other additions. Cornell's Traditional Iroquois Corn guide documents a Seneca preparation from Tonawanda. Together, these sources make clear that there is no single generic 'Iroquois soup' formula.

This home adaptation assumes you have purchased properly prepared hulled Haudenosaunee white corn from an appropriate food source. It intentionally does not reproduce ceremonial context or provide casual hardwood-ash processing instructions.

Before you begin

Preparation notes

Start with food-ready hulled white corn

Do not substitute ordinary canned sweet corn and do not improvise lye or ash processing without community-grounded instruction.

Cook the meat until yielding

The long simmer builds the broth and should leave the beef easy to pull apart.

Expect community variation

Kidney beans, white beans, turnip, onion, pork, venison and beef all appear in publicly shared versions; this is one practical pathway.

Cook it

Ingredients & detailed method

Ingredients

  • 3 cups cooked or food-ready hulled Haudenosaunee white corn
  • 1 1/2 lb beef chuck or venison, cut into 1-inch pieces
  • 10 cups water
  • 1 cup dried red kidney beans, soaked overnight and drained
  • 1 medium turnip, diced
  • 1 medium onion, diced
  • 1 1/2 tsp kosher salt, plus more to taste

Method

  1. 1.Combine meat and water in a large heavy pot. Bring to a boil, skim foam if desired, then reduce to a gentle simmer for 1 1/2 hours.
  2. 2.Add soaked beans and turnip. Continue simmering about 1 hour, adding hot water if the solids rise above the broth.
  3. 3.Add onion and cook 20 minutes.
  4. 4.Add the prepared hulled white corn and continue simmering 30–45 minutes.
  5. 5.Checkpoint: beans should be creamy inside, meat should pull apart easily and the prepared corn should be tender and swollen while still recognizable.
  6. 6.Season with salt only after the long simmer, since evaporation concentrates the broth.
  7. 7.Rest 10 minutes before serving. The soup should be substantial and corn-forward rather than heavily spiced.
