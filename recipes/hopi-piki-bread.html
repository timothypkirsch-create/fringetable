Hopi Piki Bread — Home Adaptation

Bread · 45 min · Serves 8 rolled sheets

A safety-conscious home approximation inspired by Hopi piki: ultra-thin blue-corn sheets cooked on a griddle and rolled.

Fringe Table adaptation

About this dish

Traditional Hopi piki is a highly skilled food with cultural meaning, traditionally made on a hot stone and historically involving culinary ash. The traditional hand-smearing technique is not appropriate to imitate casually in a home kitchen because of the extreme heat involved. This page therefore offers an explicitly modern griddle approximation rather than presenting it as traditional piki technique.

Cook it

Ingredients & method

Ingredients

  • 1 cup very fine blue cornmeal
  • 1 1/2–2 cups hot water, as needed
  • 1/4 tsp salt
  • 1 tsp neutral oil for the pan
  • Optional: a very small amount of food-grade culinary ash only if obtained from a trusted food source and used with knowledgeable guidance

Method

  1. 1. Do not use unknown fireplace or wood ash, and do not attempt the traditional bare-hand hot-stone technique.
  2. 2. Whisk blue cornmeal and salt with enough hot water to make a very thin, pourable batter. Rest 10 minutes and thin again if it thickens.
  3. 3. Heat a smooth nonstick crepe pan or griddle over medium heat and wipe with the thinnest film of oil.
  4. 4. Brush or spread an extremely thin layer of batter over the surface using a heat-safe silicone brush or offset spatula. Cook until dry and able to lift cleanly.
  5. 5. Peel up with a spatula, stack the thin sheets under a towel, then roll several sheets together for serving.
