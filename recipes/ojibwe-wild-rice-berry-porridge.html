Ojibwe Wild Rice & Berry Porridge

Breakfast · 50 min · 4 servings

Nutty wild rice simmers until tender, then meets berries and maple in a warm breakfast centered on manoomin.

Fringe Table adaptation

About this dish

Manoomin, often translated as wild rice, is culturally important to Ojibwe and other Great Lakes Indigenous communities. This breakfast preparation uses cooked wild rice with berries and maple while keeping the framing specific to the ingredient's cultural importance rather than presenting one modern porridge as universal tradition.

Story & history

Why this dish matters

Great Lakes tribal and conservation organizations document manoomin as both food and cultural relationship. Modern household preparations include savory and sweet uses; this porridge is a contemporary home application of those ingredients.

This is a modern home preparation centered on manoomin, not a ceremonial or universal Ojibwe recipe.

Before you begin

Preparation notes

Use true wild rice

Cultivated blends cook differently.

Rinse first

It removes surface dust.

Add berries late

They should soften without disappearing.

Cook it

Ingredients & detailed method

Ingredients

  • 1 cup wild rice
  • 4 cups water
  • 1/2 tsp salt
  • 1/2 cup dried cranberries or blueberries
  • 2 tbsp maple syrup
  • 1/2 cup chopped walnuts, optional

Method

  1. 1.Rinse wild rice well.
  2. 2.Simmer rice with water and salt 35–45 minutes.
  3. 3.Checkpoint: grains should split and curl while remaining pleasantly chewy.
  4. 4.Drain excess water if needed.
  5. 5.Stir in dried berries and rest 5 minutes.
  6. 6.Add maple syrup to taste.
  7. 7.Serve warm with optional walnuts.
