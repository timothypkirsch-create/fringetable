Ghana / West Africa

Ghanaian Kelewele

Street Food / Snack · 35 min · 4 servings

Ripe plantain turns sweet, gingery and chile-hot when a thin spice paste clings to the surface and cooks into dark caramelized edges.

Fringe Table adaptation

About this dish

Kelewele is a Ghanaian plantain snack strongly associated with roadside and evening street-food vending. A University of Ghana handbook describes very ripe plantain cubes seasoned with ginger, onion, garlic, salt and pepper, cooked until golden with dark-brown edges and commonly served with peanuts. Ghanaian formulas vary in warm spices, but ginger and chile are core flavors.

Story & history

Why this dish matters

The Ghana Tourism Authority has presented kelewele as part of Ghana's national food culture, while a 2026 Ghana News Agency feature describes its strong presence in southern Ghana and on the streets of Accra, Kumasi and Cape Coast. The dish balances ripe plantain sweetness with ginger, hot pepper and aromatic spice. Vendors usually cook small batches so the plantain browns rather than steams, then serve it immediately with roasted groundnuts.

Kelewele seasoning differs by vendor and household. This version follows the University of Ghana's ginger-and-pepper structure and uses restrained clove and nutmeg documented in contemporary Ghanaian preparations; it is not presented as one universal spice formula.

Before you begin

Preparation notes

Choose ripe but firm plantains

Yellow skins with black spotting give sweetness, but the fruit should still hold its shape when cut.

Keep the spice paste thick

Use only enough water to help the ginger, onion and chile coat the plantain.

Cook in batches

Crowding lowers the oil temperature and makes the plantain greasy instead of caramelized.

Cook it

Ingredients & detailed method

Ingredients

  • 4 large ripe plantains, yellow with black spots but still firm
  • 2 tbsp finely grated fresh ginger
  • 2 tbsp finely grated or pureed onion
  • 2 garlic cloves, finely grated
  • 1 tsp cayenne pepper, or less to taste
  • 1/4 tsp ground cloves
  • 1/4 tsp ground nutmeg
  • 1 tsp kosher salt
  • 1 tbsp water, plus up to 1 tbsp more only if needed
  • Neutral oil for cooking
  • 1/2 cup roasted unsalted groundnuts or peanuts, for serving

Method

  1. 1.Peel the plantains and cut into 1-inch cubes or thick diagonal chunks. The pieces should be ripe and slightly yielding but not collapsing.
  2. 2.Mix ginger, onion, garlic, cayenne, cloves, nutmeg, salt and 1 tablespoon water into a thick, fragrant paste. Add a second tablespoon only if needed.
  3. 3.Toss plantain gently with the spice paste until evenly coated. Rest 10-15 minutes.
  4. 4.Heat enough neutral oil for shallow or deep cooking to about 350-360°F; the oil should shimmer without smoking.
  5. 5.Cook the plantain in 2 or 3 uncrowded batches for 3-5 minutes, turning so all sides color evenly.
  6. 6.Checkpoint: finished kelewele should be deep golden to mahogany at the edges, smell strongly of ginger and warm spice, and feel lightly crisp outside while the center is soft and almost jammy.
  7. 7.Drain briefly on a rack or absorbent paper and check the first batch for salt and chile before cooking the rest.
  8. 8.Serve immediately with roasted groundnuts or peanuts; the best crisp-caramelized edge fades as the plantain sits.
