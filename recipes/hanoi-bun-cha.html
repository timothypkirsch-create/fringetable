Hanoi / Vietnam

Hanoi Bún Chả

Main · 1 hr 30 min · 4 servings

Charred pork patties and slices, cool rice vermicelli, fresh herbs and a warm sweet-sour fish-sauce broth served in distinct components.

About this dish

A composed Hanoi lunch, not one mixed noodle bowl

Bún chả is closely associated with Hanoi. Grilled pork patties and slices are served in a warm, diluted fish-sauce dipping broth, while rice vermicelli and herbs arrive separately. The pleasure comes from combining a little of each as you eat: smoky pork, cool noodles, crisp herbs, pickles and warm broth.

Story & history

A dish strongly identified with Hanoi

Vietnam’s national tourism authorities repeatedly describe bún chả as a signature Hanoian dish and traditionally a lunch food. Official tourism sources emphasize charcoal-grilled pork, rice vermicelli, fresh herbs and a lightly sweet-sour fish-sauce broth, with diners adding noodles and greens to the broth bowl as they go.

Marinades, pickle vegetables and dipping-broth ratios vary among cooks and shops. This home version preserves the serving structure and smoky grilled character while allowing a broiler when charcoal grilling is not practical.

Before you begin

Preparation notes

Make two pork textures

Ground-pork patties and sliced pork give the classic contrast. Flatten patties so they brown quickly without staying raw in the center.

Keep the broth light

This is a warm dipping broth, not straight fish sauce. It should be easy to sip between bites.

Chase real char

High heat matters. Look for browned edges and a smoky aroma without drying the pork into hard nuggets.

Cook it

Ingredients & detailed method

Ingredients

  • 8 oz ground pork
  • 8 oz pork belly or pork shoulder, thinly sliced
  • 1 small shallot, finely minced
  • 2 garlic cloves, minced
  • 1 tbsp fish sauce
  • 1 tbsp sugar
  • 1 tsp neutral oil
  • 1/2 tsp black pepper
  • 8 oz dried rice vermicelli noodles
  • 4 cups mixed lettuce, mint, cilantro and perilla if available
  • 1 small green papaya or 1 kohlrabi, thinly sliced
  • 1 small carrot, thinly sliced
  • 1/4 cup rice vinegar
  • 2 tbsp sugar, for quick pickles
  • 1 cup warm water
  • 1/4 cup fish sauce
  • 3 tbsp sugar, for dipping broth
  • 3 tbsp lime juice or rice vinegar, to taste
  • 1 garlic clove, finely minced, for broth
  • 1 small red chile, sliced, optional

Method

  1. 1.Season both pork mixtures. Mix ground pork with half the shallot, garlic, fish sauce, sugar, oil and pepper. Season the sliced pork with the remaining half. Rest 30 minutes in the refrigerator.
  2. 2.Quick-pickle the vegetables. Toss papaya and carrot with rice vinegar and 2 tablespoons sugar. Rest at least 20 minutes. Texture checkpoint: slices should bend more easily but still snap lightly when bitten.
  3. 3.Mix the dipping broth. Dissolve 3 tablespoons sugar in warm water, then add 1/4 cup fish sauce and 3 tablespoons lime juice or vinegar. Stir in garlic and chile. Taste: it should be balanced and light enough to sip, not aggressively salty.
  4. 4.Shape and heat the grill. Form ground pork into eight small flat patties. Heat a charcoal or gas grill to high, or preheat a broiler with a rack 4–5 inches from the element.
  5. 5.Grill for char. Cook patties and sliced pork in a single layer until browned with dark caramelized edges. Turn once or twice. Temperature checkpoint: ground pork must reach 160°F; sliced pork should be cooked through and still juicy.
  6. 6.Cook and cool the noodles. Prepare vermicelli according to package directions. Rinse under cool water and drain thoroughly. Texture checkpoint: strands should be tender, separate and springy, not sticky.
  7. 7.Warm the broth. Heat the dipping broth just until pleasantly hot; do not boil it hard. Divide among bowls, then add drained pickles and grilled pork.
  8. 8.Serve in components. Put noodles and herbs on separate plates. Add small bundles of noodles and herbs to the pork-and-broth bowl as you eat so each bite keeps the intended contrast of hot, cool, smoky, crisp and tangy.
Substitutions

Practical swaps

No green papaya: kohlrabi is a practical crisp substitute. Cucumber is softer and changes the texture more noticeably.

No charcoal grill: a very hot broiler gives useful browning. A grill pan works, but it produces less smoke and therefore a less characteristic aroma.

No perilla: use extra mint, cilantro and lettuce. Do not let the missing herb stop you from making the dish, but perilla adds a distinctive herbal note when available.
