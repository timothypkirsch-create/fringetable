Somali Sambuus (Beef Sambusa)

Fried Pastry / Snack · 1 hr 30 min · 16 sambuus

These crisp Somali sambuus are built around a dry, highly seasoned beef filling and a thin wrapper that fries quickly without turning greasy.

Fringe Table adaptation

About this dish

Somali writer Ifrah F. Ahmed describes sambuus as celebration food strongly associated with weddings, Ramadan and Eid, while also placing it within a broader East African and Middle Eastern family of filled pastries shaped by centuries of trade and migration. PBS documentation of Somali cooks making sambusa likewise emphasizes the thin handmade wrapper, finely broken-up meat filling and the importance of the triangular fold. This home version keeps that structure and uses a beef filling seasoned with garlic, onion, chile, coriander and cumin.

Story & history

Why this dish matters

Sambuus should not be reduced to a generic samosa with a Somali label. Somali families have their own wrapper techniques, fillings, spice preferences and serving customs, and Ahmed writes about the pastry as a carrier of memory, migration and community identity. Ramadan is an especially important context: sambuus often appears when the daily fast is broken, but it also belongs at weddings, family gatherings and tea tables. Diaspora kitchens may use tortillas or commercial wrappers for convenience; this recipe instead follows the thin flour-wrapper tradition while keeping the method manageable at home.

Beef is common, but Somali sambuus may also be filled with lamb, fish, vegetables or other household fillings. A commercial spring-roll wrapper is a practical substitute, but it produces a different texture from the layered handmade wrapper described by Somali cooks. Keep the filling dry and cool before wrapping; excess liquid is the main cause of splitting and greasy pastry.

Before you begin

Preparation notes

Cook the filling dry

Continue cooking after the beef is browned until no watery liquid collects in the pan. A dry filling protects the wrapper and keeps the finished pastry crisp.

Cool completely before wrapping

Warm filling softens the dough and creates steam pockets that can burst in hot oil.

Seal every edge

Use a flour-and-water paste and inspect each triangle for gaps before frying. Even a small opening can leak filling into the oil.

Cook it

Ingredients & detailed method

Ingredients

  • 2 cups all-purpose flour
  • 1/2 tsp kosher salt
  • 2 tbsp neutral oil
  • 3/4 cup warm water, plus more as needed
  • 2 tbsp all-purpose flour mixed with 2 tbsp water, for sealing paste
  • 1 lb lean ground beef
  • 1 tbsp neutral oil
  • 1 medium yellow onion, finely diced
  • 3 garlic cloves, minced
  • 1 tsp finely grated fresh ginger
  • 1 tsp ground coriander
  • 1 tsp ground cumin
  • 1/2 tsp freshly ground black pepper
  • 1 small green chile, finely minced
  • 1/2 green bell pepper, very finely diced
  • 3 scallions, thinly sliced
  • 1/3 cup chopped fresh cilantro
  • 1 tsp kosher salt, plus more to taste
  • Neutral high-heat oil, for frying
  • Lemon wedges or green basbaas-style chile sauce, for serving

Method

  1. 1.Mix the flour and 1/2 teaspoon salt. Add 2 tablespoons oil and most of the warm water, then knead 6 to 8 minutes until the dough is smooth, soft and no longer shaggy. Add the remaining water only if the dough feels stiff.
  2. 2.Cover the dough and rest 25 minutes. Checkpoint: after resting, it should roll thin without snapping back aggressively and should feel supple rather than sticky.
  3. 3.Heat 1 tablespoon oil in a wide skillet over medium-high heat. Add the beef and break it into very fine crumbles. Cook 5 to 7 minutes until no pink remains and the pan begins to sound like frying rather than simmering.
  4. 4.Add onion, garlic, ginger, coriander, cumin, black pepper, chile and bell pepper. Cook 5 to 7 minutes more, stirring often, until the onion is soft and any released liquid has evaporated.
  5. 5.Checkpoint: drag a spoon through the filling. The pan should stay visibly dry for several seconds, the beef should smell toasted and warmly spiced, and there should be no puddle collecting around the meat.
  6. 6.Remove from the heat, fold in scallions and cilantro, season with 1 teaspoon salt and spread the filling on a tray to cool completely.
  7. 7.Divide the dough into 8 balls. Roll each ball into a very thin 7-inch round. Lightly oil one round, dust with flour, top with a second round and roll the pair together slightly thinner. Repeat to make 4 paired rounds.
  8. 8.Dry-cook each paired round in a hot skillet for about 20 to 30 seconds per side, just until the surfaces set but do not brown deeply. Separate the two layers while still warm, then cut each circle in half to make 16 semicircular wrappers.
  9. 9.Form each semicircle into a cone, sealing the overlapping edge with flour paste. Add about 2 tablespoons cooled filling, leaving enough empty wrapper to close the top. Paint the final edge with paste and press firmly into a triangle.
  10. 10.Heat 2 inches of oil to 350°F. Fry 3 to 4 sambuus at a time for 3 to 4 minutes, turning once, until evenly deep golden and visibly blistered.
  11. 11.Checkpoint: maintain roughly 340°F to 355°F. If the pastries darken in under 2 minutes the oil is too hot; if they sit pale and absorb oil, let the temperature recover before the next batch.
  12. 12.Drain on a rack rather than stacking. Rest 3 minutes before serving so the crust stays crisp and the beef filling is hot but not scalding.
