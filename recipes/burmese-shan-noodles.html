Shan State, Myanmar / Southeast Asia

Shan Noodles

Noodles / Main · 55 min · 4 servings

A lighter Myanmar noodle bowl where tomato, rice noodles and crunchy-sour toppings do most of the work.

Fringe Table adaptation

About this dish

Shan noodles, or Shan khauk swè, come from Shan State in eastern Myanmar and are now eaten widely beyond the region. A common dry style coats rice noodles with a tomato-based chicken or pork sauce and finishes the bowl with peanuts, herbs, chile and pickled mustard greens; broth may be served alongside.

Story & history

Why this dish matters

Shan cooking has its own regional identity inside Myanmar, and the noodle dish has spread through teashops, markets and city restaurants. Sources focused on Myanmar food distinguish the dry noodle bowl from coconut-rich ohn no khao swe and fish-broth mohinga. Fermented soybean products can be important in Shan versions, while urban adaptations often lean more heavily on tomato.

Shan noodle preparations vary across Shan State and in cities throughout Myanmar. This home version uses accessible ingredients and does not claim to replace versions made with regional fermented soybean products.

Before you begin

Preparation notes

Use rice noodles

Medium-width rice noodles give the right soft, slippery base without the richness of egg noodles.

Cook tomato until jammy

The sauce should cling to the noodles rather than run to the bottom of the bowl.

Do not skip something pickled

Pickled mustard greens provide the sour-salty contrast that keeps the bowl lively.

Cook it

Ingredients & detailed method

Ingredients

  • 12 oz medium rice noodles
  • 1 lb ground or finely chopped chicken thigh
  • 2 tbsp neutral oil
  • 4 garlic cloves, minced
  • 1 tbsp grated ginger
  • 3 medium ripe tomatoes, finely chopped
  • 1 tbsp fish sauce
  • 1 tbsp light soy sauce
  • 1 tsp paprika or mild chile powder
  • 1/2 cup chicken stock or water
  • 1/2 cup roasted peanuts, crushed
  • 1/2 cup chopped pickled mustard greens
  • 3 scallions, thinly sliced
  • 1/2 cup cilantro leaves
  • Garlic oil or crisp fried garlic, for serving
  • Chile oil, for serving
  • Lime wedges, optional

Method

  1. 1.Prepare all toppings before cooking the noodles so the finished bowls can be assembled immediately.
  2. 2.Heat oil in a skillet over medium heat. Add garlic and ginger and cook 30 seconds until fragrant.
  3. 3.Add chicken and cook 4–5 minutes, breaking it into small pieces until no raw color remains.
  4. 4.Add tomatoes, fish sauce, soy sauce and paprika. Cook 10–12 minutes, stirring often. Checkpoint: tomatoes should collapse into a thick, glossy sauce that clings to the chicken.
  5. 5.Add stock and simmer 5 minutes. The sauce should be loose enough to coat noodles but not soupy.
  6. 6.Cook rice noodles according to package directions until tender but resilient. Drain thoroughly.
  7. 7.Toss warm noodles with enough chicken-tomato sauce to coat each strand.
  8. 8.Divide among bowls and top with peanuts, pickled mustard greens, scallions, cilantro, garlic oil and chile oil.
  9. 9.Serve immediately, with lime if desired and a small bowl of clear broth alongside if you want the common dry-style presentation.
