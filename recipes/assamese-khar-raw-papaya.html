Assam, India / South Asia

Assamese Khar with Raw Papaya

Starter / Side · 40 min · 4 servings

Raw papaya meets the alkaline seasoning that is so characteristic of Assamese cooking that a traditional meal often begins with khar.

Fringe Table adaptation

About this dish

Khar is both an Assamese alkaline ingredient and the category of dishes prepared with it. Assam Tourism describes kola khar as liquid filtered through ashes of banana stem or peel and notes that khar dishes traditionally begin a meal. Raw papaya is one classic partner, though vegetables, pulses and fish can all appear in khar preparations.

Story & history

Why this dish matters

Assam Tourism calls khar integral enough to Assamese cuisine that the phrase Khar Khuwa Asomiya—roughly, khar-eating Assamese—is used culturally. Its alkaline cooking medium is historically produced from banana-plant ash. Because home ash chemistry can vary unpredictably, this recipe does not ask unfamiliar cooks to improvise ash lye; it recommends a commercially prepared food-grade kola khar or a carefully measured baking-soda fallback while clearly noting that the substitute changes the result.

Do not improvise concentrated ash lye without experienced, food-specific guidance. Food-grade kola khar is preferred. A tiny amount of baking soda is only a practical substitute and is not culturally or chemically identical.

Before you begin

Preparation notes

Use green raw papaya

Ripe fruit is too sweet and soft for this savory preparation.

Measure alkalinity cautiously

More khar is not better; excess creates harsh soapiness.

Do not chase health claims

Assam Tourism records traditional beliefs about khar, but this page presents it as food, not medicine.

Cook it

Ingredients & detailed method

Ingredients

  • 1 1/2 lb green raw papaya, peeled, seeded and cut into 1-inch pieces
  • 1 tbsp mustard oil
  • 1/2 tsp mustard seeds
  • 1 dried red chile
  • 1/2 tsp turmeric
  • 1 cup water
  • 1 to 2 tsp food-grade kola khar, to taste, or no more than 1/8 tsp baking soda as a fallback
  • 3/4 tsp kosher salt, plus more to taste

Method

  1. 1.Heat mustard oil in a saucepan until shimmering. Add mustard seeds and dried chile and let the seeds crackle.
  2. 2.Add papaya and turmeric and toss 2 minutes.
  3. 3.Add water and salt, cover and simmer 15–18 minutes until papaya is nearly tender.
  4. 4.Stir in 1 teaspoon food-grade kola khar and simmer uncovered 3 minutes. If using the baking-soda fallback, use only 1/8 teaspoon.
  5. 5.Checkpoint: papaya should be soft but intact and the broth should have a subtle characteristic alkaline edge, never a strong soapy taste.
  6. 6.Taste before adding any additional kola khar. Reduce any excess liquid to a light coating consistency.
  7. 7.Serve warm near the beginning of an Assamese-style meal.
