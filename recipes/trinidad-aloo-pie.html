Trinidad Aloo Pie

Street Food / Snack · 1 hr 20 min · 8 pies

A soft fried pocket of cumin-scented potato is one of Trinidad's everyday Indo-Caribbean street foods, especially good cut open while hot and dressed with chutney, pepper sauce or channa.

Fringe Table adaptation

About this dish

Trinidad's official tourism site includes aloo pie among the country's local street-food favorites and describes it as a deep-fried pastry filled with seasoned mashed potatoes. Trinidad-born CaribbeanPot cook Chris De La Rosa documents a family method of mashed potato flavored with garlic, hot pepper and geera, enclosed in a simple rested dough and fried until golden. Vendors may sell the pie plain or slice it open and add curried channa, tamarind chutney and pepper sauce after frying.

Story & history

Why this dish matters

Aloo pie belongs specifically to Trinidad and Tobago's Indo-Caribbean food culture. The Hindi word aloo means potato, reflecting the language and food knowledge Indian indentured migrants carried to Trinidad beginning in the nineteenth century. Those traditions developed locally into a street-food repertoire that includes doubles, pholourie, saheena and aloo pie. It is tempting to call the pie simply a 'large samosa,' but the local form has its own soft fried dough, elongated shape, potato seasoning and vendor serving style. Contemporary sources also note that Debe in southern Trinidad is especially associated with stalls selling this family of Indo-Trinidadian snacks.

Geera, the Trinidadian term for cumin, is a defining seasoning in many versions and should not be omitted without acknowledging the flavor change. Chadon beni (culantro) or green seasoning can be added, but they are not mandatory in every family recipe. Curried channa belongs on or inside the pie after frying in a common street-vendor presentation; it should not be confused with the core potato filling. Baking creates a different pastry texture and is not presented here as equivalent to the fried street-food form.

Before you begin

Preparation notes

Mash the potato completely smooth

Hard potato lumps can puncture the dough as you roll and seal it. Mash while hot, then cool the filling before assembly.

Toast whole geera if you can

A short dry toast until cumin smells nutty makes the filling more aromatic. Grind it after cooling or use ground cumin as a practical substitute.

Do not overfill

Leave a clean border around the potato so the seal contains no grease or filling; otherwise the pie can open in the fryer.

Cook it

Ingredients & detailed method

Ingredients

  • 2 1/2 cups all-purpose flour, plus more for dusting
  • 2 tsp baking powder
  • 1 tsp instant yeast
  • 1 tsp light brown sugar
  • 3/4 tsp kosher salt
  • 1 tbsp neutral oil, plus more to coat the dough
  • 1 to 1 1/4 cups warm water, as needed
  • 1 1/2 lb Yukon Gold or russet potatoes, peeled and cut into chunks
  • 3 garlic cloves, finely grated
  • 1 small hot chile or 1/2 Scotch bonnet, minced to taste
  • 1 1/2 tsp cumin seeds, toasted and ground, or 1 tsp ground cumin (geera)
  • 2 tbsp finely chopped chadon beni (culantro) or cilantro, optional
  • 3/4 tsp kosher salt, plus more to taste for the filling
  • Neutral high-heat oil, for deep-frying
  • Tamarind or mango chutney, for serving
  • Trinidad pepper sauce, optional for serving
  • Curried channa, optional for splitting and filling the cooked pies

Method

  1. 1.Whisk the flour, baking powder, yeast, sugar and 3/4 teaspoon salt in a bowl. Add 1 tablespoon oil and 1 cup warm water, mixing until a soft dough forms; add the remaining water a tablespoon at a time only as needed.
  2. 2.Knead 5 to 7 minutes until smooth and supple. Rub with a thin film of oil, cover and rest 25 to 30 minutes. Checkpoint: the dough should relax enough that a pressed indentation springs back slowly rather than snapping flat immediately.
  3. 3.While the dough rests, boil the potatoes in salted water 15 to 20 minutes until a knife slides through without resistance. Drain well and let steam dry for 2 minutes.
  4. 4.Mash the potatoes completely smooth with garlic, chile, toasted ground cumin, optional chadon beni and 3/4 teaspoon salt. Taste while warm, then spread the filling on a plate to cool until it no longer sends up steam.
  5. 5.Checkpoint: the filling should smell strongly of geera and garlic and hold together as a soft mound; visible hard potato chunks mean it needs more mashing before it goes into the dough.
  6. 6.Divide the rested dough into 8 equal balls. On a lightly floured surface roll each into an oval about 6 inches long and 1/8 inch thick.
  7. 7.Place about 1/3 cup cooled potato filling along one half of each oval, leaving at least a 1/2-inch clean border. Fold the dough over, press out trapped air and pinch firmly closed; reinforce the seam with fork tines if needed.
  8. 8.Heat 2 to 3 inches neutral oil in a deep pot to 350°F. Fry one test pie first. It should bubble immediately but not turn dark brown within the first minute.
  9. 9.Fry the remaining pies in small batches for about 2 to 3 minutes per side, turning as needed. Keep the oil between 340°F and 355°F so the dough cooks through before the outside over-browns.
  10. 10.Checkpoint: each pie should be puffed and evenly golden, with a light crisp skin and soft breadlike layer beneath. If a cut test shows a gummy seam of raw dough around the potato, lower the heat slightly and extend the fry time.
  11. 11.Drain on a rack. Serve hot with tamarind or mango chutney and pepper sauce. For a common street-style presentation, slit the pie lengthwise without cutting through the bottom and spoon in warm curried channa before adding chutney.
