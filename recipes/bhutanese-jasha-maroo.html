Bhutan / Himalayas

Bhutanese Jasha Maroo

Stew / Main · 45 min · 4 servings

Chicken, tomato and a generous amount of fresh chile simmer into a bright Bhutanese stew whose broth is meant to run into a bowl of red rice.

Fringe Table adaptation

About this dish

Bhutan's official tourism site describes jasha maroo as a spicy chicken stew made from fresh diced chicken, chillies, onions, tomato and coriander leaves. Published home versions commonly add ginger, garlic and enough stock or water to keep the finished dish brothy rather than reducing it into a thick curry. This recipe stays with that light-stew structure.

Story & history

Why this dish matters

Chiles are central to Bhutanese cooking, where they often function as a vegetable rather than a token seasoning. Jasha maroo gives that preference a comparatively simple form: chicken and tomatoes provide body while ginger, garlic and fresh green chile keep the broth sharp and aromatic. It is commonly served with rice, especially Bhutanese red rice, which absorbs the broth and tempers the heat. Exact proportions and whether cooks use spring onion, leek or both vary from kitchen to kitchen.

This is a home-scale Bhutanese adaptation, not an attempt to dilute the dish into a generic South Asian chicken curry. The chile level may be reduced for comfort, but omitting chile entirely changes a defining feature. Use chicken thighs for reliable tenderness; boneless breast can be used but should be watched closely to avoid overcooking.

Before you begin

Preparation notes

Cut the chicken evenly

Pieces around 1 inch cook quickly and stay tender; mixed sizes make it hard to hit safe temperature without drying smaller pieces.

Use fresh chile deliberately

Green chiles provide both flavor and heat. Seed some rather than removing them all if you want a gentler pot.

Keep it brothy

Jasha maroo should have enough light tomato-chicken broth to spoon over rice; do not reduce it to a dry masala.

Cook it

Ingredients & detailed method

Ingredients

  • 1 1/2 lb boneless skinless chicken thighs, cut into 1-inch pieces
  • 2 tbsp neutral oil
  • 1 medium yellow onion, finely chopped
  • 4 garlic cloves, minced
  • 2 tbsp finely grated fresh ginger
  • 4 green chiles, sliced; seed 1 or 2 for less heat if needed
  • 2 large ripe tomatoes, diced
  • 4 scallions, white and green parts sliced
  • 1 cup low-sodium chicken stock or water
  • 1 tsp kosher salt, plus more to taste
  • 1/2 cup roughly chopped fresh cilantro
  • Cooked Bhutanese red rice or another plain rice, for serving

Method

  1. 1.Pat the chicken dry and season it lightly with about half the salt. Heat the oil in a wide heavy skillet or saucepan over medium-high heat until shimmering.
  2. 2.Add onion, garlic, ginger and half the sliced chiles. Cook 4-5 minutes, stirring often, until the onion softens and the ginger-garlic aroma loses its raw sharpness; do not brown the garlic.
  3. 3.Add the chicken and spread it into one layer. Cook 4-5 minutes, turning, until the exterior has changed from pink to opaque but the centers are not yet fully cooked.
  4. 4.Stir in tomatoes, scallion whites and remaining chiles. Cook 4-5 minutes until the tomatoes soften, release juice and begin to form a light sauce around the chicken.
  5. 5.Pour in the stock or water, bring just to a boil, then reduce to a lively simmer. Cover partially and cook 8-12 minutes.
  6. 6.Temperature checkpoint: test the largest chicken piece in the center of the pan. It must reach at least 165°F and should feel tender and juicy rather than rubbery. The broth should smell of ginger and fresh chile and remain loose enough to spoon over rice.
  7. 7.Taste the broth and add the remaining salt only as needed. Stir in scallion greens and most of the cilantro, then simmer uncovered 1 minute so the herbs stay fresh-smelling.
  8. 8.Serve hot over or beside red rice, spooning some of the chile-tomato broth over each portion. Garnish with the remaining cilantro.
