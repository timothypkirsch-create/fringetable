Azerbaijan / South Caucasus

Azerbaijani Meat Qutab

Flatbread / Street Food · 1 hr 10 min · 8 qutab

A thin unleavened dough folds around just enough meat to cook through on a blistering-hot saj or skillet.

Fringe Table adaptation

About this dish

Qutab is a widespread Azerbaijani folded flatbread or pastry cooked on a hot saj. Fillings include meat, herbs, pumpkin and offal. Meat qutab uses a very thin layer of minced lamb or beef with onion so the filling cooks in the short time needed to blister the dough.

Story & history

Why this dish matters

Azerbaijan’s official tourism site describes qutab as a portable street snack whose name is connected with the Azerbaijani verb for folding. Meat versions are commonly served with sumac or qatıq yogurt, and pomegranate can appear inside or alongside. The extreme thinness of both wrapper and filling is what makes the quick dry-pan cooking possible.

Regional qutab fillings and serving styles differ. This page follows Azerbaijan.travel’s published meat version rather than treating qutab as one fixed recipe.

Before you begin

Preparation notes

Roll almost paper-thin

Thick dough will stay chewy before the meat finishes.

Spread meat thinly

A dense mound cannot cook safely in the short griddle time.

Use a very hot dry pan

Traditional saj cooking is fast and relatively dry; a cast-iron skillet is the home substitute.

Cook it

Ingredients & detailed method

Ingredients

  • 2 1/2 cups all-purpose flour
  • 3/4 tsp kosher salt
  • 3/4 to 1 cup water
  • 12 oz ground lamb or beef
  • 1 small onion, grated and squeezed lightly
  • 1/2 tsp black pepper
  • 1/2 tsp salt for filling
  • 1/3 cup pomegranate seeds, optional
  • Melted butter, for brushing
  • Ground sumac and plain yogurt, for serving

Method

  1. 1.Mix flour and salt, then add water gradually and knead 6–8 minutes until smooth and firm. Cover and rest 30 minutes.
  2. 2.Mix meat with grated onion, pepper and filling salt.
  3. 3.Divide dough into 8 balls and roll each into a thin 8-inch circle, using flour sparingly.
  4. 4.Spread a very thin layer of meat across one half, leaving a narrow clean border. Add a few pomegranate seeds if using.
  5. 5.Fold the empty half over the filling and press the curved edge tightly closed, pushing out trapped air.
  6. 6.Cook on a very hot dry cast-iron skillet or griddle 2–3 minutes per side.
  7. 7.Checkpoint: dough should show dark toasted spots and filling should be fully cooked with no raw center. If qutab browns before meat cooks, the filling is too thick.
  8. 8.Brush lightly with butter and serve immediately with sumac and yogurt.
