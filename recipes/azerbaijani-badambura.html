Azerbaijan / South Caucasus

Azerbaijani Badambura

Pastry / Sweet · 2 hrs 45 min · 12 pastries

Badambura hides an almond-cardamom filling inside dozens of visible pastry layers, with a pale shell that is tender, flaky and fragrant rather than deeply browned.

Fringe Table adaptation

About this dish

Azerbaijan's national tourism site describes badambura as a popular traditional sweet served for Novruz, weddings and tea, and gives its defining structure as layered dough filled with almonds, sugar and cardamom. The same official recipe specifically instructs cooks to bake at a low enough temperature that the pastries stay pale. This scaled home version preserves that almond-cardamom center and the coiled laminated shape while reducing the very large festival proportions to a dozen pastries.

Story & history

Why this dish matters

Badambura belongs to Azerbaijan's broader culture of festive pastries, where nuts, cardamom, butter and careful shaping appear across sweets served with tea and during Novruz. It is visually distinct because stacked, buttered sheets are rolled into a log, sliced to expose concentric layers, then opened into a small cup for filling. The target is not croissant-like expansion or a dark bakery crust; the pastry is traditionally kept comparatively pale so the layered pattern remains delicate and the almond-cardamom aroma stays central.

Walnuts or hazelnuts can make a pleasant filled pastry, but they change the specific almond profile described in Azerbaijan's tourism recipe and should not be presented as identical. Ground cardamom is essential to the traditional flavor. Do not increase the oven heat simply to force browning; a pale finish is part of the documented style.

Before you begin

Preparation notes

Roll the sheets very thin

The visible spiral depends on many thin layers. Aim for sheets thin enough to become slightly translucent without tearing.

Keep the butter soft, not hot

Melted-hot butter can soak into the dough instead of keeping layers distinct. Use softened or barely melted butter.

Watch color closely

Badambura should bake through while remaining ivory to light gold. A dark brown surface means the oven is too hot or the pastries have baked too long.

Cook it

Ingredients & detailed method

Ingredients

  • 3 1/2 cups all-purpose flour, plus more for rolling
  • 2 1/4 tsp instant yeast
  • 2 tbsp granulated sugar
  • 1/2 tsp kosher salt
  • 3/4 cup sour cream
  • 1 large egg
  • 1/2 cup warm whole milk
  • 6 tbsp unsalted butter, softened, for the dough
  • 8 tbsp unsalted butter, very soft or barely melted, for layering
  • 1 1/2 cups finely ground blanched almonds
  • 3/4 cup granulated sugar
  • 1 1/2 tsp ground cardamom
  • Powdered sugar, for finishing

Method

  1. 1.Mix the flour, yeast, sugar and salt. Add sour cream, egg, warm milk and 6 tablespoons softened butter. Knead 8 to 10 minutes until smooth and elastic.
  2. 2.Cover and let rise 45 to 60 minutes in a warm place. Checkpoint: the dough should look visibly fuller and an indentation should spring back slowly, but it does not need to fully double.
  3. 3.Mix ground almonds, 3/4 cup sugar and cardamom. Rub a spoonful between your fingers; it should smell distinctly floral and nutty, with no large almond chunks that could tear the dough.
  4. 4.Divide the risen dough into 6 equal pieces. Roll each into a very thin rectangle, roughly 10 by 14 inches, keeping the work surface lightly floured.
  5. 5.Lay the first sheet flat and brush it lightly but completely with layering butter. Stack the second sheet on top and repeat until all 6 layers are stacked and buttered except the very top.
  6. 6.Roll the stacked sheets tightly from a long side into a compact log. Chill 20 minutes so the butter firms enough for clean slicing.
  7. 7.Cut the log crosswise into 12 equal rounds. Place each cut side up and gently press the center down with both thumbs while supporting the outer rings, opening it into a small layered cup without crushing the spiral.
  8. 8.Add about 2 tablespoons almond filling. Pinch the opening closed securely, then place seam-side down on a parchment-lined tray. The layered spiral should remain visible on top.
  9. 9.Bake at 325°F for 28 to 35 minutes, rotating the tray once. Checkpoint: the bottoms should be set and lightly golden, the tops should remain pale ivory to very light gold, and the pastry layers should feel dry rather than doughy when gently tapped.
  10. 10.Cool 10 minutes, then dust with powdered sugar. Let the pastries finish cooling before storing so trapped steam does not soften the layers.
