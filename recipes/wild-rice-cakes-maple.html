Wild Rice Cakes with Maple

Main / Side · 45 min · 8 cakes

Cooked wild rice binds with cornmeal into crisp-edged cakes that keep manoomin's nutty chew at the center.

Fringe Table adaptation

About this dish

This is explicitly a modern home recipe inspired by documented Great Lakes Indigenous ingredients—wild rice, corn and maple—not a claim to reproduce a specific ceremonial or historic dish. The cakes are pan-seared so the outside crisps while the wild rice remains distinct inside.

Story & history

Why this dish matters

Manoomin has deep cultural significance across Ojibwe and other Great Lakes communities. Maple sugaring and corn also have long regional histories. Combining them in a modern cake can be respectful only when that modern framing is stated clearly.

This is a contemporary Fringe Table adaptation using culturally important regional ingredients, not a traditional community recipe.

Before you begin

Preparation notes

Cool the rice

Warm rice makes the mixture loose.

Do not overbind

The cakes should still taste like wild rice.

Use maple lightly

It is a finish, not a dessert glaze.

Cook it

Ingredients & detailed method

Ingredients

  • 2 cups cooked wild rice, cooled
  • 1/2 cup fine cornmeal
  • 2 eggs
  • 3 scallions, chopped
  • 1/2 tsp salt
  • 2 tbsp sunflower oil
  • 1 tbsp maple syrup

Method

  1. 1.Combine wild rice, cornmeal, eggs, scallions and salt.
  2. 2.Rest mixture 10 minutes so cornmeal hydrates.
  3. 3.Form 8 compact cakes.
  4. 4.Heat oil in a skillet and cook cakes 4–5 minutes per side.
  5. 5.Checkpoint: cakes should hold together with deeply browned edges and a tender, chewy center.
  6. 6.Brush very lightly with maple syrup during the final minute.
  7. 7.Serve warm with vegetables or a bean dish.
