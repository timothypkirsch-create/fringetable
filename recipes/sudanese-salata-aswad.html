Sudan / Northeast Africa

Sudanese Salata Aswad

Side / Salad · 45 min · 6 servings

Charred eggplant is mashed with a sharp, creamy dressing and finished with sesame oil in this flexible Sudanese salad made for bread.

Fringe Table adaptation

About this dish

Salata aswad—literally “black salad,” referring to eggplant—is a Sudanese family of eggplant preparations. In salata aswad mahrousa, the eggplant is charred until collapsed, mashed to a deliberately uneven texture and balanced with garlic, lime, vinegar and a creamy seed or nut paste.

Sudanese Kitchen documents tahini and yogurt in the dressing, with peanut butter as a common alternative. This version gives both routes rather than treating them as interchangeable without explanation.

Story & history

Why this dish matters

Sudanese salata aswad shows how a dish can resemble eastern Mediterranean eggplant dips while developing a distinct Sudanese balance through lime, vinegar, sesame oil and, in many homes, peanut butter. Its identity is not simply “Sudanese baba ganoush.”

Charring supplies the smoke; the dressing supplies acidity and richness. The cook adjusts those elements after tasting because eggplant size, lime strength, tahini bitterness and peanut butter sweetness all vary.

Fried, boiled, oven-roasted and live-fire versions are documented. This charred version follows salata aswad mahrousa and retains a lumpy rather than fully puréed texture.

Before you begin

Preparation notes

Char beyond browning

The eggplants should deflate and feel soft from end to end.

Drain bitter liquid

A brief drain prevents watery separation without rinsing away the smoke.

Balance after mixing

Add lime, vinegar and salt incrementally once the creamy dressing meets the eggplant.

Cook it

Ingredients & detailed method

Ingredients

  • 3 medium eggplants (about 1 3/4 pounds)
  • 1 teaspoon fine salt, divided
  • 1 tablespoon tahini or smooth unsweetened peanut butter
  • 3 tablespoons water
  • 2 tablespoons fresh lime juice, plus more to taste
  • 2 teaspoons malt or cider vinegar
  • 1 tablespoon plain thick yogurt, optional
  • 1 garlic clove, finely grated
  • 1/2 teaspoon freshly ground black pepper
  • 2 tablespoons chopped parsley
  • 2 tablespoons toasted sesame oil, for finishing
  • Flatbread, for serving

Method

  1. 1.Prick each eggplant deeply in several places so steam can escape. Heat a grill, broiler or gas burner for direct charring.
  2. 2.Cook the whole eggplants, turning every 2–3 minutes, until blackened over most of the surface and collapsed, 15–25 minutes depending on the heat.
  3. 3.Checkpoint: a skewer should pass through the thickest part with no resistance. If the center remains springy, continue cooking even if the skin is dark.
  4. 4.Rest until cool enough to handle, then peel away the charred skin. Split, remove any large hard seed pockets and drain the flesh in a sieve for 5 minutes.
  5. 5.Mash the eggplant coarsely with 1/2 teaspoon salt. Leave small pieces rather than blending it completely smooth.
  6. 6.Whisk tahini or peanut butter with water, lime juice, vinegar, optional yogurt, garlic, pepper and the remaining salt until creamy.
  7. 7.Fold the dressing into the eggplant. Taste and adjust lime, vinegar and salt; the result should be smoky, tangy and rich without one element dominating.
  8. 8.Checkpoint: the salad should sit in a soft mound with no watery ring. If loose, rest 10 minutes; if stiff, stir in water 1 teaspoon at a time.
  9. 9.Spoon into a shallow bowl, scatter with parsley and finish with sesame oil. Serve warm or at room temperature with bread.
