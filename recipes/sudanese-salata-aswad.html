Sudan / Northeast Africa

Sudanese Salata Aswad

Side / Salad · 45 min · 6 servings

Soft eggplant and peanut paste meet tomato, citrus and chile in a distinctly Sudanese balance of smoke, richness and acidity.

Fringe Table adaptation

About this dish

Salata aswad is a Sudanese eggplant salad commonly combining cooked eggplant with peanut butter or ground peanuts, tomato, lemon and chile. It can be smooth or rustic and is often served with flatbread as part of a larger meal.

Story & history

Why this dish matters

Groundnuts are important across Sudanese cooking, and their use in salata aswad gives the eggplant unusual body and richness. Household versions vary in whether eggplant is fried, roasted or boiled.

This version roasts the eggplant to reduce oil use while preserving the smoky character.

Before you begin

Preparation notes

Roast until collapsed

Firm eggplant will not mash properly.

Use unsweetened peanut butter

Sweetened spreads distort the balance.

Add lemon gradually

The salad should be bright, not sour.

Cook it

Ingredients & detailed method

Ingredients

  • 2 large eggplants
  • 2 tbsp peanut butter
  • 2 tomatoes, chopped
  • 2 garlic cloves
  • 2 tbsp lemon juice
  • 1 small chile
  • 1 tsp salt
  • 2 tbsp sesame or olive oil

Method

  1. 1.Roast eggplants at 425°F until collapsed and deeply soft.
  2. 2.Scoop flesh and mash coarsely.
  3. 3.Cook tomatoes and garlic in oil until jammy.
  4. 4.Stir tomato mixture and peanut butter into eggplant.
  5. 5.Add chile, lemon and salt.
  6. 6.Checkpoint: salad should be thick, spoonable and glossy with no watery separation.
  7. 7.Serve warm or room temperature with bread.
