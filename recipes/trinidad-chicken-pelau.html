Trinidad Chicken Pelau

Rice / Main · 1 hr 25 min · 8 servings

Brown sugar becomes nearly black caramel before chicken hits the pot—the signature first move in Trinidad pelau.

Fringe Table adaptation

About this dish

Pelau is a Trinidad and Tobago one-pot rice dish in which seasoned chicken is browned in deeply caramelized sugar before rice, pigeon peas, coconut milk and vegetables cook together. The browned sugar contributes color and a bittersweet depth; the finished dish should not taste sugary.

Story & history

Why this dish matters

Tourism Trinidad lists pelau among the traditional foods that express the island’s layered culinary heritage. Foodie Nation’s Trinidad method emphasizes the crucial browning stage, where sugar reaches a dark amber ‘black jack’ color before seasoned chicken is added. Rice then parches briefly with the chicken before pigeon peas, aromatics, coconut and vegetables complete the pot.

Pelau varies in meat, peas, vegetables and moisture. This chicken version follows a common Trinidad structure and keeps the final rice moist but not wet.

Before you begin

Preparation notes

Watch the sugar continuously

Caramel goes from deep brown to burnt very quickly; have seasoned chicken ready before you start.

Use a heavy pot

Even heat gives you more control over browning and reduces scorching during the rice stage.

Finish by texture

Pelau can be drier or wetter by household; stop when rice is tender and the pot is cohesive, not soupy.

Cook it

Ingredients & detailed method

Ingredients

  • 2 lb chicken thighs and drumsticks, cut into pieces and seasoned with green seasoning
  • 2 tbsp neutral oil
  • 1/3 cup brown sugar
  • 2 cups long-grain rice, rinsed
  • 1 can (15 oz) pigeon peas, drained
  • 4 garlic cloves, minced
  • 1/2 cup chopped onion
  • 4 pimento peppers or 1 small sweet pepper, chopped
  • 1 cup diced pumpkin
  • 1 cup coconut milk
  • 3 cups water or stock, approximately
  • 1 whole Scotch bonnet
  • 1 carrot, diced
  • 1/3 cup chopped culantro or cilantro
  • 1 tsp kosher salt, plus more to taste

Method

  1. 1.Heat oil in a heavy pot over medium-high heat. Add brown sugar in an even layer and watch until it melts, bubbles and reaches very dark amber.
  2. 2.Immediately add seasoned chicken and turn vigorously to coat in caramel. Brown 8–10 minutes.
  3. 3.Add rice and stir 3–4 minutes so grains toast lightly in the chicken and caramel.
  4. 4.Add pigeon peas, garlic, onion, pimento pepper and pumpkin. Stir well.
  5. 5.Add coconut milk and enough water or stock to sit just above the rice. Add whole Scotch bonnet and salt.
  6. 6.Cover and simmer over low heat 20 minutes, then add carrot and stir once.
  7. 7.Continue covered or partially covered until rice is tender, adding small amounts of hot water only if needed.
  8. 8.Checkpoint: grains should be cooked through, chicken tender and the pot moist enough to hold together without free liquid pooling. Fold in culantro, remove whole chile and rest 10 minutes before serving.
