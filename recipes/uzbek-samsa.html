Uzbekistan / Central Asia

Uzbek Samsa

Pastry / Main · 2 hrs · 12 pastries

A crisp laminated shell encloses a juicy onion-heavy lamb filling inspired by tandoor-baked Uzbek samsa.

Fringe Table adaptation

About this dish

Samsa are baked pastries found across Central Asia, with Uzbek versions commonly filled with lamb and abundant onion and baked against the walls of a tandir oven. A home oven cannot duplicate that environment exactly, but high heat and a well-chilled dough get close to the intended crisp shell and juicy center.

Story & history

Why this dish matters

Uzbek samsa are everyday bakery and market food as well as a home preparation. Shape and dough vary by region, from layered round pastries to triangular forms. This recipe uses a rolled butter layer to mimic flaky laminated dough.

This is a home-oven adaptation; tandir-baked samsa develop a different crust and aroma.

Before you begin

Preparation notes

Chop meat

Hand-cut lamb stays juicier than very fine mince.

Use plenty of onion

It supplies moisture to the filling.

Bake very hot

Lower heat dries the filling before the crust colors.

Cook it

Ingredients & detailed method

Ingredients

  • 3 cups flour
  • 1 tsp salt
  • 1 cup water
  • 5 tbsp melted butter
  • 1 lb lamb, finely chopped
  • 2 onions, finely chopped
  • 1 tsp cumin seeds
  • 1 tsp salt
  • 1/2 tsp pepper
  • 1 egg for brushing
  • Sesame seeds

Method

  1. 1.Mix flour, salt and water into a firm dough; rest 20 minutes.
  2. 2.Roll thin, brush with butter, roll into a log and chill 30 minutes.
  3. 3.Mix lamb, onion, cumin, salt and pepper.
  4. 4.Slice dough log into 12 pieces and roll into rounds.
  5. 5.Fill and seal into triangles or rounds.
  6. 6.Brush with egg, sprinkle sesame and bake at 450°F for 25–30 minutes.
  7. 7.Checkpoint: crust should be deeply browned and lamb filling at least 160°F.
