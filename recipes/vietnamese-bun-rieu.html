Vietnamese Bún Riêu Cua

Noodle Soup / Main · 1 hr 30 min · 6 servings

Tomatoes tint the broth red-orange while delicate crab-and-egg curds float above rice vermicelli, tofu and a pile of fresh herbs and greens.

Fringe Table adaptation

About this dish

Bún riêu cua is a Vietnamese rice-vermicelli soup defined by tomato-rich broth and riêu: soft crab curds traditionally made from freshwater paddy crabs that are pounded and strained. Because whole Vietnamese rice-field crabs are difficult to source in the United States, this home adaptation follows Vietnamese chef approaches that combine cooked crabmeat with prepared crab paste and egg to reproduce the soft curd texture while clearly acknowledging the substitution.

Story & history

Why this dish matters

Food writers and Vietnamese chefs associate bún riêu strongly with northern Vietnam and the Red River Delta, where small freshwater crabs from rice fields supplied both flavor and the characteristic floating riêu. The soup later spread throughout Vietnam and developed regional variations, especially in the quantity and type of toppings. Tomatoes bring acidity and color; fried tofu absorbs the broth; fermented shrimp paste, fresh herbs, water spinach and banana blossom sharpen and freshen the bowl. The dish's identity lies in that balance rather than in a single overloaded restaurant garnish list.

Freshwater paddy crab is the traditional defining ingredient. This recipe uses cooked crabmeat plus prepared Vietnamese crab paste with soybean oil as a practical diaspora-style substitute, which changes the broth's exact flavor. Do not present the shortcut as identical to a bowl made from freshly pounded cua đồng.

Before you begin

Preparation notes

Keep the crab curd gentle

Once the riêu mixture enters the broth, maintain a bare simmer. A hard boil breaks the soft curds into grainy fragments.

Cook noodles separately

Rice vermicelli left in the soup continues absorbing broth. Cook, rinse and portion it into bowls just before serving.

Treat mắm tôm as a powerful seasoning

Fermented shrimp paste is characteristic but intense. Start with a small amount in the broth and offer more at the table rather than overwhelming the crab.

Prep herbs before the soup is done

Bún riêu is best when boiling-hot broth meets cool crisp vegetables and fragrant herbs at the moment of serving.

Cook it

Ingredients & detailed method

Ingredients

  • 10 oz dried medium rice vermicelli (bún)
  • 2 tbsp neutral oil
  • 1 small yellow onion, finely diced
  • 1 1/2 lb ripe tomatoes, cut into wedges
  • 6 cups unsalted chicken or pork stock
  • 1 tbsp fish sauce, plus more to taste
  • 1 to 2 tsp Vietnamese fermented shrimp paste (mắm tôm), to taste
  • 1 tsp tamarind concentrate or 1 tbsp fresh lime juice, added only if the tomatoes need more acidity
  • 8 oz cooked lump crabmeat, picked over for shell
  • 3 tbsp Vietnamese crab paste with soybean oil (gia vị cua or similar)
  • 3 large eggs
  • 1/2 tsp black pepper
  • 8 oz fried tofu puffs, halved
  • 2 cups bean sprouts
  • 2 cups finely shredded water spinach stems or green cabbage
  • 1 cup thinly sliced banana blossom, optional
  • 1 cup mixed Vietnamese balm, perilla, mint and cilantro leaves, or the best available combination
  • 2 limes, cut into wedges
  • Sliced fresh chile, for serving

Method

  1. 1.Cook the rice vermicelli according to the package directions until tender but still springy. Drain, rinse under cool water until the noodles stop steaming, and divide into six loose bundles so they do not clump.
  2. 2.Heat the oil in a soup pot over medium-high heat. Add the onion and cook 3 to 4 minutes until translucent. Add two-thirds of the tomato wedges and cook 6 to 8 minutes, stirring gently, until their edges soften and the oil turns orange-red while some wedge shape remains.
  3. 3.Add the stock, fish sauce and 1 teaspoon mắm tôm. Bring to a simmer for 15 minutes. Taste the broth: it should be savory, tomato-bright and gently funky; add tamarind only if the tomatoes do not provide enough tang.
  4. 4.While the broth simmers, gently fold the crabmeat, prepared crab paste, eggs and black pepper together. Do not whip; visible crab flakes should remain in the mixture.
  5. 5.Lower the broth to the gentlest possible simmer, about 190 to 200°F if checking with a thermometer. Spoon the crab mixture onto the surface in 2-tablespoon portions. Do not stir for 4 to 5 minutes while the curds set.
  6. 6.Checkpoint: the riêu should float in soft irregular islands that feel custardy but hold together when nudged. If the broth boils hard, lower the heat immediately so the curds do not shatter.
  7. 7.Add the fried tofu and remaining tomato wedges around the curds and simmer gently 8 to 10 minutes. The tofu should swell with broth and the fresh tomato wedges should soften while still looking distinct.
  8. 8.Taste the broth again and adjust fish sauce, mắm tôm or acidity in small increments. The aroma should lead with tomato and crab, with fermented shrimp paste in the background rather than dominating.
  9. 9.To serve, place a bundle of vermicelli in each bowl with bean sprouts, shredded water spinach or cabbage and optional banana blossom. Ladle boiling-hot broth, tomatoes, tofu and several crab curds over the noodles, then finish with herbs, lime and sliced chile at the table.
