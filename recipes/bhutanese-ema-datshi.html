Bhutan / Himalayas

Bhutanese Ema Datshi

Main · 25 min · Serves 4

Chiles treated as the main vegetable, simmered tender and enveloped in a simple cheese sauce.

Fringe Table adaptation

About this dish

In ema datshi, chiles are not a background spice: they are the vegetable. The name itself pairs ema, chile, with datshi, cheese. Bhutanese dairy products differ from U.S. supermarket cheeses, so this version combines mild farmer's cheese with a little feta to approximate a soft, tangy, salty sauce without claiming an exact substitute.

Serve with plenty of rice. If Bhutanese red rice is unavailable, the Steamed Basmati Rice Essential is a practical accompaniment.

Story & history

When chile becomes the vegetable

Ema datshi is widely described as one of Bhutan's defining foods. The most important conceptual shift for cooks outside Bhutan is the quantity of chile: Bhutanese cooking often treats chiles as a substantial ingredient rather than a cautious seasoning.

The dish also belongs to a larger family of datshi preparations that pair cheese with vegetables such as potatoes, mushrooms or other produce. That framework helps explain why the sauce is simple—the vegetables and cheese are meant to remain clearly recognizable.

Bhutanese cheeses, chile varieties and family proportions vary. This adaptation uses deliberately adjustable heat and accessible cheeses.

Before you begin

Preparation notes

Mix hot and mild chiles

You can preserve the chile-forward structure without making the dish punishingly hot. Long mild green chiles can provide volume while a few hot chiles provide character.

Cook the chiles fully

They should become tender enough to eat as a vegetable, not remain raw-crisp.

Do not boil the cheese hard

High heat can make accessible substitute cheeses tighten or split. Add them over low heat and stop once a loose sauce forms.

Cook it

Ingredients & detailed method

Ingredients

  • 10–12 long green chiles, using a mix of mild and hot varieties
  • 1 small onion, thinly sliced
  • 2 garlic cloves, sliced
  • 1 small tomato, chopped, optional
  • 1 1/2 cups water
  • 8 oz farmer's cheese, crumbled
  • 2 oz feta, crumbled
  • 1 tbsp butter
  • Kosher salt, only if needed
  • Steamed red or white rice, for serving

Method

  1. 1.Choose the heat level. Taste or identify your chile varieties before cooking. Use mostly mild chiles if necessary, but keep the total quantity generous.
  2. 2.Prepare the chiles. Slit them lengthwise. Remove some seeds only if you need to reduce heat; leaving the chile halves intact helps them remain recognizable.
  3. 3.Start the vegetables. Combine chiles, onion, garlic, optional tomato and water in a wide pan.
  4. 4.Simmer. Bring to a gentle simmer and cook 8–12 minutes. Checkpoint: the chiles should bend easily and lose their raw crunch while still holding their shape.
  5. 5.Lower the heat. Reduce to low before adding dairy. There should be only occasional small bubbles.
  6. 6.Add cheese and butter. Scatter in farmer's cheese, feta and butter. Let them warm for 1 minute before stirring gently.
  7. 7.Form the sauce. Nudge and fold the cheese into the liquid rather than whisking aggressively. Checkpoint: the liquid should become creamy and lightly thickened, with some soft cheese curds still visible.
  8. 8.Do not overcook. Once the cheese is hot and the sauce coats the chiles, remove from heat. Hard boiling can make the sauce grainy.
  9. 9.Taste before salting. Feta may provide all the salt needed. Adjust only after the cheeses are fully incorporated.
  10. 10.Serve immediately. Spoon ema datshi beside or over steamed rice while the sauce is loose and hot.
