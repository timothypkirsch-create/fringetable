Sudan / Northeast Africa

Sudanese Kisra

Bread / Staple · 14 hrs · 10 to 12 thin breads

Fermented sorghum batter becomes impossibly thin, flexible bread on a hot griddle—a Sudanese staple designed to meet stew.

Fringe Table adaptation

About this dish

Kisra is a Sudanese fermented sorghum flatbread cooked in very thin sheets on a hot griddle. The batter is fermented until mildly sour, diluted to a flowing consistency, then spread rapidly so the bread sets almost immediately. It is traditionally paired with mullah stews and functions as a staple rather than an occasional specialty.

Story & history

Why this dish matters

Sudan's embassy describes kisra as a major national staple made from durra, or sorghum. Archaeological and food-science research traces sorghum flatbread traditions deep into Sudanese history and documents the distinctive fermented batter and hot doka or saj cooking surface. Contemporary Sudanese cooks still describe the spreading technique as the difficult part: a good sheet is extremely thin, soft and tangy rather than pancake-thick.

Traditional kisra technique uses specialized griddles and spreading tools and takes practice. This nonstick-skillet adaptation preserves sorghum fermentation and one-sided thin cooking while acknowledging that the shaping technique is simplified.

Before you begin

Preparation notes

Ferment until tangy

The batter should smell pleasantly sour before cooking; an unfermented batter misses a defining part of kisra.

Thin after fermentation

Ferment a somewhat thicker mixture first, then dilute until it flows easily across the pan.

Cook only one side

Kisra is spread very thin and traditionally lifts once the upper surface dries; flipping is unnecessary when the batter is thin enough.

Cook it

Ingredients & detailed method

Ingredients

  • 2 cups sorghum flour
  • 2 cups lukewarm water for the initial batter, plus 1 to 2 cups more after fermentation
  • 2 tbsp plain yogurt or 1/4 cup active kisra starter, optional but helpful
  • 1/2 tsp salt
  • Neutral oil, for wiping the pan

Method

  1. 1.Whisk sorghum flour with 2 cups lukewarm water and yogurt or starter if using until smooth and thickly pourable.
  2. 2.Cover loosely and leave at room temperature 12–24 hours, depending on room temperature and starter strength.
  3. 3.Checkpoint: the batter should smell distinctly but pleasantly sour and show small bubbles or other signs of fermentation.
  4. 4.Whisk in salt and enough additional water to make a very thin batter, looser than American pancake batter.
  5. 5.Heat a large nonstick skillet or flat griddle over medium-high heat and wipe it with the thinnest film of oil.
  6. 6.Pour about 1/3 cup batter near one edge and immediately swirl the pan or use the back of a flat spreader to sweep it into the thinnest possible layer.
  7. 7.Cook without flipping until the top is dry and the sheet releases at the edges, roughly 30–90 seconds depending on thickness.
  8. 8.Lift onto a clean cloth and repeat, adjusting batter with water if the sheets are too thick. Stack only after steam has dissipated slightly.
