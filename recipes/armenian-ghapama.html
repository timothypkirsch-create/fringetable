Armenia / Caucasus

Armenian Ghapama

Festive Main / Sweet · 2 hrs 15 min · 6-8 servings

A whole pumpkin becomes both cooking vessel and centerpiece, holding honeyed rice, dried fruit and nuts until the flesh turns tender enough to slice open like petals.

Fringe Table adaptation

About this dish

Ghapama is an Armenian stuffed pumpkin strongly associated with festive tables. The Smithsonian Folklife Festival published a Goris regional version demonstrated by Armenian participants using rice, raisins, dried apricots, walnuts, pumpkin seeds, cornelian cherries, ghee, honey and cinnamon. Armenia's official tourism site likewise describes ghapama as a pumpkin filled with rice, dried fruit, honey and walnuts and highlights its holiday and harvest-season associations.

Story & history

Why this dish matters

Ghapama's visual drama is part of the dish: the baked pumpkin is carried whole to the table, then cut down its natural ribs so it opens around the fragrant rice filling. Smithsonian foodways documentation notes that the cost of nuts and dried fruit historically helped reserve the dish for feasts and festivals, while regional versions vary in fruit, added pumpkin and even meat. The Goris version presented at the Smithsonian emphasizes dried Armenian apricots and cornelian cherries; this home recipe keeps that fruit-and-nut profile while allowing a clearly labeled dried-cherry substitute.

Ghapama varies by Armenian region and family. This recipe follows the documented sweet Goris-style structure rather than claiming every ghapama is vegetarian or identical. Dried tart cherries can replace difficult-to-find cornelian cherries, but the substitution changes the fruit's specific tartness. Use a sturdy edible pumpkin sold for cooking, not a decorative pumpkin.

Before you begin

Preparation notes

Choose a stable edible pumpkin

A 4-5 lb sugar or pie pumpkin with firm walls and a flat-enough base is easier to fill, bake and carry safely.

Par-cook the rice

The rice should still have a firm center when drained because it finishes inside the pumpkin; fully cooked rice can become mushy.

Leave expansion room

Fill the pumpkin loosely and stop about 1 inch below the rim so the rice can finish cooking without compacting into a dense plug.

Cook it

Ingredients & detailed method

Ingredients

  • 1 edible sugar or pie pumpkin, about 4-5 lb
  • 1 cup long-grain white rice, rinsed
  • 1/2 cup golden or white raisins
  • 1/2 cup dried apricots, chopped
  • 1/3 cup dried cornelian cherries, or dried tart cherries as a clearly labeled substitute
  • 1/2 cup walnuts, toasted and roughly chopped
  • 1/4 cup hulled pumpkin seeds, toasted
  • 4 tbsp ghee or unsalted butter, melted, plus 1 tbsp for the pumpkin interior
  • 1/4 cup honey, plus 1 tbsp for the pumpkin interior
  • 1 tsp ground cinnamon
  • 1/2 tsp kosher salt
  • Hot water as needed for par-cooking the rice

Method

  1. 1.Heat the oven to 375°F. Wash and dry the pumpkin. Cut a lid around the stem, keeping the top intact, then scoop out seeds and fibrous strands without gouging deeply into the flesh.
  2. 2.Bring a saucepan of salted water to a boil. Add the rinsed rice and cook 10-12 minutes, then drain very well.
  3. 3.Rice checkpoint: the outside of each grain should be flexible but the center should still feel distinctly firm when bitten. If the rice is already fully tender, shorten the final bake checks because it can overcook inside the pumpkin.
  4. 4.Mix the warm drained rice with raisins, apricots, cornelian or tart cherries, walnuts, pumpkin seeds, 4 tablespoons melted ghee or butter, 1/4 cup honey, cinnamon and salt. The filling should smell buttery and fruity but remain loose, not syrupy.
  5. 5.Brush the pumpkin cavity with the remaining tablespoon of ghee or butter followed by the remaining tablespoon honey. Spoon in the rice mixture loosely, stopping about 1 inch below the rim, and replace the pumpkin lid.
  6. 6.Set the pumpkin in a sturdy baking dish and bake 60 minutes. Begin checking every 10-15 minutes after that; most 4-5 lb pumpkins need about 75-100 minutes total depending on wall thickness.
  7. 7.Doneness checkpoint: a thin knife or skewer should slide through the thickest pumpkin wall with very little resistance, the exterior should look deeper orange and lightly browned, and rice sampled from the center should be fully tender. If the pumpkin softens before the rice, cover loosely with foil and continue baking in short intervals.
  8. 8.Rest the ghapama at least 15 minutes before moving it. Transfer carefully to a platter, remove the lid and cut downward along the pumpkin's natural ribs so wedges fall outward around the rice filling. Serve warm, making sure each portion includes both pumpkin flesh and fruit-filled rice.
