Georgia / Caucasus

Georgian Khinkali

Dumplings / Main · 2 hrs 15 min · 24 dumplings, 4 to 6 servings

Pleated Georgian dumplings built to hold a spoonful of hot meat broth inside every pouch.

Fringe Table adaptation

About this dish

Khinkali are large Georgian dumplings whose sturdy pleated wrappers enclose seasoned meat and enough added water or broth to create a hot soup inside as they cook. Mountain styles and city styles differ in meat and herbs; this version follows the widespread beef-and-pork kalakuri direction documented by Georgia Travel.

Story & history

Why this dish matters

Georgia's national tourism authority identifies Pshavi and Tusheti as important homelands of khinkali and describes the dish's movement from mountain regions into Georgian cities, where mutton gave way in many versions to beef and pork and herbs became more common. Khinkali are eaten by hand: the top knot becomes a handle so the diner can bite the side and drink the broth before eating the rest of the dumpling.

Origin stories and symbolic claims around khinkali vary. This page focuses on the publicly documented culinary method and a common city-style meat filling rather than treating one legend or pleat count as universally authoritative.

Before you begin

Preparation notes

Keep the dough firm

The wrapper must survive boiling while holding hot broth, so it should be stronger than delicate pasta dough.

Make the filling loose

Water mixed into the meat is what turns into broth inside the dumpling.

Seal before chasing perfect pleats

A tight closure matters more than an impressive pleat count on your first batch.

Cook it

Ingredients & detailed method

Ingredients

  • 4 cups all-purpose or bread flour, plus more for dusting
  • 1 1/2 tsp kosher salt, divided
  • 1 1/3 cups cool water for the dough, approximately
  • 3/4 lb ground beef
  • 3/4 lb ground pork
  • 1 medium onion, minced very finely
  • 1/2 cup chopped cilantro
  • 1/4 cup chopped parsley
  • 1/2 tsp ground cumin
  • 1/2 tsp black pepper, plus more for serving
  • 1/4 tsp red chile flakes
  • 3/4 to 1 cup cold water or unsalted beef broth for the filling

Method

  1. 1.Mix flour with 1 teaspoon salt. Add cool water gradually and knead 8–10 minutes until smooth, firm and elastic. Cover and rest 30 minutes.
  2. 2.Mix beef, pork, onion, cilantro, parsley, cumin, pepper, chile and remaining salt. Gradually work in cold water or broth until the mixture is soft enough that a spoon sinks into it easily. Chill 20 minutes.
  3. 3.Roll the dough about 1/8 inch thick and cut 4- to 5-inch circles. Re-roll each circle slightly thinner at the outer edge while keeping some strength in the center.
  4. 4.Place about 1 1/2 tablespoons filling in the center. Lift the edge and pleat around the filling, pinching each fold into the next.
  5. 5.Gather the pleats tightly at the top and twist or pinch into a sealed knot. Checkpoint: there should be no gaps where broth can escape.
  6. 6.Bring a very large pot of salted water to a boil. Lower 6–8 khinkali in carefully and stir once very gently to prevent sticking.
  7. 7.When they float, reduce to a steady rather than violent boil and cook 8–10 minutes more. Checkpoint: wrappers should be cooked through and swollen with liquid, without splitting.
  8. 8.Lift out with a slotted spoon and drain briefly. Sprinkle generously with black pepper.
  9. 9.Serve immediately. Hold each dumpling by its top knot, bite a small opening in the side, sip the hot broth carefully, then eat the filled portion.
