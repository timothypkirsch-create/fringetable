Nigeria / West Africa

Nigerian Akara

Breakfast / Snack · 9 hrs · 18 fritters

Black-eyed peas become breakfast when they are peeled, ground, aerated and dropped into hot oil as crisp-edged akara.

Fringe Table adaptation

About this dish

Akara are West African bean fritters, especially familiar in Nigeria as breakfast or street food. Peeled black-eyed peas are ground with onion and chile, then the batter is whipped to trap air before frying. Good akara are crisp outside and remarkably light inside rather than dense bean paste.

Story & history

Why this dish matters

Nigerian food photography and culinary sources consistently identify akara as a staple breakfast and street snack. Food Network’s Nigerian Dinner episode features a recipe by Simbo Fowora built from soaked peeled black-eyed peas, onion and red pepper. Closely related bean fritters exist across West Africa under names including koose, and the tradition also traveled across the Atlantic, where it became part of Afro-Brazilian acarajé culture.

Akara seasoning and texture differ by household and region. This Nigerian version uses black-eyed peas, onion and fresh chile and does not attempt to merge Nigerian akara with Brazilian acarajé serving traditions.

Before you begin

Preparation notes

Peel the beans

Removing the skins makes the interior much lighter; rubbing soaked beans between your palms helps loosen them.

Keep batter thick

Too much blender water makes the fritters absorb oil and spread.

Whip before frying

Several minutes of beating incorporates air and is one of the keys to a fluffy center.

Cook it

Ingredients & detailed method

Ingredients

  • 2 cups dried black-eyed peas, soaked overnight
  • 1 small onion, roughly chopped
  • 1 red bell pepper or 2 red chiles, chopped
  • 1 Scotch bonnet or habanero, optional
  • 1 tsp kosher salt
  • 1/4 cup water, approximately, only as needed for blending
  • Neutral or palm oil, for frying

Method

  1. 1.Drain soaked peas and rub handfuls vigorously between your palms in water to loosen skins. Skim away floating skins and repeat until most are removed.
  2. 2.Blend peeled peas with onion and peppers, adding only enough water for the blades to move. The batter should be thick and spoonable.
  3. 3.Transfer to a bowl, add salt and beat vigorously with a whisk or wooden spoon for 3–5 minutes.
  4. 4.Checkpoint: batter should look slightly lighter and fluffier and a spoonful should hold a mound rather than run flat.
  5. 5.Heat 2 inches oil to 350–360°F. Drop rounded tablespoons of batter into the oil without crowding.
  6. 6.Fry 3–5 minutes, turning, until deep golden brown.
  7. 7.Checkpoint: fritters should feel crisp outside and split open to an airy, fully cooked bean interior with no wet paste.
  8. 8.Drain and serve hot with pap/ogi, bread or a fresh pepper sauce.
