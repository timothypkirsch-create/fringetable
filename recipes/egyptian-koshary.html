Egyptian Koshary (Rice, Lentils, Pasta and Chickpeas)

Layered Rice, Lentil and Pasta Bowl / Main · 1 hr 45 min · 6 servings

Koshary is abundance assembled from pantry staples: each grain and pulse keeps its texture, while tomato sauce, daqqa, chile and fried onions let every diner tune the same generous bowl.

Fringe Table adaptation

About this dish

Koshary is an Egyptian everyday dish made from inexpensive staples cooked separately and assembled in layers. UNESCO's description centers rice, pasta, black lentils and fried onions, with vinegar, garlic and hot sauce added according to preference; chickpeas and tomato sauce are also central to the widely recognized modern bowl. Good koshary is not a soft mixed casserole. The rice is fluffy, the lentils tender but intact, the pasta chewy, the chickpeas creamy, the tomato sauce concentrated, and the onions emphatically crisp. Daqqa, a sharp garlic-vinegar condiment, wakes up all of those starches at the table.

Story & history

Why this dish matters

UNESCO inscribed the daily-life dish and practices associated with koshary on the Representative List of the Intangible Cultural Heritage of Humanity in 2025, emphasizing its affordability, broad social reach, street and restaurant culture, and role in sharing and community. The Smithsonian's account cautions against a single neat origin story. A mid-19th-century written reference describes a rice-lentil-onion dish in Suez, while pasta, chickpeas and the modern sauces accumulated over time. Connections to Indian khichdi, Mediterranean migration and older Egyptian grain-and-legume eating are discussed, but the exact path remains debated. Today's layered bowl is unmistakably part of Egyptian daily life.

Spellings include koshary, koshari and kushari. Component ratios, pasta shapes, lentil varieties and sauce heat differ among vendors, regions and homes. This version uses brown lentils and medium-grain rice cooked together after the lentils receive a head start; separate cooking is equally valid and gives even more control. Canned chickpeas make the home workflow manageable. Fry onions cautiously in a stable neutral oil, keep children away from the hot pan, and let the onions darken only to deep golden because they continue crisping after removal.

Before you begin

Preparation notes

Slice onions evenly

A mandoline makes consistent 1/8-inch slices, but use its hand guard. Uneven onions burn before thicker pieces crisp.

Cook in coordinated stages

Start the onions and tomato sauce first, then the lentils and rice, and boil the pasta last. Koshary is best when all components are warm and distinct at assembly.

Keep the condiments separate

Serve tomato sauce, garlic-vinegar daqqa and chile sauce individually so diners can build the acidity and heat they prefer.

Cook it

Ingredients & detailed method

Ingredients

  • 3 large yellow onions, sliced into even 1/8-inch half-moons
  • 3/4 cup all-purpose flour
  • 1 tsp kosher salt, plus more to taste
  • 2 cups neutral oil, for frying
  • 1 cup brown or black lentils, rinsed and picked over
  • 1 cup medium-grain white rice, rinsed until the water is nearly clear
  • 1/2 cup broken vermicelli noodles
  • 1 tsp ground cumin, divided
  • 4 cups water, divided, plus more for pasta
  • 8 oz small elbow macaroni or ditalini
  • 1 can chickpeas, 15 oz, drained and rinsed
  • 5 garlic cloves, finely minced, divided
  • 2 tbsp tomato paste
  • 1 can crushed tomatoes, 28 oz
  • 1 tsp ground coriander
  • 1/2 tsp red chile flakes, plus more to taste
  • 1/2 cup white vinegar, divided
  • 2 tbsp fresh lemon juice
  • 1 small hot chile, minced, optional

Method

  1. 1.Toss the sliced onions with the flour and 1/2 teaspoon salt, separating every strand. Heat 1 inch of oil in a wide heavy skillet to 325°F. Shake excess flour from half the onions and fry 8 to 12 minutes, stirring often, until evenly deep golden. Transfer to a rack or paper towels, then repeat with the remaining onions.
  2. 2.Let the onions cool uncovered. Checkpoint: they may feel slightly flexible when first lifted but should become crisp within 5 minutes. If still leathery, return them to 325°F oil for 1 to 2 minutes; discard any bitter black pieces. Reserve 3 tablespoons onion oil for cooking.
  3. 3.Put the lentils and 2 cups water in a medium saucepan. Bring to a boil, reduce to a simmer and cook 12 minutes. The lentils should be partly tender but still firm at the center; drain only if more than 1/2 cup liquid remains.
  4. 4.Heat 1 tablespoon reserved onion oil in a separate heavy saucepan. Toast the vermicelli over medium heat for 2 to 3 minutes until nut-brown. Stir in the rice, lentils, 1/2 teaspoon cumin, 1/2 teaspoon salt and 2 cups water. Bring to a boil, cover, reduce to low and cook 15 minutes.
  5. 5.Remove the rice-lentil pot from the heat and rest, covered, 10 minutes. Fluff gently with a fork. The rice should be separate and the lentils tender but intact; if either is still firm, sprinkle in 2 tablespoons hot water, cover and steam over the lowest heat for 5 minutes.
  6. 6.Meanwhile, heat 2 tablespoons reserved onion oil in a saucepan over medium. Cook 3 minced garlic cloves for 30 seconds, then stir in the tomato paste, coriander, remaining 1/2 teaspoon cumin and chile flakes for 1 minute. Add the crushed tomatoes and 1/2 teaspoon salt.
  7. 7.Simmer the tomato sauce uncovered for 20 to 25 minutes, stirring occasionally, until brick red and thick enough that a spoon drawn across the pan leaves a path for a second. Stir in 2 tablespoons vinegar, taste and adjust salt or chile.
  8. 8.For the daqqa, crush the remaining 2 garlic cloves with a pinch of salt. Whisk with 1/4 cup vinegar, lemon juice and 1/4 cup water. For optional shatta, mix the minced hot chile with the remaining 2 tablespoons vinegar and 2 tablespoons tomato sauce; set both condiments aside.
  9. 9.Boil the macaroni in well-salted water until just al dente according to the package. Drain thoroughly. Warm the chickpeas in a small saucepan with 1/4 cup water and a pinch of salt for 5 minutes, then drain.
  10. 10.To assemble each bowl, spoon in rice and lentils, add a layer of macaroni and scatter over chickpeas. Ladle on tomato sauce and crown with a generous handful of crisp onions. Serve daqqa and shatta at the table rather than stirring them into the full batch.
  11. 11.Eat immediately while the onions are crisp and the components are warm. Store onions airtight at room temperature for 1 day and refrigerate all other components separately for up to 4 days; reheat the rice, lentils, pasta and sauce to steaming before assembling.
