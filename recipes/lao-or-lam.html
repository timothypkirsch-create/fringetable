Luang Prabang / Laos

Lao Or Lam

Main · 1 hr 45 min · Serves 6

A thick, deeply herbal Luang Prabang stew with meat, eggplant, mushrooms and sticky rice.

Fringe Table adaptation

About this dish

Or lam is strongly associated with Luang Prabang and is valued for a layered herbal character rather than one dominant spice. Traditional versions may include sakhaan, a peppery woody vine that is difficult to source outside Laos. This adaptation treats sakhaan as optional instead of pretending another spice is equivalent.

Sticky rice is used as a gentle thickener. The finished stew should feel substantial and cohesive, but it should still spoon rather than set like porridge.

Story & history

A stew closely tied to Luang Prabang

Or lam is repeatedly identified with Luang Prabang in Lao cooking, where the combination of local herbs, vegetables, fermented seasoning and sticky rice reflects the region's ingredients and eating habits. The dish is especially notable for ingredients that can be difficult to translate outside Laos, including sakhaan and local eggplants.

That sourcing challenge is why this page avoids aggressive substitution. When a defining ingredient is unavailable, it is more useful to explain what is missing than to rename an unrelated flavor as a replacement.

Family and restaurant versions differ in meat, herbs, vegetables and fermented seasoning. This is a home adaptation that preserves the technique and overall structure.

Before you begin

Preparation notes

Cook tough meat first

Beef chuck needs substantially more time than chicken thigh. Do not add delicate vegetables until the meat is nearly tender.

Thin the sticky-rice paste

Mash cooked sticky rice with hot broth before it goes into the pot so it disperses evenly instead of forming lumps.

Add fresh herbs off heat

Dill and basil should stay aromatic and green. Long boiling turns them muddy.

Cook it

Ingredients & detailed method

Ingredients

  • 1 1/2 lb beef chuck or boneless chicken thigh, cut into chunks
  • 5 cups water or unsalted stock
  • 2 stalks lemongrass, smashed and cut into large pieces
  • 1-inch piece galangal, sliced
  • 2 shallots, halved
  • 1–2 bird chiles, optional
  • 1/3 cup cooked sticky rice
  • 2 cups small eggplant pieces
  • 2 cups oyster or wood-ear mushrooms
  • 1 tbsp fish sauce or padaek, plus more to taste
  • 1/2 cup chopped dill
  • 1/2 cup Lao or Thai basil leaves
  • 2 scallions, sliced
  • Sakhaan, if authentically sourced, optional

Method

  1. 1.Start the aromatic broth. Combine meat, stock, lemongrass, galangal, shallots and chiles in a heavy pot and bring to a gentle simmer.
  2. 2.Cook according to the meat. Simmer beef 45–75 minutes or chicken about 25–35 minutes. Checkpoint: the meat should be nearly tender before vegetables go in.
  3. 3.Make the rice thickener. Mash cooked sticky rice with about 1 cup hot broth until it becomes a loose paste.
  4. 4.Thicken gradually. Stir the rice mixture back into the pot. Simmer 5 minutes, stirring occasionally.
  5. 5.Add vegetables. Stir in eggplant, mushrooms and sakhaan if using.
  6. 6.Cook until tender. Simmer 10–15 minutes. Checkpoint: eggplant should be soft through the center without completely dissolving; mushrooms should be tender.
  7. 7.Season. Add fish sauce or padaek a little at a time. The broth should taste savory and herbal, not simply salty.
  8. 8.Adjust consistency. Checkpoint: the stew should lightly coat a spoon. Add stock if too thick or simmer uncovered if too loose.
  9. 9.Remove coarse aromatics. Pull out large lemongrass and galangal pieces if desired.
  10. 10.Finish with herbs. Turn off the heat and fold in dill, basil and scallions. Serve immediately while their aroma is vivid.
