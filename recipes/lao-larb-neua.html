Laos / Southeast Asia

Lao Larb Neua

Main / Salad · 40 min · 4 servings

Warm minced beef tossed with lime, toasted rice, chile and herbs—the Lao dish where freshness and roasted aroma matter as much as heat.

Fringe Table adaptation

About this dish

Larb is one of Laos’s defining dishes: minced or finely chopped meat, fish or other protein seasoned with toasted rice powder, chile, savory fermented seasonings and fresh herbs. Larb neua uses beef. It is typically eaten with sticky rice and raw vegetables rather than treated as a Western-style lettuce salad.

Story & history

Why this dish matters

The Lao tourism authority describes larb as a hallmark of Lao cuisine and a lucky dish associated with festivals, weddings and special gatherings. Lao state cultural reporting likewise connects the dish’s name with ideas of luck and prosperity and describes its role in hospitality and communal celebrations. There are many regional forms, including cooked, raw, bitter and fish-based preparations; this version is a fully cooked beef larb intended for a home kitchen.

Lao larb varies widely, and some traditional versions include offal, bile, raw meat or padaek. This adaptation uses fully cooked beef and fish sauce for accessibility while retaining toasted rice powder, herbs, chile and lime as defining elements.

Before you begin

Preparation notes

Toast your own rice powder

Freshly toasted sticky or jasmine rice has a deeper nutty aroma than old packaged powder.

Cool the meat slightly before herbs

Very hot meat wilts mint and cilantro and can dull fresh lime juice.

Serve with sticky rice

Sticky rice is not just a side; it is the traditional eating partner that balances the concentrated seasoning.

Cook it

Ingredients & detailed method

Ingredients

  • 2 tbsp uncooked sticky rice or jasmine rice
  • 1 lb ground beef or finely hand-chopped beef
  • 2 tbsp water
  • 2 tbsp fish sauce, plus more to taste
  • 2 to 3 tbsp fresh lime juice
  • 1 to 2 tsp dried chile flakes or ground roasted chile
  • 2 small shallots, thinly sliced
  • 3 scallions, thinly sliced
  • 1/2 cup mint leaves, torn
  • 1/2 cup cilantro leaves and tender stems
  • 1/4 cup chopped culantro, optional
  • Warm Lao sticky rice, cabbage, cucumber and long beans, for serving

Method

  1. 1.Toast the uncooked rice in a dry skillet over medium heat for 7–10 minutes, stirring often, until evenly golden and nutty-smelling. Cool, then grind to a coarse powder.
  2. 2.Place beef and 2 tablespoons water in a skillet over medium-high heat. Cook, breaking the meat into small pieces, until just cooked through and no pink remains.
  3. 3.Remove from heat and let the beef cool for 3–4 minutes. Checkpoint: it should still be warm but no longer aggressively steaming.
  4. 4.Add fish sauce, lime juice, chile and 1 1/2 tablespoons toasted rice powder. Toss thoroughly.
  5. 5.Taste before adding herbs. The meat should be distinctly savory and sour with roasted-rice aroma; adjust fish sauce, lime or chile as needed.
  6. 6.Fold in shallots, scallions, mint, cilantro and culantro if using. Checkpoint: herbs should remain bright and leafy, not cooked into the meat.
  7. 7.Sprinkle with additional toasted rice powder and serve immediately with warm sticky rice and a platter of raw vegetables.
