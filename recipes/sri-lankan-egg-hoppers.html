Sri Lanka / South Asia

Sri Lankan Egg Hoppers (Bittara Appa)

Breakfast / Bread · 5 hrs · 8 hoppers

Crisp as lace around the rim and tender in the bowl, a hopper becomes breakfast when an egg steams right in its center.

Fringe Table adaptation

About this dish

Hoppers, or appa, are bowl-shaped Sri Lankan pancakes made from fermented rice batter and coconut milk. The rounded hopper pan creates an exceptionally thin crisp rim while a small pool of batter stays soft in the center. For bittara appa, an egg is cracked into that center and cooks under a lid without flipping.

Story & history

Why this dish matters

Sri Lanka Tourism lists hoppers among the island’s staple foods alongside string hoppers and pittu. They are eaten at breakfast and supper with sambols and curries, and egg hoppers are one of the most recognizable forms. Older methods can begin with soaked raw rice and a fermented starter; rice-flour-and-yeast batters are a common practical approach for modern kitchens.

Fermentation systems vary, including toddy, old batter and yeast. This home method uses yeast for predictability and should be understood as a practical adaptation rather than the only Sri Lankan hopper batter.

Before you begin

Preparation notes

Use a rounded hopper pan if possible

The curved sides produce the crisp thin rim and soft center that a flat skillet cannot fully duplicate.

Ferment until bubbly

A batter with no visible aeration produces dense, leathery hoppers.

Serve immediately

Steam from the egg begins softening the crisp rim as soon as the hopper leaves the pan.

Cook it

Ingredients & detailed method

Ingredients

  • 2 cups rice flour
  • 1 tsp instant yeast
  • 1 tsp sugar
  • 1 cup lukewarm water, plus more as needed
  • 1 cup coconut milk
  • 1/2 tsp kosher salt
  • 8 eggs
  • Neutral oil, for wiping the pan
  • Black pepper and salt, for the eggs
  • Pol sambol or lunu miris, for serving

Method

  1. 1.Whisk rice flour, yeast, sugar and 1 cup lukewarm water into a smooth thick batter. Cover and ferment in a warm place 3–4 hours until bubbly and lightly sour-smelling.
  2. 2.Whisk in coconut milk and salt. Add water gradually until the batter has the consistency of light cream and pours in a continuous stream.
  3. 3.Heat a hopper pan or small rounded wok over medium heat and wipe with a film of oil.
  4. 4.Pour in about 1/4 cup batter and immediately lift and swirl the pan so batter climbs the sides in a thin layer while a little remains pooled in the bottom.
  5. 5.Crack one egg into the center, season lightly, cover and cook 3–4 minutes.
  6. 6.Checkpoint: the edge should be deeply crisp and lacy, the center soft, the egg white opaque and the yolk cooked to your preference.
  7. 7.Run a thin spatula around the rim and lift the hopper out carefully. Repeat and serve each one immediately with sambol, dhal or curry.
