Cambodia / Southeast Asia

Cambodian Fish Amok

Main · 1 hr · 4 servings

Fish cooks gently inside a fragrant coconut custard whose flavor comes from fresh kroeung rather than heavy chile heat.

Fringe Table adaptation

About this dish

Fish amok is one of Cambodia's best-known dishes, combining fish, coconut milk, egg and kroeung, the aromatic paste built from lemongrass, galangal, turmeric, lime leaf and other aromatics. The finished texture should be softly set rather than soupy.

Story & history

Why this dish matters

Cambodian amok preparations vary by fish, leaf lining and richness. Banana-leaf cups are traditional and aromatic but a covered heatproof dish is a practical home alternative.

This recipe uses a home steamer and ramekins; banana-leaf cups add aroma but are optional.

Before you begin

Preparation notes

Use firm fish

Cod, snapper or catfish hold shape.

Blend kroeung finely

Fibrous lemongrass should become paste-like.

Steam gently

Hard boiling can curdle the custard.

Cook it

Ingredients & detailed method

Ingredients

  • 1 1/2 lb firm white fish
  • 1 can coconut milk
  • 1 egg
  • 2 tbsp fish sauce
  • 1 tsp palm or brown sugar
  • 2 lemongrass stalks, tender parts
  • 1 inch galangal or ginger
  • 1 inch turmeric
  • 3 garlic cloves
  • 2 makrut lime leaves
  • 1 shallot
  • 1 cup shredded cabbage or spinach

Method

  1. 1.Blend lemongrass, galangal, turmeric, garlic, lime leaf and shallot into a fine paste.
  2. 2.Whisk coconut milk, egg, fish sauce and sugar with the kroeung.
  3. 3.Line ramekins with cabbage or spinach and add fish pieces.
  4. 4.Pour coconut mixture over fish.
  5. 5.Steam covered 20–25 minutes.
  6. 6.Checkpoint: custard should be softly set and fish opaque and flaky.
  7. 7.Rest 5 minutes before serving with rice.
