Trinidad and Tobago / Caribbean

Trinidad Callaloo

Side / Stew · 50 min · 6 servings

Dasheen leaves, okra and pumpkin melt into coconut milk until a whole Scotch bonnet has perfumed one of Trinidad’s defining Sunday-table dishes.

Fringe Table adaptation

About this dish

Trinidadian callaloo is a thick leafy dish made from dasheen or taro leaves with okra, pumpkin, coconut milk and aromatics. Crab or salted meat may be included, while meatless versions are also common. It is distinct from Jamaican sautéed callaloo greens and from other Caribbean dishes that share the same name.

Story & history

Why this dish matters

Trinidad-raised food writer Brigid Washington describes callaloo as deeply rooted in Caribbean history and especially significant in Trinidad and Tobago. Foodie Nation’s Trinidadian method uses dasheen bush, okra, pumpkin, coconut milk, pimento peppers, herbs and a whole hot pepper, then partially purées the softened mixture. The goal is a thick, cohesive side dish rather than a thin soup.

Raw taro/dasheen leaves contain irritating calcium oxalate crystals and must be cooked thoroughly. If true dasheen leaves are unavailable, collards plus spinach provide a practical but noticeably different substitute.

Before you begin

Preparation notes

Cook dasheen completely

Do not taste raw or undercooked taro leaves; full cooking is essential.

Keep the Scotch bonnet intact

A whole unbroken chile adds aroma with more controlled heat.

Swizzle rather than liquefy

Traditional texture can retain some body instead of becoming a perfectly smooth purée.

Cook it

Ingredients & detailed method

Ingredients

  • 1 lb cleaned dasheen/taro leaves, chopped, or 1 lb mixed collards and spinach
  • 1 tbsp neutral oil
  • 1 medium onion, chopped
  • 3 garlic cloves, minced
  • 4 pimento peppers, chopped
  • 8 okra, sliced
  • 1 cup diced calabaza or butternut squash
  • 1 tsp thyme leaves
  • 2 tbsp chopped culantro or cilantro
  • 2 cups coconut milk
  • 1 cup water
  • 1 whole Scotch bonnet
  • 1 tbsp butter, optional
  • 1 tsp kosher salt, plus more to taste
  • Black pepper to taste

Method

  1. 1.Heat oil in a large pot. Add onion, garlic and pimento peppers and cook 2–3 minutes until fragrant.
  2. 2.Add okra and pumpkin and sauté 4 minutes.
  3. 3.Add chopped dasheen leaves, thyme, culantro, coconut milk, water and the whole Scotch bonnet.
  4. 4.Bring to a simmer, cover and cook 25–30 minutes until every leaf and stem is completely tender and the pumpkin crushes easily.
  5. 5.Remove the Scotch bonnet intact. Checkpoint: there should be no raw firmness in the taro leaves and the okra and pumpkin should have naturally thickened the liquid.
  6. 6.Use a traditional swizzle stick, immersion blender or potato masher to break the mixture into a thick, somewhat textured stew rather than a completely smooth soup.
  7. 7.Stir in butter if using, season with salt and pepper and serve with rice, macaroni pie or Sunday lunch dishes.
