Eritrea / Horn of Africa

Eritrean Alicha Birsen

Main / Side · 50 min · 6 servings

Lentils simmer with patiently softened onion, turmeric and aromatics into a mild golden stew for sharing with injera and more assertive dishes.

Fringe Table adaptation

About this dish

Alicha names a mild family of stews in Eritrean and Ethiopian cooking, distinguished from chile-red berbere preparations. Birsen refers to lentils. This home-kitchen adaptation uses red or yellow lentils, which soften into a creamy sauce around onion, garlic, ginger and turmeric.

The defining choice is restraint: turmeric supplies color and earthiness, but the pot should remain gentle enough to sit beside a hotter stew without competing with it.

Story & history

Why this dish matters

A shared injera platter is built from contrast—legumes, greens, vegetables and richer or hotter stews are eaten together rather than treated as isolated courses. A mild lentil preparation contributes body and protein while giving the diner a pause from berbere heat.

Lentil and vegetable dishes also have an important place during the many fasting periods observed in Eritrean and Ethiopian Orthodox traditions, when meals exclude meat and dairy.

Names, spellings, lentil varieties and seasoning differ across languages, regions and households. This recipe is a documented-style adaptation, not a claim that one version represents every Eritrean table.

Before you begin

Preparation notes

Rinse thoroughly

Wash until the water is much less cloudy so the stew turns creamy rather than gummy.

Build the onion base

Give the onion time to soften and sweeten before adding the lentils.

Control with hot water

Lentils thicken quickly; keep extra hot water nearby and adjust near the end.

Cook it

Ingredients & detailed method

Ingredients

  • 2 cups red or yellow lentils, picked over
  • 2 tablespoons neutral oil
  • 1 large yellow onion, very finely chopped
  • 3 garlic cloves, finely grated or minced
  • 1 tablespoon finely grated fresh ginger
  • 1 teaspoon ground turmeric
  • 5 cups water, plus more as needed
  • 1 teaspoon fine salt, plus more to taste
  • Injera, for serving

Method

  1. 1.Rinse the lentils in several changes of cool water, rubbing them gently between your fingers, until the draining water is only lightly cloudy. Drain well.
  2. 2.Warm the oil in a heavy saucepan over medium-low heat. Add the onion and a pinch of the salt; cook for 10–12 minutes, stirring often, until very soft and pale golden but not browned.
  3. 3.Add the garlic and ginger. Cook for 1 minute, stirring, until the raw aroma fades. Add the turmeric and stir for 20 seconds so it blooms in the oil without scorching.
  4. 4.Stir in the drained lentils and 4 cups of the water. Bring just to a boil, then immediately lower the heat to maintain a gentle simmer.
  5. 5.Simmer uncovered for 20 minutes, stirring across the bottom every few minutes. Skim only if a large amount of foam collects; ordinary surface bubbles are harmless.
  6. 6.Add the remaining 1 cup water in portions as the lentils swell. Continue simmering 10–15 minutes, stirring more frequently as the pot thickens.
  7. 7.Checkpoint: the individual lentils should collapse when pressed against the side of the pan. The stew should fall slowly from a spoon; if it sits in a stiff mound, stir in hot water 1/4 cup at a time.
  8. 8.Season with the remaining salt. Simmer 2 minutes, then turn off the heat and rest for 5 minutes; it will thicken further as it stands.
  9. 9.Taste once more for salt and loosen if necessary. Serve warm with injera as one component of a shared platter.
