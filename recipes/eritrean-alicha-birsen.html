Eritrea / Horn of Africa

Eritrean Alicha Birsen

Main / Side · 50 min · 6 servings

Lentils simmer with turmeric and aromatics into a mild, golden stew meant to share an injera platter with spicier dishes.

Fringe Table adaptation

About this dish

Alicha preparations in Eritrean and Ethiopian cooking are generally milder than berbere-driven wots, often using turmeric, onion, garlic and ginger. Birsen refers to lentils, making this a gentle lentil stew with a soft, spoonable consistency.

Story & history

Why this dish matters

Alicha-style stews show how Horn of Africa meals balance heat across the table rather than making every dish fiery. Lentil versions are particularly useful on fasting or meatless spreads.

Terminology and lentil choice vary across Eritrean households; this is a practical mild version.

Before you begin

Preparation notes

Rinse lentils

Excess starch can make the pot pasty.

Cook onions first

They build sweetness without chile.

Stop before gluey

The stew should be creamy but still spoonable.

Cook it

Ingredients & detailed method

Ingredients

  • 2 cups red or yellow lentils
  • 1 onion, chopped
  • 3 garlic cloves
  • 1 tbsp ginger
  • 2 tbsp oil
  • 1 tsp turmeric
  • 5 cups water
  • 1 tsp salt

Method

  1. 1.Cook onion in oil until soft.
  2. 2.Add garlic, ginger and turmeric for 1 minute.
  3. 3.Add rinsed lentils and water.
  4. 4.Simmer 25–35 minutes, stirring often.
  5. 5.Checkpoint: lentils should be fully tender and creamy but not stiff.
  6. 6.Add water if needed and season with salt.
  7. 7.Serve with injera and hotter stews.
