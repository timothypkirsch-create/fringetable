Lowcountry Hoppin' John

Rice / Main · 1 hr 15 min · 6 servings

Field peas and rice cook together with smoked pork in a Lowcountry dish inseparable from African American food history.

Fringe Table adaptation

About this dish

Hoppin' John is a Lowcountry rice-and-pea dish traditionally associated with field peas or cowpeas, rice and smoked pork. It is especially linked to New Year's tables, though the dish belongs to a much broader Lowcountry food tradition.

Story & history

Why this dish matters

Food historians connect Hoppin' John to West African rice and cowpea traditions preserved and transformed by enslaved Africans and their descendants in the Carolina Lowcountry. Modern versions using black-eyed peas are common, while Sea Island red peas more closely reflect older regional ingredients.

Sea Island red peas are ideal where available; black-eyed peas are a practical substitute.

Before you begin

Preparation notes

Use bean broth

It seasons the rice.

Do not over-stir rice

Keep grains distinct.

Choose smoked pork modestly

It should season, not dominate.

Cook it

Ingredients & detailed method

Ingredients

  • 1 1/2 cups cooked field peas or black-eyed peas
  • 1 cup long-grain rice
  • 4 oz smoked bacon or ham hock
  • 1 onion, chopped
  • 3 cups bean broth or stock
  • 1 tsp thyme
  • Black pepper
  • Salt to taste

Method

  1. 1.Render bacon or brown ham hock in a pot.
  2. 2.Add onion and cook until soft.
  3. 3.Stir in rice for 1 minute.
  4. 4.Add peas, broth and thyme.
  5. 5.Cover and simmer 18 minutes without stirring.
  6. 6.Checkpoint: rice should be tender and separate with liquid fully absorbed.
  7. 7.Rest covered 10 minutes, fluff and season.
