Jamaica / Caribbean

Jamaican Brown Stew Chicken

Main · 1 hr 45 min · 6 servings

Savory, aromatic chicken browned first for color and flavor, then gently simmered with thyme, scallion, peppers and tomato until the gravy turns glossy.

About this dish

Brown first, stew second

Brown stew chicken is a widely loved Jamaican home dish whose identity comes from the sequence as much as the ingredient list: well-seasoned chicken is browned until deeply colored, then simmered in a savory gravy with aromatics. Thyme, scallion, Scotch bonnet and sweet pepper are common anchors, while browning sauce, ketchup, carrot and tomato vary from cook to cook.

Story & history

A Jamaican Sunday-table favorite

Jamaican food writing often treats brown stew chicken as everyday comfort food and a familiar Sunday meal. The Jamaica Observer connects the dish to a longer Jamaican practice of adapting European fricassee-style stews through local ingredients, seasoning and browning techniques, while Jamaican recipe sources document the familiar pattern of marinating, browning and simmering.

There is no single household formula. Some cooks use commercial browning sauce, some caramelize sugar in the pan, and others rely mainly on hard searing for color. This Fringe Table version uses measured browning sauce for repeatability and keeps the Scotch bonnet whole for aroma with controlled heat.

Before you begin

Preparation notes

Use bone-in dark meat

Thighs and drumsticks stay juicy through browning and the later simmer. Breast meat can work, but add it later and stop around 165°F.

Reserve the vegetables

Scrape them off before searing. Wet onion and pepper stuck to the chicken prevent browning and can scorch before the meat colors.

Control the Scotch bonnet

Keep it whole for floral heat. A split pepper makes the gravy dramatically hotter; remove it before that happens.

Cook it

Ingredients & detailed method

Ingredients

  • 3 lb bone-in chicken thighs and drumsticks
  • 1 1/2 tsp kosher salt
  • 1 tsp ground allspice
  • 1/2 tsp black pepper
  • 1 tbsp Jamaican browning sauce
  • 2 tbsp soy sauce
  • 4 scallions, sliced
  • 1 medium onion, sliced
  • 1 green bell pepper, sliced
  • 1 ripe tomato, chopped
  • 4 garlic cloves, minced
  • 1 tbsp grated fresh ginger
  • 4 thyme sprigs
  • 1 Scotch bonnet pepper, left whole and pierced once
  • 2 tbsp neutral oil
  • 1 tbsp tomato ketchup
  • 1 1/2 cups low-sodium chicken stock or water
  • 1 medium carrot, sliced
  • 1 tsp brown sugar, optional to balance acidity

Method

  1. 1.Season and rest. Rub chicken with salt, allspice, pepper, browning sauce and soy sauce. Add scallion, onion, bell pepper, tomato, garlic, ginger and thyme. Refrigerate 30 minutes or up to overnight.
  2. 2.Prepare for browning. Scrape the vegetables into a bowl and reserve them. Pat the chicken surface mostly dry. Checkpoint: it should look tacky with seasoning, not wet enough to drip.
  3. 3.Brown the chicken. Heat oil in a wide heavy pan over medium-high. Brown in batches 3–5 minutes per side. Look for mahogany edges and rendered fat; the aroma should be roasted and savory, never acrid or burnt.
  4. 4.Soften the aromatics. Reduce heat to medium. Add the reserved vegetables and cook about 5 minutes, stirring, until onion is translucent and tomato starts collapsing into the pan juices.
  5. 5.Build the gravy. Stir in ketchup, stock and carrot, scraping the fond from the bottom. Add the whole Scotch bonnet. Bring only to a lively simmer, not a hard boil.
  6. 6.Stew gently. Return chicken and juices. Cover partially and simmer 30–40 minutes, turning once. Doneness checkpoint: dark meat should register at least 175°F in the thickest part and feel tender when pierced close to the bone.
  7. 7.Reduce to gloss. Uncover and simmer 8–12 minutes. The gravy is ready when it coats the back of a spoon and leaves a brief trail when you drag the spoon across the pan. Remove the Scotch bonnet before it splits.
  8. 8.Balance and serve. Taste for salt. Add the optional teaspoon of brown sugar only if the tomato reads sharply acidic. Serve hot with rice and peas, plain rice, steamed provisions or fried ripe plantain.
Substitutions

What can change—and what changes the dish

No commercial browning sauce: caramelize 2 teaspoons brown sugar in the cooking oil until dark amber before browning the chicken, then proceed carefully; the flavor will be slightly more caramel-forward.

No Scotch bonnet: use habanero for similar fruitiness and heat. A jalapeño gives much less floral aroma and changes the profile.

Boneless chicken: workable, but shorten the simmer and begin checking at 20 minutes so the meat does not dry out.
