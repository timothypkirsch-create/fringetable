Ghana / West Africa

Ghanaian Red Red

Main · 1 hr · 6 servings

Creamy black-eyed peas, red palm oil and tomato make a deeply colored Ghanaian stew built for sweet fried plantain.

Fringe Table adaptation

About this dish

Red red is a Ghanaian bean stew most often made with black-eyed peas or cowpeas, tomato, onion, chile and red palm oil. Its name is commonly linked to the reddish stew and the ripe fried plantain served beside it. The beans should be creamy but still recognizable, with oil visibly seasoning rather than flooding the sauce.

Story & history

Why this dish matters

Red red is everyday food in Ghana and a strong example of the importance of cowpeas and palm oil in West African cooking. Street-food and home versions vary in smoked fish, chile and bean texture. This plant-forward version keeps the defining bean-tomato-palm-oil structure and serves plantain alongside.

Some Ghanaian versions include smoked fish or additional seasonings. This meatless version preserves the core bean and palm-oil character.

Before you begin

Preparation notes

Use red palm oil

It contributes both flavor and the characteristic orange-red color.

Cook beans until creamy

Undercooked cowpeas make the stew feel chalky.

Plantains should be ripe

Yellow skins with dark spots give the sweet contrast expected beside the stew.

Cook it

Ingredients & detailed method

Ingredients

  • 2 cups cooked black-eyed peas
  • 1/3 cup red palm oil
  • 1 large onion, sliced
  • 3 tomatoes, chopped
  • 2 tbsp tomato paste
  • 1 Scotch bonnet, left whole or minced to taste
  • 1 tsp grated ginger
  • 1 tsp salt
  • 1/2 cup bean cooking liquid
  • 2 ripe plantains
  • Neutral oil for frying plantain

Method

  1. 1.Heat palm oil over medium and cook half the onion until softened and lightly browned.
  2. 2.Add ginger, tomato paste and chopped tomatoes; cook 10–12 minutes until the tomatoes collapse and oil begins to show around the edges.
  3. 3.Add chile, beans and bean liquid. Simmer 15–20 minutes.
  4. 4.Checkpoint: sauce should cling to beans and look glossy, not watery.
  5. 5.Taste for salt and chile, then keep warm.
  6. 6.Slice ripe plantains and fry in shallow neutral oil until caramelized brown on both sides.
  7. 7.Serve red red with fried plantain and remaining fresh onion if desired.
