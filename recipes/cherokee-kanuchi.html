Cherokee / Indigenous Americas

Cherokee Kanuchi

Soup / Traditional food · 2 hrs · 6 servings

Black walnuts become a creamy, deeply nutty soup through pounding and patient simmering rather than dairy or flour.

Fringe Table adaptation

About this dish

Kanuchi is a Cherokee black-walnut preparation traditionally made by pounding shelled nuts into a dense paste, historically with a large mortar and pestle, then simmering portions of the paste in water. Modern home cooks can reproduce the core method with a food processor while keeping the ingredient list restrained.

Story & history

Why this dish matters

Cherokee cultural and foodway sources describe kanuchi as an enduring winter food built around native black walnuts. The labor of shelling and pounding is part of the dish's history, and community recipes vary in sweetness, thickness and whether the nut solids are strained. This version follows the publicly documented home-kitchen approach without presenting one household formula as universal.

Black walnuts are strongly flavored and different from English walnuts. This is a home adaptation of a documented Cherokee preparation; community methods vary.

Before you begin

Preparation notes

Use black walnuts

English walnuts make a milder, different soup.

Grind to a paste

The nuts should clump and release oil, not remain coarse crumbs.

Simmer gently

Hard boiling can make the nut solids separate harshly.

Cook it

Ingredients & detailed method

Ingredients

  • 2 cups shelled black walnuts
  • 6 cups water, plus more as needed
  • 1/2 tsp kosher salt
  • 1 to 2 tbsp maple syrup or sugar, optional

Method

  1. 1.Pulse black walnuts in a food processor until very finely ground and beginning to form an oily paste.
  2. 2.Checkpoint: a squeezed spoonful should hold together rather than fall apart as dry crumbs.
  3. 3.Bring 6 cups water to a gentle simmer in a heavy saucepan and whisk in the walnut paste a little at a time.
  4. 4.Simmer 35–45 minutes over low heat, stirring often and scraping the bottom.
  5. 5.Checkpoint: the broth should turn opaque tan-brown, smell intensely nutty and lightly coat a spoon.
  6. 6.Season with salt and, only if desired, a little maple syrup or sugar.
  7. 7.Rest 10 minutes before serving so the natural walnut oils settle back into the soup.
