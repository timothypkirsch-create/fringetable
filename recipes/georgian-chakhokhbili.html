Georgia / Caucasus

Georgian Chakhokhbili

Main · 1 hr 20 min · 6 servings

Chicken cooked first in its own juices, then finished with tomato, onion, garlic and a generous handful of fresh herbs.

About this dish

Chicken, tomato and herbs without a heavy sauce

Chakhokhbili is a Georgian chicken stew whose character comes from concentrated poultry juices, ripe tomatoes, onions and fresh herbs rather than a large amount of added liquid. The sauce should cling to the meat and taste fresh, savory and herbal, not like a generic tomato braise.

Story & history

From pheasant to chicken

Georgia’s national tourism authority notes that the name is linked with khokhobi, pheasant, and that older versions were associated with pheasant while modern chakhokhbili is usually made with chicken. The same source emphasizes a defining technique: cook the bird in its own fat and juices, then build the onion-and-tomato mixture around it rather than diluting the pot with water.

Herb combinations vary by household and season. Cilantro is central in many modern versions; basil, parsley, savory, tarragon, adjika or wine may appear in others. This version keeps the core tomato-onion-garlic-herb structure and labels optional additions as such.

Before you begin

Preparation notes

Start in a cold pot

Skin-on chicken releases fat gradually as the pan heats, creating the cooking medium without immediately scorching the skin.

Use ripe tomatoes

Grated fresh tomatoes give enough moisture to braise the chicken. If tomatoes are pale and firm, high-quality canned whole tomatoes are the better substitute.

Add herbs late

Long cooking flattens cilantro and basil. Fold them in near the end so the finished stew smells green and fresh.

Cook it

Ingredients & detailed method

Ingredients

  • 3 to 3 1/2 lb bone-in skin-on chicken thighs and drumsticks
  • 2 tsp kosher salt, divided
  • 1/2 tsp black pepper
  • 3 medium onions, thinly sliced
  • 1 1/2 lb ripe tomatoes, grated or finely chopped
  • 5 garlic cloves, minced
  • 1 small green chile, finely chopped, optional
  • 1 cup chopped fresh cilantro
  • 1/2 cup chopped fresh basil
  • 1/4 cup chopped flat-leaf parsley
  • 1 tsp dried summer savory or 1 tbsp fresh savory, optional
  • 1/2 tsp ground coriander
  • 1 tbsp butter only if chicken renders very little fat

Method

  1. 1.Render slowly. Arrange chicken skin-side down in a cold heavy pot. Set over medium-low, cover and cook 12–15 minutes. Checkpoint: a shallow layer of clear chicken fat should collect while the meat releases its first juices.
  2. 2.Brown in its own fat. Uncover, raise heat to medium and turn the pieces as needed until the skin is golden. The pot should smell roasted and chicken-rich, not burnt.
  3. 3.Soften the onions. Add onions and half the salt. Cook 8–10 minutes, stirring. Use the optional butter only if the pan is unusually dry. The onions should become soft and translucent without taking on dark color.
  4. 4.Add tomato and aromatics. Stir in tomatoes, garlic, chile and ground coriander. Bring to a simmer. Visual checkpoint: tomato juices should rise around the chicken but not turn the dish into soup.
  5. 5.Simmer gently. Cover partially and cook 25–35 minutes. The thickest chicken pieces should register at least 175°F and feel tender when pierced near the bone.
  6. 6.Concentrate the sauce. Uncover and simmer 5–10 minutes if needed. The sauce is ready when it coats the chicken and a spoon dragged across the bottom leaves a trail for a second or two.
  7. 7.Finish with herbs. Fold in cilantro, basil, parsley and savory. Simmer only 2 minutes. Aroma checkpoint: the stew should suddenly smell bright, green and garlicky.
  8. 8.Rest and serve. Turn off heat, rest 5 minutes and adjust salt and pepper. Serve with bread, boiled potatoes or Georgian mchadi.
Substitutions

Practical swaps

Canned tomatoes: use one 28-ounce can of whole peeled tomatoes, crushed by hand. The flavor will be slightly more cooked but the sauce structure remains appropriate.

No summer savory: omit it rather than replacing it with a large amount of rosemary or oregano, which pushes the dish in a different direction.

Boneless chicken: possible, but the sauce loses some body and the cooking time shortens; begin checking thighs after 18–20 minutes.
