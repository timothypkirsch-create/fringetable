Jamaica / Caribbean

Jamaican Festival Fried Dumplings

Fried Bread / Side · 55 min · 10 festivals

Festival sits between bread and dumpling: lightly sweet cornmeal dough is rolled long, fried to a deep gold and served beside Jamaican fish, jerk or other savory foods.

Fringe Table adaptation

About this dish

Jamaican festival is a sweet-leaning fried dumpling made with wheat flour, cornmeal, sugar and baking powder. Jamaican chef Noel Cunningham publishes a version with brown sugar, butter, milk, water and vanilla, while other Jamaican recipe sources use a simpler flour-and-cornmeal dough. What distinguishes festival from the plainer Jamaican fried dumpling is the cornmeal and sweetness. This recipe keeps those features while using a moderate cornmeal ratio for a tender home-kitchen result.

Story & history

Why this dish matters

Festival is closely associated with Jamaican street food and fish meals, especially escovitch fish, although it also appears beside jerk and other savory dishes. Its elongated shape gives more crisp surface than a round dumpling while leaving enough interior to stay soft. Recipes vary noticeably in the flour-to-cornmeal ratio, sweetness, milk, butter, vanilla and nutmeg, so no single formula should be presented as universal. The important technical point is controlled frying: because the dough contains sugar, oil that is too hot can brown the outside before the center cooks through.

Festival and Jamaican fried dumpling or johnnycake are related but not identical. Festival normally includes cornmeal and more sweetness; omitting the cornmeal changes the bread enough that it should be called a different preparation.

Before you begin

Preparation notes

Use fine cornmeal

Coarse polenta-style cornmeal can remain gritty during the short fry. Fine yellow cornmeal hydrates more evenly in the dough.

Rest before shaping

A 20-minute rest lets the cornmeal absorb moisture so the dough rolls without cracking.

Keep the oil moderate

Aim for about 335°F. Sugar speeds browning, so hotter oil can leave a dark shell around a raw center.

Cook it

Ingredients & detailed method

Ingredients

  • 1 1/2 cups all-purpose flour
  • 1/2 cup fine yellow cornmeal
  • 3 tbsp packed brown sugar
  • 2 tsp baking powder
  • 1/2 tsp kosher salt
  • 2 tbsp unsalted butter, softened
  • 1/2 tsp vanilla extract
  • 1/2 cup whole milk
  • 1/4 to 1/3 cup cool water, as needed
  • Neutral frying oil, enough for about 2 inches depth

Method

  1. 1.Whisk flour, cornmeal, brown sugar, baking powder and salt in a bowl. Rub in the softened butter with your fingertips until no large butter pieces remain.
  2. 2.Stir vanilla into the milk. Add the milk to the dry ingredients, then add cool water 1 tablespoon at a time until the mixture forms a soft but shapeable dough.
  3. 3.Knead gently in the bowl for about 2 minutes, just until the dough looks cohesive. Cover and rest 20 minutes.
  4. 4.Checkpoint: after resting, the dough should feel supple and slightly tacky but should not smear on your hands. Pinch a piece and roll it; the surface should stay mostly smooth rather than splitting into dry cracks.
  5. 5.Divide into 10 equal pieces. Roll each between your palms and the counter into a 5- to 6-inch log with slightly tapered ends and a gently flattened profile.
  6. 6.Heat 2 inches of neutral oil in a heavy pot to 330 to 340°F. Fry 3 or 4 festivals at a time for 5 to 7 minutes, turning several times so the color develops evenly. Adjust the burner to keep the oil in range after each batch is added.
  7. 7.Checkpoint: the exterior should be an even deep golden brown, not mahogany, and the festivals should feel noticeably lighter when lifted. Split one from the first batch: the center should be fluffy and bread-like with no wet dough line or raw cornmeal taste.
  8. 8.Drain on a rack or paper towels for 2 to 3 minutes. Serve hot with Jamaican escovitch fish, jerk dishes or another savory main; the light sweetness is meant to contrast with chile, vinegar and smoke.
