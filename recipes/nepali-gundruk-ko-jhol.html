Nepali Gundruk ko Jhol

Soup / Side · 40 min · 4 servings

Fermented greens turn broth tangy and earthy, with tomato and timur adding brightness rather than masking the fermentation.

Fringe Table adaptation

About this dish

Gundruk is a Nepali fermented leafy-green product made from mustard, radish or cauliflower leaves. Gundruk ko jhol turns those greens into a light, tangy soup or curry served with rice and other dishes.

Story & history

Why this dish matters

Fermentation preserves seasonal greens for later use and gives gundruk its distinctive sour aroma. It is an important example of Himalayan preservation knowledge and is widely associated with Nepali home cooking.

Commercial gundruk varies in salt and sourness. Taste before adding extra acid or salt.

Before you begin

Preparation notes

Rinse only if very salty

Over-rinsing removes flavor.

Soak briefly

It softens tough dried greens.

Do not over-spice

Fermentation should remain central.

Cook it

Ingredients & detailed method

Ingredients

  • 2 cups dried gundruk
  • 4 cups water
  • 2 tomatoes, chopped
  • 3 garlic cloves
  • 1 tbsp grated ginger
  • 1/2 tsp turmeric
  • 1/2 tsp timur pepper, crushed
  • 1 tbsp mustard oil
  • Salt to taste

Method

  1. 1.Soak gundruk in warm water 10 minutes and drain.
  2. 2.Heat mustard oil and cook garlic, ginger and turmeric briefly.
  3. 3.Add tomatoes and cook until soft.
  4. 4.Add gundruk and water and simmer 20 minutes.
  5. 5.Crush timur and stir in near the end.
  6. 6.Checkpoint: greens should be tender and broth distinctly tangy but not harsh.
  7. 7.Adjust salt and serve with rice or dal bhat.
