Assam / Northeast India

Assamese Aloo Pitika

Side · 30 min · 4 servings

Warm potatoes are roughly mashed with raw mustard oil, onion and green chile in an Assamese side whose simplicity makes ingredient balance essential.

Fringe Table adaptation

About this dish

Aloo pitika is an Assamese mashed-potato preparation served with rice and dal as part of an everyday meal. Unlike butter-rich mashed potatoes, it relies on the pungency of raw mustard oil, fresh onion and green chile.

The potatoes should remain slightly uneven rather than whipped. Mustard oil is the defining aroma, so use a culinary product labeled for food use and add it gradually according to its strength.

Story & history

Why this dish matters

Pitika refers to mashed or crushed preparations in Assamese cooking, and potato is only one possibility. Fish, eggplant and other vegetables may be treated in related ways, often with mustard oil, onion, chile and herbs.

Aloo pitika's short ingredient list is not evidence that the dish lacks technique. Potatoes must be dry enough to take up oil, while raw onion and chile must be cut finely enough to season each bite without overwhelming it.

Some households roast rather than boil the potatoes or add roasted garlic, egg, fish or lime. This vegetarian boiled-potato version follows a common everyday form.

Before you begin

Preparation notes

Dry the potatoes

Drain thoroughly and return them to the warm pot so excess water evaporates.

Use culinary mustard oil

Check the label and begin with less if the oil is especially pungent.

Mash, do not whip

Uneven pieces give pitika its intended rustic texture.

Cook it

Ingredients & detailed method

Ingredients

  • 1 1/2 pounds Yukon Gold or other waxy potatoes
  • 1/3 cup red onion, very finely chopped
  • 1–2 green chiles, very finely chopped
  • 2 tablespoons chopped cilantro
  • 1 1/2 tablespoons culinary mustard oil, plus more to taste
  • 3/4 teaspoon fine salt, plus more to taste
  • 1 teaspoon fresh lime juice, optional

Method

  1. 1.Scrub the potatoes and place them in a saucepan. Cover with cool water by 1 inch and salt the water lightly.
  2. 2.Bring to a boil, lower to a steady simmer and cook 20–30 minutes, until a knife passes through the center without resistance.
  3. 3.Drain completely. When cool enough to handle but still warm, peel if desired and return the potatoes to the warm dry pan for 2 minutes.
  4. 4.Break the potatoes into rough pieces with a masher or clean hands. Do not beat or process them.
  5. 5.Add onion, green chile, cilantro, salt and 1 tablespoon mustard oil. Fold and press until combined while preserving small potato pieces.
  6. 6.Checkpoint: the mixture should hold together without becoming gluey and should smell distinctly mustardy. If the potatoes seem wet, let them stand uncovered before adding more oil.
  7. 7.Taste and add the remaining mustard oil gradually. Its strength varies, and it should perfume the dish without making every bite harsh.
  8. 8.Add optional lime only if the mixture needs brightness; it is a household variation rather than a required ingredient.
  9. 9.Rest 5 minutes so the onion and chile season the warm potato. Serve warm or at room temperature with rice and dal.
